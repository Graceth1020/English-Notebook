# 12-Week Curriculum

Five teaching lessons plus one review lesson per week; 72 sessions total.
Each lesson has one small goal. Speaking and workplace output lead; listening
rides on the learner's own video material; reading and writing are trained
through workplace artifacts.

Track actual progress in `coach/plan.md` - this file is the arc, not the record.

## Phase 1 (Weeks 1-4): Fluency and Core Speech Acts

Get sentences out fast and make them sound like speech, not translated text.

- Week 1: reacting and buying time; describing your own work clearly;
  disagreeing politely; asking for clarification; hedging a guess.
- Week 2: narrating past work (what you built, what broke); cause and effect;
  quantifying and comparing; explaining a decision; admitting uncertainty.
- Week 3: opinion with reasons; agreeing partially; pushing back on scope;
  interrupting politely; checking understanding.
- Week 4: giving and receiving feedback; small talk that isn't awkward;
  self-introduction that lands; talking about a bug; talking about a tradeoff.

## Phase 2 (Weeks 5-8): Workplace Fluency

Move from correct sentences to effective communication in real formats.

- Week 5: daily standup (blockers, progress, plans); async status updates.
- Week 6: code review English - suggesting, questioning, disagreeing, praising;
  writing PR descriptions.
- Week 7: technical design discussion - proposing, comparing options, defending
  a choice, conceding a point; estimates and pushback on deadlines.
- Week 8: incidents and escalation - reporting impact, stating the ask,
  postmortem language; meetings you don't control (talking over noise,
  reclaiming the floor).

## Phase 3 (Weeks 9-12): Range, Pressure, and Polish

Handle unscripted pressure and register shifts.

- Week 9: 1:1s and career conversations - asking for scope, raise, feedback.
- Week 10: interviews - behavioral answers with structure; explaining projects
  end to end; system design narration out loud.
- Week 11: cross-cultural nuance - indirectness, humor, disagreement across
  cultures; working with other non-native speakers.
- Week 12: consolidation under pressure - long-turn speaking, spontaneous
  opinion, mixed-format simulation, and a final self-assessment against Week 1.

## Workplace Scenario Bank

Draw output tasks from these. Rotate; do not exhaust one area.

**Daily engineering:** standup updates; sprint planning; grooming a ticket;
pairing out loud; asking for help without seeming lost; handing off work;
reporting a blocker; time-zone coordination.

**Code and design:** review comments (suggest / question / object / approve);
PR descriptions and commit messages; design docs; proposing a refactor;
defending a library choice; discussing tech debt; explaining a tradeoff to a
non-engineer.

**Pressure moments:** disagreeing with a senior engineer; saying a deadline is
unrealistic; declining scope creep; admitting you broke production; reporting
an incident; correcting a manager's wrong assumption.

**Career and social:** 1:1 with a manager; performance self-review; asking for
promotion or raise; behavioral and technical interviews; networking; team
lunch small talk; onboarding a new teammate.

**Everyday (secondary line, weave in):** appointments and phone calls;
renting and moving; travel problems; doctor visits; customer service
complaints; making plans and cancelling politely.

## Progression Rules

- Advance when the learner produces the target pattern correctly without a
  model in two consecutive lessons.
- Repeat or re-angle a goal when major fixes exceed roughly half the answers.
- Any pattern unresolved for two weeks becomes a warm-up item until it clears.
- Raise difficulty by lengthening turns and removing scaffolding, not by using
  rarer vocabulary.
