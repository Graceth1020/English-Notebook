## 2026-11-04 10:30
- **Input**: 表达我非常享受某件事
- **Expressions**: I really love it / I'm really into it / I'm loving it / It's a blast / I get such a kick out of it / I'm a huge fan of it
- **Register**: neutral to very casual; all six work in everyday US conversation, from casual chats to normal work small talk
- **Examples**:
  - I love this coffee shop, I come here every morning.
  - I've been really into pottery lately, it's so relaxing.
  - I'm loving this new show, I binged half of it last night.
  - The hike was a blast, we should do it again next weekend.
  - I get such a kick out of watching my dog try to catch his tail.
  - I'm a huge fan of their new album, it's on repeat.
- **Avoid**: 「I derive great pleasure from it」 and 「I take great delight in it」 — both sound literary and stiff in everyday speech; 「I am thoroughly enjoying it」 sounds like a textbook sentence, not how Americans actually talk.
- **Notes**: 「I'm loving it」 uses present continuous for current enjoyment, which is extremely common in American speech (the McDonald's jingle didn't invent this pattern). 「It's a blast」 is very casual — avoid in formal settings or with people you don't know well. 「Get a kick out of」 often reduces to 「get such a kick outta」 in fast speech. 「I'm into it」 means hooked/enthusiastic, not literal location. BrE often says 「I really enjoy it」 as the default; AmE favors 「I love it」 more.
