## 2026-08-28 14:59
- **Input**: 我认为
- **Expressions**: I think / I feel like / I'd say / My take is / If you ask me / The way I see it
- **Register**: I think (neutral, default) - I feel like (casual, softest) - I'd say (neutral, gentle recommendation) - My take is (slightly formal, work) - If you ask me (casual, mildly opinionated) - The way I see it (neutral, framing a longer argument)
- **Examples**:
  - I think we should just ship it and see what happens.
  - I feel like we're overcomplicating this.
  - I'd say give it another week.
  - My take is that the deadline is too tight.
  - If you ask me, we should have caught this in review.
  - The way I see it, we've got two options.
- **Avoid**: "I am of the opinion that" (stiff, legal/written only); "In my humble opinion" / IMHO (reads sarcastic in speech); "According to me" (not English - Chinese speakers over-produce this); "I consider that" (unnatural as an opinion frame)
- **Notes**: Drop "that" after "I think" - "I think we should", not "I think that we should". "I feel like" is the dominant hedge among under-40 US speakers and works even for facts, not just feelings. "I'd say" = "I would say", reduced to /aid sei/. "I think" can also move to the end as a tag: "That's the wrong call, I think." For disagreeing, native speakers front a softener: "I don't know, I feel like..."

## 2026-08-28 15:16
- **Input**: 对于......来说不合适（例：这个设计方案对于这个场景不合适）
- **Expressions**: isn't a good fit for / doesn't work for / isn't right for / doesn't make sense for / is overkill for / is the wrong tool for / doesn't really suit
- **Register**: isn't a good fit for (neutral, the default in US tech/work speech) - doesn't work for (casual, plainest and most frequent) - isn't right for (neutral, gentle) - doesn't make sense for (neutral, implies a reasoning mismatch) - is overkill for (casual, specifically too heavy/complex) - is the wrong tool for (casual, idiomatic "wrong tool for the job") - doesn't really suit (slightly formal, more common in BrE)
- **Examples**:
  - This design isn't a good fit for this use case.
  - Honestly, that approach just doesn't work for what we're building.
  - A relational DB isn't right for this kind of workload.
  - I don't think microservices make sense for a team this size.
  - Kafka is overkill for three events a day.
  - Yeah, that's the wrong tool for this job.
  - This pattern doesn't really suit our constraints.
- **Avoid**: "is not suitable for" - grammatically fine but textbook-stiff in speech; Chinese speakers massively over-produce it. Keep it for written specs. | "is not fit for" - wrong unless you use the fixed idiom "not fit for purpose" (BrE-leaning). | "is not appropriate for" - sounds like a policy or HR judgment, not a technical fit. | "is unsuitable for" - written register only. | "doesn't fit for this scenario" - no "for" after the verb "fit": say "doesn't fit this scenario" or "isn't a good fit for this scenario".
- **Notes**: "fit" as a NOUN needs an article: "isn't A good fit for", never "isn't good fit for". Softeners: "isn't a great fit", "isn't the best fit" - both are polite ways to say no in a review. "scenario" is rarer than the Chinese 场景 1:1 suggests; Americans say "use case", "situation", "setup", or just "here" ("that doesn't work here"). Fronting a hedge is standard when disagreeing: "I don't know, I feel like this isn't a great fit for..." Reductions: "doesn't" -> /dʌzənt/, often "duzn't"; "isn't a" runs together as "iz-nə". To sound decisive instead of polite, use "is the wrong X for" or "is overkill for".

## 2026-08-28 17:17
- **Input**: 特别是......（类似 especially 的表达）
- **Expressions**: especially / especially when + clause / particularly / ... in particular / more so if / above all / not to mention / least of all
- **Register**: especially (neutral, by far the most frequent in US speech) - especially when/if + clause (neutral, the highest-value pattern to drill) - particularly (slightly formal, written or careful speech) - X in particular (neutral, post-modifier, adds emphasis) - more so if/when (neutral, degree comparison) - above all (slightly formal, ranks one item top) - not to mention (casual, adds a further point) - least of all (casual, negative contexts only)
- **Examples**:
  - The build is slow, especially on Windows.
  - Especially when the cache is cold, it can take five minutes.
  - Reviews take forever, particularly for big PRs.
  - Everyone was tired, above all the on-call folks.
  - It's risky, more so if we ship on a Friday.
  - Nobody read the doc, least of all me.
  - Deploys are painful, and Fridays in particular.
  - We need better tests, and I mean especially around auth.
- **Avoid**: "specially" - a different word (= for a special purpose: "specially made"); using it for "especially" is a very common non-native slip. | "specially when" - wrong; "especially when". | "Especially, we need to fix auth." - do not use it as a standalone sentence-opening adverb with a comma; "especially" must attach to a specific element. | "more especially" - dated and stiff. | "in especial" - archaic, do not use. | "expecially" / "exspecially" - misspelling and mispronunciation; there is no /k/ + /s/ cluster.
- **Notes**: Position: "especially" goes IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the thing it emphasises - "especially on Windows", not "on Windows especially" in most cases (though the post-position exists for extra weight). Sentence-initial "Especially when..." IS fine because it attaches to the following clause. | "especially when/if + clause" is the single most useful pattern here: it lets you tack a condition onto a complaint or claim without a new sentence. | "in particular" is a POST-modifier: "Fridays in particular", never "in particular Fridays". | Spoken emphasis often adds "and I mean": "and I mean especially around auth". | Reductions: "especially" is said /əˈspeʃli/, four syllables collapsing to roughly "uh-SPESH-lee"; the second "a" disappears. | "not to mention" takes a noun phrase, not a clause: "not to mention the cost" ✓ / "not to mention it costs a lot" is common in speech but looser. | "least of all" only works after a negative: "Nobody read it, least of all me."
