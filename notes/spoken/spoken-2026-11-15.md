## 2026-11-15 09:59
- **Input**: 而且、还有（除了 on top of that 以外的说法）
- **Expressions**: Plus / Besides / Not to mention / And then there's XX / And get this / To top it all off
- **Register**: neutral to very casual; all six are sentence-initial add-ons in everyday American speech
- **Examples**:
  - Plus, it's way cheaper than the alternative.
  - Besides, we don't even have the budget for it.
  - Not to mention the traffic - it'll take two hours.
  - And then there's the timeline, which is already tight.
  - And get this, they want it done by Friday.
  - To top it all off, the client changed the requirements again.
- **Avoid**: 「Moreover」「Furthermore」「In addition」「Additionally」 - all four are written register; said out loud they sound like someone reading a report. 「And also plus」 - stacking two add-ons, redundant and non-native.
- **Notes**: 「Plus」 is the highest-frequency add-on in American casual speech and works as a one-word opener: 「Plus, ...」. Do not pair it with 「and also」 - pick one. 「Besides」 carries an anyway-this-settles-it tone; it usually introduces the reason that closes the argument, so it is stronger than 「plus」. 「Not to mention」 is a fragment and takes a noun phrase: 「not to mention the cost」 is right, 「not to mention it costs a lot」 sounds off. 「And get this」 is a discourse marker announcing surprising info, very casual, best in storytelling. 「To top it all off」 caps a list and skews toward a final annoyance or a final impressive item. Sentence-initial 「Also」 reads slightly stiff in AmE speech - mid-sentence 「also」 or trailing 「too」 is the safer spoken default. BrE leans more on 「what's more」 and 「on top of that」; AmE casual leans on 「plus」 and 「and then there's」.
