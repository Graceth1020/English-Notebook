- Input: 特别是XX部分，...（例：Especially the transfer part, we need one day to sort out the complex logic and a week to develop.）
- Expressions: The X part especially - ... / As for the X part, ... / The X part alone - ... / X specifically (mid-sentence) / Especially on the X side, ... / And that is before you get to the X part
- Register: The X part especially - ... (neutral, most common in speech; the dash gives the natural spoken pause and keeps it glued to the main clause) - As for the X part, ... (neutral, slightly more organised; good when you are running down a list of items) - The X part alone - ... (neutral, adds the implication "and that is only one piece of it"; strong for pushing back on an estimate) - X specifically (neutral, drops into the middle of a clause instead of fronting it) - Especially on the X side, ... (casual to neutral; "on the X side" is the idiomatic way to name a workstream) - And that is before you get to the X part (casual, sets up a warning that the number is going up)
- Examples:
  - The transfer part especially - we need a day just to sort out the logic, then about a week to build it.
  - As for the transfer part, one day to sort out the logic and a week to build it.
  - The transfer part alone is a week. That is before the testing.
  - We need a day to sort out the transfer logic specifically, and then a week to develop it.
  - Especially on the transfer side, the logic is not obvious yet.
  - Two weeks for the main flow. And that is before you get to the transfer part.
- Avoid: Especially the transfer part, we need one day... - grammatical words but a dangling modifier: fronted "Especially + noun" has nothing in the main clause to attach to, so it lands like a sentence that was abandoned halfway. Move "especially" after the noun, or switch to "As for". | Specially the transfer part - "specially" means "for a special purpose" (specially made for you); the intensifier is "especially". | Particularly the transfer part, we need... - same dangling problem as "Especially"; "particularly" is also a register step up from normal speech. | Especially, the transfer part needs one day - the comma after "Especially" turns it into a sentence adverb, which is not what it does; "especially" needs something to point at. | In particular of the transfer part - not English; the phrase is "in particular" (no "of") and it goes after the noun: "the transfer part in particular". | Above all the transfer part - "above all" ranks importance overall, not "this item more than the others"; it also sounds written.
- Notes: Rule of thumb: put "especially" AFTER the thing it points at when that thing is the subject of your comment - "the transfer part especially". Fronting it only works when a full clause follows: "Especially if the transfer logic changes, we will need more time." | The dash is doing real work here. In speech there is a pause after the fronted noun, and the dash is how that pause is written. A comma reads as a list separator and makes the dangling problem worse. | Naming a workstream: "on the X side" (on the transfer side, on the business side, on the frontend side) is the standard collocation - see also chunk "the business side". | "X alone" is the estimate-defence move: it concedes one item while implying the rest is still uncounted. Very useful in a scoping conversation. | Also fix in the source sentence: "a week to develop" needs an object - "a week to develop it" / "a week to build it" (see error chat:E055, dropped object after a transitive verb).
