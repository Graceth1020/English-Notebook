- **Input**: 它尝起来特别美味
- **Expressions**: This is so good / This is really good / That's delicious / This tastes amazing / These are incredible / This is bomb / That hit the spot
- **Register**: This is so good (casual, BY FAR the most common - what Americans actually say at the table) - This is really good (neutral, safe anywhere) - That's delicious (neutral to slightly formal, common when complimenting a host) - This tastes amazing (casual, enthusiastic) - These are incredible (casual, strong praise) - This is bomb / fire / slaps (very casual, younger speakers, slang) - That hit the spot (casual, means it satisfied a craving, not that it tasted refined)
- **Examples**:
  - Oh my god, this is so good.
  - This is really good, you have to try it.
  - Wow, that's delicious.
  - This tastes amazing.
  - Mmm, that's so good.
  - These are incredible - where did you get them?
  - Dude, this is bomb.
  - That hit the spot.
- **Avoid**: "It tastes especially delicious" - textbook translation of 特别美味; no American says this. "especially" needs a comparison point, it is not a general intensifier. | "It is very delicious" - "delicious" is already an extreme adjective, so it does not take "very". Use "absolutely delicious" or just "delicious". | "It tastes very good" - grammatical but flat and non-native-sounding; "It's really good" is the natural version. | "It tastes deliciously" - wrong; "taste" is a linking verb and takes an adjective. | "How delicious it is!" - literary exclamation, sounds like a 1950s textbook. | "Yummy" - fine from a child or in a cute/ironic tone; adults saying it straight can sound childish.
- **Notes**: KEY POINT: Americans usually drop "tastes" entirely and just say "This is so good." The verb is understood from context. Translating 尝起来 word-for-word as "tastes" is the main tell. | "so" is the default spoken intensifier for food, not "very": "so good" >> "very good". | Non-gradable adjectives (delicious, amazing, incredible, perfect) take "absolutely" or "really", never "very": "absolutely delicious" ✓ / "very delicious" ✗. | Reaction-first is the native rhythm: "Oh my god / Wow / Mmm" comes BEFORE the sentence. | The follow-up move matters as much as the compliment: "you have to try it", "what's in this?", "where did you get this?" | Slang tier (bomb, fire, slaps, so good it's stupid) is Gen Z / casual friends only - avoid at a work dinner or with someone's parents. | "That hit the spot" = it satisfied a need (you were hungry), which is different from praising the flavor.
