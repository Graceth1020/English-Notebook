## 2026-11-05 14:59
- **Input**: 约着出去做某事
- **Expressions**: Wanna grab [coffee/dinner]? / Are you doing anything [day]? / Let's hang out sometime / We should go [do activity] sometime / Hit me up when you're free / Let's catch up over drinks
- **Register**: casual; these are everyday friend/peer suggestions, not formal invitations
- **Examples**:
  - Hey, wanna grab coffee after work?
  - Are you doing anything Saturday? I was thinking we could check out that new exhibit.
  - We should hang out sometime — it's been way too long.
  - We should go see that new movie this weekend.
  - Hit me up when you're free, we'll grab dinner.
  - Let's catch up over drinks next week, my treat.
- **Avoid**: 「Would you like to go out with me?」 — sounds like a formal romantic date proposal, not how Americans casually suggest plans. 「I would like to invite you to...」 — extremely stiff, like a formal letter. 「Shall we go out?」 — BrE and dated; AmE almost never uses 「shall」 for casual suggestions.
- **Notes**: 「Wanna」 = 「want to」 reduction; 「grab」 is the key verb in AmE for getting food/drink casually (grab coffee, grab lunch, grab a beer). 「Hit me up」 is texting slang — very casual, do not use with colleagues you barely know. 「Hang out」 is the default verb for spending time together platonically. BrE uses 「shall we」 more often for suggestions; AmE favors 「wanna」 or 「let us」 / 「let's」. Adding 「sometime」 softens a suggestion — it can mean 「let us do this one day」 without committing to a specific date, which sometimes frustrates people waiting for concrete plans.
