- **Usage**: In 'I swear, ...' it is a casual way to make a strong promise or insist that something is true; often followed by a comma before the statement. Two main senses: promise or insist (swear that ..., swear to do something) and use bad language (swear at someone).

  - He swore at the driver who cut him off.

- **Antonyms**: deny, disclaim; bless (for the cursing sense)

- **Definition**: In a serious way; also a discourse marker that shows you are now saying something honest or important, or a one-word reaction expressing disbelief or annoyance.
- **Usage**: As an adverb: take something seriously (treat it as important), seriously injured. As a discourse marker at the start of a clause: 'Seriously, ...' means 'to be honest / I mean it', often after a joke. As a reaction: 'Seriously?!' shows disbelief or annoyance.
- **Examples**:

  - You should take his advice seriously.

- **Word**: expect
- **Sentence**: Dude, I don't know what I expected that to taste like.
- **Definition**: To think that something will probably happen or that someone will do something; to believe or suppose something is true; also, to consider something appropriate or required.
- **Usage**: Common patterns: expect + noun (expect rain), expect to + verb (expect to win), expect that + clause (I expect he'll come), expect someone to do something (We expected you to call), be expected to + verb (Students are expected to arrive on time). In 'what I expected that to taste like', expect takes an object 'that' plus an infinitive complement 'to taste like'.
- **Examples**:
  - I don't know what I expected that to taste like.
  - We expected the train to be late.
  - Parents expect their kids to do well in school.
- **Synonyms**: anticipate, suppose, assume, count on, foresee
- **Antonyms**: doubt, be surprised by
