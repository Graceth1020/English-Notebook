- **Word**: Unfortunately
- **Definition**: Used to say that something is sad, disappointing, or unfavorable; expresses regret or bad luck about a situation.
- **Usage**: Typically placed at the start of a sentence as a sentence adverb, often followed by a comma; also used before an adjective or adverb. Common in both formal and informal English.
- **Examples**:
  - Unfortunately, we missed the last train.
  - The event was unfortunately cancelled.
  - Unfortunately, I cannot make it to the meeting.
- **Synonyms**: regrettably, sadly, unluckily, unhappily
- **Antonyms**: fortunately, luckily, thankfully

- **Word**: conspiracy
- **Definition**: A secret plan made by two or more people to do something harmful or illegal; the act of plotting together.
- **Usage**: Often followed by to + a verb (a conspiracy to do something). Common collocations: conspiracy theory, conspiracy to commit a crime, conspiracy of silence. Used in formal and informal English, especially in legal and political contexts.
- **Examples**:
  - They were arrested for conspiracy to commit fraud.
  - The rumor about the moon landing was just a conspiracy theory.
  - There was a conspiracy of silence around the manager's resignation.
- **Synonyms**: plot, scheme, collusion, intrigue, cabal
- **Antonyms**: openness, transparency, candor

- **Word**: kidnap
- **Definition**: To take someone away illegally and keep them prisoner, usually in order to demand money or something else for their release.
- **Usage**: Transitive verb: kidnap someone. Past forms: kidnapped, kidnapping (also kidnaped/kidnaping in American English). Often followed by for ransom. Commonly used in news and crime contexts; can also be used as a noun (a kidnap).
- **Examples**:
  - The businessman was kidnapped for ransom.
  - Police rescued the kidnapped child within hours.
  - They planned to kidnap the ambassador's daughter.
- **Synonyms**: abduct, seize, capture, snatch, take hostage
- **Antonyms**: release, free, liberate, set free

- **Word**: rebound
- **Definition**: To bounce back after hitting a surface, or to recover and improve after a setback or decline.
- **Usage**: Can be an intransitive verb (the ball rebounded) or a noun (on the rebound). Common phrases: on the rebound (recently out of a romantic relationship), rebound effect. In basketball a rebound is catching the ball after a missed shot; in business and markets it means a recovery in prices or performance.
- **Examples**:
  - The ball rebounded off the wall.
  - Share prices rebounded sharply after the announcement.
  - She started dating him on the rebound.
- **Synonyms**: bounce back, recover, rally, bounce, recuperate
- **Antonyms**: decline, slump, worsen, deteriorate

- **Word**: flavor
- **Definition**: The distinctive taste of a food or drink, or a distinctive quality or character that something has.
- **Usage**: Noun: the flavor of coffee, a flavor of ice cream. Verb: flavor something with X (give it a particular taste). Common phrases: flavor of the month (popular for a short time), local flavor (distinctive character). British spelling: flavour.
- **Examples**:
  - This ice cream comes in three flavors.
  - She flavored the sauce with garlic and lemon.
  - The market adds local flavor to the town.
- **Synonyms**: taste, savor, zest, seasoning, character
- **Antonyms**: blandness, tastelessness, flatness

## 2026-08-09 11:19
- **Word**: suspicion
- **Definition**: A feeling that someone has done something wrong or that something bad may happen; a lack of trust. Also: a very small amount or hint of something.
- **Usage**: Common patterns: suspicion of (suspicion of theft), under suspicion (believed to be guilty), on suspicion of (arrested on suspicion of murder). Uncountable when referring to the feeling generally, countable for specific instances. A suspicion of means a tiny hint (a suspicion of garlic). Related verb: suspect.
- **Examples**:
  - He was arrested on suspicion of theft.
  - She looked at the stranger with suspicion.
  - Add just a suspicion of lemon to the sauce.
- **Synonyms**: distrust, doubt, misgiving, mistrust, hunch
- **Antonyms**: trust, confidence, certainty

## 2026-08-09 11:32
- **Word**: trifle
- **Definition**: Something of little value or importance; a small amount. Also: a cold dessert made of sponge cake, fruit, custard, and cream.
- **Usage**: Noun: a mere trifle (something unimportant); a trifle means slightly or a little (a trifle odd). Verb: trifle with someone or something means to treat them without respect or seriousness (not a man to be trifled with). As a dessert: a sherry trifle.
- **Examples**:
  - Don't worry about it; it's only a trifle.
  - The soup tasted a trifle salty.
  - He was not a man to be trifled with.
  - Grandma made a sherry trifle for dessert.
- **Synonyms**: triviality, detail, nothing; also bit, trace, touch (small amount)
- **Antonyms**: necessity, essential; also lot, abundance (large amount)

## 2026-08-09 11:34
- **Word**: distracting
- **Definition**: Making it difficult to pay attention to something; causing your mind to move away from what you should be focusing on.
- **Usage**: Adjective that can go before a noun (a distracting noise) or after a linking verb (the music is distracting). Often followed by from (distracting from the task). Related forms: distract (verb), distraction (noun).
- **Examples**:
  - The construction noise was very distracting.
  - She found the flashing ads distracting.
  - Stop playing games; it's distracting from your homework.
- **Synonyms**: disruptive, disturbing, unsettling, intrusive
- **Antonyms**: calming, soothing, relaxing, unobtrusive

## 2026-08-09 11:37
- **Word**: Awkward
- **Definition**: Causing difficulty, embarrassment, or discomfort; not graceful or smooth; inconvenient or hard to deal with.
- **Usage**: Multiple senses: socially uncomfortable (an awkward silence), clumsy (awkward movements), inconvenient (an awkward time), hard to handle (an awkward situation). Adverb: awkwardly; noun: awkwardness. Common phrase: feel awkward about something.
- **Examples**:
  - There was an awkward silence after his joke.
  - He made an awkward movement and dropped his cup.
  - This is an awkward time to call; can we talk later?
- **Synonyms**: embarrassing, uncomfortable, clumsy, ungainly, inconvenient
- **Antonyms**: graceful, smooth, comfortable, convenient, easy

- **Word**: secret
- **Definition**: Something kept hidden from others or known only by a small number of people; as an adjective, kept hidden or not known or seen by others.
- **Usage**: Noun: keep a secret, tell someone a secret, in secret, a closely guarded secret. Adjective: a secret plan, a secret meeting, secret agent. Common phrases: top secret, secret admirer, open secret (supposed to be secret but widely known).
- **Examples**:
  - Can you keep a secret?
  - They met in secret to plan the surprise.
  - The recipe is a closely guarded secret.
  - She received flowers from a secret admirer.
- **Synonyms**: noun: confidence, confidential matter, mystery; adjective: hidden, confidential, covert, private
- **Antonyms**: noun: public knowledge, open information; adjective: public, open, known, overt
