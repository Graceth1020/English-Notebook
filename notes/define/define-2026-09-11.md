- **Word**: for what it's worth
- **Definition**: A spoken disclaimer placed before an opinion the listener did not ask for, may not welcome, or that the speaker is unsure is useful - it lowers the stakes of the contribution in advance: you can take this or leave it. Written form: FWIW.
- **Usage**: Sentence-initial, or mid-sentence set off by commas. Three natural homes: (1) a small dissent after a decision is settled, stated once without reopening the debate; (2) an unsolicited compliment or reassurance; (3) flagging that your information may be outdated. Do not use it for your main argument (it undercuts you), not for plain facts, never stacked with other hedges, and at most once per conversation. Register: spoken and friendly, fine at work; FWIW is standard in Slack and email.
- **Examples**:
  - We're going with Wednesday. For what it's worth, I still think Friday was safer.
  - For what it's worth, I thought you handled that meeting well.
  - For what it's worth, last time I checked the docs were fine.
  - FWIW, I'd still put it closer to a week.
- **Synonyms**: just my two cents, if it helps, FWIW (written), take this with a grain of salt
- **Antonyms**: for the record (assertive, not a hedge), mark my words, clearly / obviously
