- Sentence 1: So Jensen was here two weeks ago, and I asked a question about the kind of monopoly they have on all the kind of hardware used for pre training.
- Sentence 2: So Jensen was here two weeks ago, and I asked a question about the kind of monopoly they have on all the hardware used for pre-training.
- Question: Should "kind" be singular or plural here?
- Answer: The first "kind" is correct as a singular ("the kind of monopoly they have"). The second one should be PLURAL: "all the kind_s_ of hardware used for pre-training" - or, more naturally, drop it entirely: "all the hardware used for pre-training".
- Reasoning: Rule: the number of "kind" is decided by its own determiner/quantifier, not by the noun after "of".
  Singular "kind" after: a / the / this / that / each / every / one / what / some (singular sense).
  Plural "kinds" after: all / many / several / various / different / both / these / those / a lot of / numbers.
  "the kind of monopoly they have" - determiner "the" points at one specific type of monopoly, so singular is right. "Monopoly" is also singular here, which matches.
  "all the kind of hardware" - "all" quantifies over multiple types, so the head noun must be plural: "all the kinds of hardware". Keeping "kind" singular after "all" is a spoken slip, not standard.
  Best rewrite: "all the hardware used for pre-training." "Hardware" is uncountable and already covers the whole category, so "kinds of" adds nothing. This is the version a native speaker would write.
  Related note: "kind of" also works as a hedge/approximator meaning "sort of" ("it's kind of expensive", "the kind of thing I like"). In that use it is frozen and never pluralised. The second "kind of" in the original is halfway between the classifier use and this filler use - which is exactly why it slipped out singular in speech.
  Well-known colloquialism: "these kind of things" / "those kind of people" are extremely common in speech but flagged as errors in writing; standard forms are "these kinds of things" / "those kinds of people" or "this kind of thing".
  Separate fix: "pre training" should be hyphenated or closed up - "pre-training" or "pretraining".
