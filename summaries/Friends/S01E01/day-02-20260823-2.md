## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 7. Do not drop the negative in "don't feel like"

Original: "If you don't feel like being alone tonight, Joey and Chandler are helping me with my furniture."
My version: "If you want to be alone tonight, ..."
Model: "If you don't want to be alone tonight, ..."

"Feel like + -ing" means "want to", so "don't feel like being alone" is
"don't want to be alone". Converting the phrase but keeping the negative is
the whole job here; without it, Monica is inviting Rachel to come along only
if she wants to be by herself.

### 2. "Buy" takes "for", not "to"

Original: "I guess he bought her the pipe organ, and she's really not happy about it."
My version: "I guess he bought the pipe organ to her, ..."
Model: "I guess he bought the pipe organ for her, ..."

Two patterns exist: "buy her the pipe organ" (no preposition) or "buy the
pipe organ for her". Once you move the person after the object, the
preposition must be "for". "To" belongs with verbs of transfer like "give" and
"send".

### 9. "Get through" and "go through" are not interchangeable

Original: "How did you get through it?"
My version: "How did you go through it?"
Model: "How did you cope with it?"

"Go through" means to experience something; "get through" means to survive it
and come out the other side. The question asks about surviving, so "go
through" leaves the sentence incomplete in meaning. "Cope with" is the safe
plain substitute.

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "get out of + place" / "get away from + place": "I had to get away from there."
- "buy someone something" = "buy something for someone": "He bought the pipe organ for her."
- "I guess we've established + clause" (mock-formal conclusion): "I guess we've confirmed she's staying with Monica."
- "come to someone" / "turn to someone" (for help): "You can always turn to Joey if you need anything."
- "hit on someone" / "flirt with someone": "Joey, quit flirting with her!"
- "buzz someone in" / "let someone in": "Buzz him in."
- "feel like + -ing" = "want to": "If you don't feel like being alone tonight ..."
- "should've + past participle" for late realization: "I should've seen that coming."
- "get through something" (survive it) vs "go through something" (experience it): "How did you get through it?"
- "cut someone off" (interrupt or disconnect): "The machine cut me off again."
