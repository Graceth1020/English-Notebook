frames: "there has to be", "all of a sudden", "it turns out", "go through",

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 4. "It turns out" is the turn in the story

Original: "And it turns out it's my mother."
My version: "And that's my mother."
Model: "And it turns out it's my mom."

"It turns out" tells the listener that what comes next was unexpected. Without
it the sentence is just a label on the caller, and the moment that made the
story worth telling disappears. Keep the frame and change only what follows
it.

### 10. A modal replaces "is", it does not follow it

Original: "This is probably for the best, you know?"
My version: "This is might for the best, you know?"
Model: "This might be for the best, you know?"

"Might" is a modal verb, so it takes the position of the main verb and pulls
"be" after it. The hedge itself was a good idea: "probably" and "might" both
soften the claim, and the tag "you know?" was kept correctly.

### 7. "Keep + -ing" is what sounds annoyed

Original: "Why does everyone keep fixating on that?"
My version: "Why is everyone focus on that?"
Model: "Why does everyone keep focusing on that?"

Two things: after "is" the verb needs "-ing", and "keep + -ing" is the part
that says it happens over and over. Dropping "keep" turns a complaint into a
neutral question.

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "There's gotta be ..." (spoken form of "there has to be"): "There's gotta be something wrong with him."
- "go through + noun/clause" (live through something bad): "I don't want her to go through what I went through with Carl."
- "all of a sudden" / "out of nowhere": "All of a sudden, the phone starts to ring."
- "it turns out + clause": "And it turns out it's my mom."
- "move ... out" (separable): "Carol moved her stuff out today."
- "leave ... alone" / "lay off ...": "Just leave my aura alone, okay?"
- "keep + -ing" (repeated, annoying action): "Why does everyone keep focusing on that?"
- "freak out" and "it hit me": "And then it hit me, and I totally freaked out."
- "kind of + -ing" as a hedge: "I was kind of hoping that wouldn't be a problem."
- "This might be for the best, you know?" (hedged consolation with a tag)

## How This Builds Speaking

The three majors and the four blanks point the same way: the frames are
understood when read but not yet available when speaking, especially the
phrasal verbs ("go through", "move out", "leave alone", "freak out"). Read the
ten corrected versions aloud three times, one breath each, then say sentences
2, 5, 6, and 8 again from the Chinese column without looking at the English.
Before the next round, plant "it turns out ..." and "kind of hoping" into two
sentences about your own day.
