## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 6. Wordplay is the point

Original: "Will you join us in holy matri-gnome-y?"
My version: "Will you join us in holy gnome marry?"
Model: "Will you join us in holy gnome-mony?"

"Matri-gnome-y" is a pun on "matrimony": the gnomes replaced the middle of
the word with "gnome". The joke is the whole point, so keep a gnome-based
pun ("gnome-mony") instead of translating the meaning into plain words.

### 10. Fixed flavor phrases

Original: "Bada-bing, bada-bam!"
My version: "Done!"
Model: "Bada-bing, bada-bam!" or "And that's that!"

"Bada-bing, bada-bam" is a fixed phrase that marks something done neatly and
with flair. "Done" communicates the result but drops the rhythm and the
flavor. When the source is a fixed phrase, the best rephrase is often a
different fixed phrase.

### 3. A rephrase must change something

Original: "Ooh! Little muscle there."
My version: (identical)
Model: "Ooh, I see a little muscle there!"

The original was copied back. Small changes count: adding "I see" turns it
into a real rephrase.

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "They shipped us up north to... and we stayed at..."
- "My suspicions have been verified."
- "He's going to eat your brain!"
- "I'm not gonna let you ruin it with your crazy plots!"
- "Will you join us in holy gnome-mony?"
- "Bada-bing, bada-bam!"

## How This Builds Speaking

This round shows what steady practice does: the long causal sentence held,
the threats stayed casual, and the teasing tone survived. The two misses are
about flavor, keeping a pun or a fixed phrase whole instead of translating
it to plain words. Follow up by saying "matri-gnome-y" and "bada-bing,
bada-bam" aloud, and by making one small change whenever you rephrase a
line.
