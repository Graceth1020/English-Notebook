# Day 6 - Tourist Trapped: idioms and phrasal verbs

Date: 2026-08-05 | Source: Gravity Falls S01E01 Tourist Trapped | Level: Tier 2 ([M])

## Today's Practice

Ten idioms and phrasal verbs: look on the bright side, spill the beans,
freak out, mess with, look out for, do the trick. The idiom meanings were
mostly caught, but the grammar skeleton around them wobbled: gerund subjects,
participle forms, and active versus passive all slipped. The lesson is that
an idiom is fixed, but the sentence around it still follows grammar rules.

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 6. A verb cannot sit in the subject slot

Original: "Better safe than sorry!"
My version: "Be safe is better than sorry!"
Model: "It's better to be safe than sorry!"

"Be safe" is a verb form, and English does not put bare verbs in the subject
position. You need a gerund ("Being safe is better...") or the fixed idiom
("Better safe than sorry!").

### 9. Concerned, not concerning

Original: "You really were just looking out for me."
My version: "You really were concerning about me."
Model: "You really were just looking out for me."

"Concerning" is a preposition or an adjective about content ("a concerning
report"). When a person cares about someone, the form is "concerned about".
The simplest fix is to keep the phrasal verb itself.

### 10. Passive voice is not a style choice here

Original: "That oughta do the trick."
My version: "That might be worked."
Model: "That should do it."

"Worked" as a passive participle means employed or processed; "it worked"
does not passivize naturally. The everyday alternatives are active: "That
should work", "That should do it", "That might just work".

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "My sister always looked for the positive side of things."
- "It's better to be safe than sorry!"
- "Don't panic, okay?"
- "You've picked the wrong person to mess with."
- "That should do it."

## How This Builds Speaking

Idioms come as whole chunks, which is why the meanings were caught. The
grammar slips show that chunks still need a correct sentence around them.
Follow up by saying the three fixed forms aloud: "better safe than sorry",
"concerned about", and "that should do it".
