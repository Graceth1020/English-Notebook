## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 7. Conceding is not agreeing

Original: "Fair enough."
My version: "That's right."
Model: "That's fair."

"Fair enough" is a concession: you accept that the other person has a
reasonable point, even if you do not fully agree. "That's right" means the
person is correct. In a debate, mixing them up changes your position.

### 5. "So bad" goes too far

Original: "Oh! Oh! Not good."
My version: "Oh! Oh! So bad."
Model: "Oh no, this is bad."

"So bad" reads as "extremely bad". The original is a smaller alarm, so keep
the level: "this is bad" or "that doesn't look good".

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "Are you hurt?"
- "You need to have a seat?"
- "Oh, hold on. I'm sorry."
- "That's fair." / "Fair point."
- "We are alone at last."
- "I am the girl of your dreams."

## How This Builds Speaking

This round shows automaticity building: most alternatives came out without
translating. The "fair enough" slip matters for real conversation, where
conceding and agreeing pull a discussion in different directions. Follow up
by saying "That's fair" and "Fair point" aloud a few times so they are ready
when you need them.
