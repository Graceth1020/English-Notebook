## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 1. "Look" is not "let's say"

Original: "Look, dude, I believe you."
My version: "Let's say, dude, I trust you."
Model: "Look, man, I believe you."

"Look" as a discourse marker means "listen, here is my point". "Let's say"
introduces a hypothetical example. Mixing them makes the sentence sound like
an imaginary case instead of a real claim.

### 4. "Wouldn't you know it" is a fixed idiom

Original: "Wouldn't you know it?"
My version: "Don't you know it?"
Model: "Wouldn't you know it?"

The phrase is a fixed reaction to coincidence or irony, like "what do you
know". "Don't you know it" changes the meaning into an assertion that the
listener knows something. Keep the idiom whole.

### 7. One greeting, not a mix

Original: "How's it hangin'?"
My version: "How's it going on?"
Model: "How's it going?"

"How's it going?" and "How's it hanging?" are both greetings, but "going on"
is not a greeting at all. Choose one greeting and use it whole.

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "Look, man, I believe you."
- "Otherwise people are gonna think you're crazy."
- "For the third time, it's not a real face!"
- "Wouldn't you know it?"
- "This girl has a date!"

## How This Builds Speaking

Discourse markers are the hinges of real conversation: they open claims,
react to coincidence, and pivot a point. They are fixed, so the fastest way
to own them is to repeat the whole chunk. Follow up by saying "Look, man...",
"Wouldn't you know it?", and "How's it going?" aloud until they feel like
one piece.
