# Day 2 - Tourist Trapped: short reactions and quick responses

Date: 2026-08-04 | Source: Gravity Falls S01E01 Tourist Trapped | Level: Tier 1

## Today's Practice

Ten short everyday bursts: warnings, pauses, quick acknowledgements, and
reactions. The warnings and thanks came out naturally, but several rephrases
drifted in meaning or copied Chinese word order into English. The lesson is
to reach for the fixed spoken form of a reaction before translating it word
for word.

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 1. "Oh", not "on"

Original: "Oh, my gosh, you like turtles?"
My version: "On my goodness, you like turtles?"
Model: "Oh my goodness, you like turtles?"

"Oh my goodness" is a fixed exclamation; "on" changes the phrase into a
preposition and breaks it.

### 6. "Make it" means something else

Original: "I'm on it."
My version: "I'll make it."
Model: "I've got it."

"Make it" usually means arrive somewhere or succeed ("I'll make it to the
party"). "I'm on it" means you are handling the task right now, so "I've got
it" or "On it" keeps the meaning.

### 10. English loves its fixed phrases

Original: "Oh! I love jam!"
My version: "Oh! Jam is my heart!"
Model: "Oh! I'm crazy about jam!"

"X is my heart" follows Chinese word order. English reacts with set phrases:
"I'm crazy about jam", "Jam is my favorite", "I absolutely love jam".

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "Oh my goodness, you like turtles?"
- "Wait a minute!"
- "Watch out!"
- "I've got it."
- "That did the trick!"
- "Check this out!"
- "I'm crazy about..."

## How This Builds Speaking

Short reaction phrases are the glue of real conversation, and they are fixed:
you either know the spoken form or you translate and sound wrong. This day
shows why drilling these chunks beats composing from grammar rules. Follow up
by reading the corrected versions aloud three times each, especially
"I've got it" and "I'm crazy about jam".
