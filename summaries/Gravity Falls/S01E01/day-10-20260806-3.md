# Day 10 - Tourist Trapped: fixed expressions with prepositions

Date: 2026-08-06 (round 3) | Source: Gravity Falls S01E01 Tourist Trapped | Level: Tier 2 ([M])

## Today's Practice

Ten fixed expressions built on prepositions and particles. The core meaning
was caught in most sentences, and three came out well. The main slips were
confusing similar phrases ("make sure" for "pretty sure"), dropping
particles ("gone with" for "getting away with"), and one blank ("long story
short") that was taught below. Fixed expressions resist word-for-word
swaps, which is exactly what this round practiced.

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 2. "Pretty sure" is not "make sure"

Original: "Pretty sure that dude's a werewolf."
My version: "Make sure that dude's a werewolf."
Model: "I'm pretty sure that dude's a werewolf."

"Pretty sure" reports a strong belief. "Make sure" means to check or ensure
something happens. They look similar but do different jobs, and the
sentence needs the belief version.

### 9. "Get away with" is the phrasal verb

Original: "He's getting away with our queen!"
My version: "He's gone with our queen."
Model: "He's getting away with our queen!"

"Get away with" means to escape while taking something. "Gone with" states
departure but loses the escaping nuance, and it changes the urgency of the
line.

### 8. "Way off" is one fixed piece

Original: "Gnomes? Huh. I was way off."
My version: "Gnomes? Huh, what I guess is wrong."
Model: "Gnomes? Huh, I was way off."

"Way off" is the fixed phrase for "completely wrong in my guess". The
rebuilt version "what I guess is wrong" is grammatical but wordy and not
what a speaker says.

## Don't Know - Taught Today

### 6. "Long story short"

Meaning: 长话短说，略过细节、直接说重点。Used to skip the background and get
to the point.
Model: "Anyway, to make a long story short, we gnomes have been looking for
a new queen."
Note: the full form is "to make a long story short"; in speech it often
shortens to "long story short".

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "It's a quandary, to be sure."
- "I'm pretty sure that dude's a werewolf."
- "It was time to go get some evidence."
- "First off, let's get that out of the way."
- "To make a long story short..."
- "I was way off."

## How This Builds Speaking

Fixed expressions are the fastest shortcut to natural speech, but only if
they come as whole pieces. Splitting them ("what I guess is wrong") or
swapping lookalikes ("make sure" for "pretty sure") is what gives learners
away. Follow up by saying the five fixed phrases aloud whole, especially
"I was way off" and "long story short".
