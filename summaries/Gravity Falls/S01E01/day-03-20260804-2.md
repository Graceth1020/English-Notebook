## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 6 and 7. You tell a joke, you do not play it

Original: "I'm joking!" / "I'm not joking!"
My versions: "I'm playing a joke!" / "I'm not playing a joke."
Model: "I'm just kidding!" / "I'm serious!"

English collocates "joke" with "tell" ("tell a joke") and "play" with
"prank" or "trick" ("play a prank on someone"). The everyday spoken forms
are "I'm kidding" and "I'm serious".

### 8. Funny is not interesting

Original: "That is not funny, Dipper."
My version: "It is not interesting, Dipper."
Model: "That's not funny, Dipper."

"Funny" means humorous; "interesting" means intellectually engaging. They
are different feelings, and in this scene the complaint is about humor, so
the word must stay "funny" or move to "amusing".

### 10. Greet takes no "to"

Original: "Say hello to my new boyfriend!"
My version: "Greet to my new boyfriend!"
Model: "Say hi to my new boyfriend!"

"Greet" is transitive and takes a direct object: "greet him". "Greet to" is
ungrammatical, and "greet" sounds formal anyway; "say hi to" is the casual
spoken form.

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "I'm crazy about turtles."
- "What is going on here?"
- "It's coming closer!"
- "I'm just kidding!" / "I'm serious!"
- "Say hi to my new boyfriend!"
