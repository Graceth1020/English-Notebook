## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 6. The dramatic echo

Original: "The fishes?! They seek revenge!"
Model: "The fish?! They're out for revenge!"

The joke has two moves: repeat a word with disbelief (echo), then give the
fish a human plan ("seek revenge"). "Seek revenge" is literary; casual
speech says "out for revenge" or "want revenge". Say the echo with rising
tone so the disbelief lands.

### 9. "Would", not "could"

Original: "Why would that make me feel better?!"
My version: "Why could that make me feel better?!"
Model: "Why would that make me feel better?!"

"Would" sets up the sarcastic hypothetical, "even if that happened, it
wouldn't help". "Could" asks whether it is possible, which is not the joke.
The same frame works for "Why would I do that?" and "Why would anyone care?"

### 2. Keep both halves of the parallel

Original: "Like when my wife left me and I created a homicidal pterodactyl-tron, or when my pal Ernie didn't come to my retirement party and I constructed an 80-ton shame-bot..."
My version: "...I created a homicidal pterodactyl-tron when my wife left me, or when my pal Ernie didn't come..."
Model: "It's like when my wife left me and I built a homicidal pterodactyl-tron, or when my pal Ernie didn't show up to my retirement party and I built an 80-ton shame-bot..."

The comedy lives in the matching rhythm: "when A happened and I built X, or
when B happened and I built Y". Keep the same order in both halves, and the
formal verbs can drop to casual ones ("constructed" -> "built", "exploded"
-> "blew up").

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "Now that + clause": "Now that we're alone, Rosanna..."
- "A burning question" / "I've been dying to ask": "...there's a burning question I've been dying to ask you."
- Parallel "like when ... or when ...": "It's like when my wife left me and I built a pterodactyl-tron, or when my pal Ernie didn't show up and I built a shame-bot..."
- "Side character" / "minor character": "Dude, am I a side character?"
- Parallel role assignment: "You'll be lookout, Zeus can work the steering wheel, and I'll be captain."
- Repetition emphasis: "This is it. This is it!"
- Dramatic echo + personification: "The fishes?! They're out for revenge!"
- "Anything for ...": "Anything for my fishin' buddies!"
- "A secret from ... to another" / "What's said between ... stays between ...": "That's a secret from one fishin' buddy to another."
- Sarcastic "Why would that ...?!": "Why would that make me feel better?!"
- "All of a sudden": "Why do you want to bond with us all of a sudden?"
