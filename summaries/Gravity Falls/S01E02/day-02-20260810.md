## Today's Practice

Day 2 practiced discourse markers and hedging: the small words that frame a
statement, soften a doubt, or buy thinking time (well, you see, but
seriously, maybe, I don't know). The round showed a real strength with
natural word substitutes ("competition" for "contest", "so difficult" for
"that hard"), and a clear target: fixed chunks and their grammar ("on it",
"worth doing", "family fun"). Four rephrases needed major fixes and one was
left blank.

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 6. "On it" means handling it, not looking at it

Original: "But seriously, I'm on it."
My version: "But honestly, I am looking at it."
Model: "But honestly, I've got it covered."

"On it" is an idiom for "I'm dealing with it"; "looking at it" describes
examining something, which changes the meaning. The model keeps the honest
marker and uses the natural equivalent "I've got it covered".

### 7. The pattern is "worth doing", not "worth of doing"

Original: "Dude, I don't know, man, maybe this isn't worth it."
My version: "Dude, I don't know whether it is worth of doing it."
Model: "Dude, I don't know, man, maybe it's not worth doing."

"Worth" is followed directly by a noun or an -ing form ("worth it", "worth
doing"); "worth of doing" is not English. "Whether" also makes it sound like
a two-option choice, while "maybe" keeps the softer doubt.

### 1. "Family fun" is time together, not an interest

Original: "But I swear, today we're gonna have some real family fun."
My version: "But I promise, today we're gonna have some real family interest."
Model: "But I promise, today we're gonna have a real good time together."

"Fun" here means enjoyable activities or a good time; "interest" means
something you are curious about. "Promise" was a good substitute for "swear".

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "I swear, ..." promise frame: "But I swear, today we're gonna have some real family fun."
- "I think ... actually ..." hedge: "I think he actually wants to fish with us."
- "Well, that happened." deadpan response
- "You see what I'm doing here?" checking understanding ("get" = understand)
- "I don't know what I expected ... to ...": "I don't know what I expected that to taste like."
- "I'm on it." / "I've got it covered.": "But seriously, I'm on it."
- "not worth it" / "not worth doing": "maybe this isn't worth it."
- "after all" hedge: "Maybe that old guy was crazy after all."
- "It's not that hard, all right?" casual tag question
- "so much for ...": "Well, so much for the photo contest."

## How This Builds Speaking

Discourse markers and hedges are the glue of real conversation: they frame
what you are about to say and soften how it lands. The errors this round
came from fixed chunks, exactly the pieces that need to become automatic.
Read the model versions aloud three times, then plant "so much for ..." and
"not worth doing" into two sentences of your own before the next round.
