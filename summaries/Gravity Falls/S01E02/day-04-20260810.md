## Today's Practice

Day 4 closed out the medium block with longer sentences that carry two moves
each: a phrasal verb or fixed phrase (throw away, keep company, ditch over

discourse frame. All ten sentences were answered with no blanks and no

## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 5. "Become success" is not English

Original: "Tell me, Dipper, what's the secret to your success?"
My version: "Tell me, Dipper, How do you become success?"
Model: "Tell me, Dipper, what's your secret to success?"

"Success" is a noun; to describe the person you need the adjective
"successful". The easiest fix is to keep the original frame, "what's your
secret to success", and drop the "How" question.

### 6. "Abandon" is a dictionary word, "ditch" is what we say

Original: "We ditched him over nothing."
My version: "We abondonded him over nothing."
Model: "We bailed on him over nothing."

Besides the spelling ("abandoned"), "abandon" sounds like a novel or a news
report. In casual speech you "ditch" someone or "bail on" them.

### 8. "Got" means already, "gotta take" means future

Original: "Dude, if it makes you feel any better, I got tons of pictures of those beavers, dude!"
My version: "Dude, I gotta take tons of pictures of those beavers if you feel any better by this."
Model: "Dude, if that makes you feel any better, I took tons of pictures of those beavers."

The comfort depends on the pictures already existing ("I got"), but "I gotta
take" pushes them into the future, which weakens the joke. Keep the order of
the original, "if it/that makes you feel any better, ...", so the condition
comes first.

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "It's just gonna be ..." (who/what list): "It's just gonna be you, me, and those goofy hats on a boat for ten hours!"
- "keep (someone) company": "I got a whole box of creepy fishin' lures to keep me company."
- "throw away" / "chuck": "Dude, I just threw two away."
- "aren't you supposed to be + -ing?": "Aren't you supposed to be doing lookout?"
- "what's the secret to ...?": "Tell me, Dipper, what's the secret to your success?"
- "ditch (someone) over nothing" / "bail on": "We ditched him over nothing."
- "all you gotta do is + verb": "All you gotta do is point and shoot."
- "if it makes you feel any better, ...": "If it makes you feel any better, I got tons of pictures of those beavers."
- "catch someone's fancy": "Maybe I'd catch his fancy with a 15-ton aquatic robot."
- "so I guess ...?": "So I guess there isn't room in that boat for three more?"

## How This Builds Speaking

The pattern this round was protection: the phrasal verbs and idioms you know
get replaced by safer formal words when the sentence gets longer ("accompany",
"abandon", "presume"). Reading the model versions aloud keeps those fixed
chunks on the tongue, then plant "all you gotta do is ..." and "if it makes
you feel any better, ..." into two real sentences before the next round.
