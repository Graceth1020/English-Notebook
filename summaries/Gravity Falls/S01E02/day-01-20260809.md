## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 4. Might as well is one fixed phrase

Original: "Ha ha! Nah, but with these cataracts, I might as well be."
My version: "I might be with these cataracts as well."
Model: "Nah, but with eyes this bad, I might as well be blind."

"Might as well" is a fixed chunk meaning "it makes no difference, so I'll
accept it". Splitting it into "might be ... as well" turns it into "also",
which loses the joke: with cataracts this bad, being blind would be no
different.

### 10. There is no such thing as

Original: "There's no such thing as co-captain."
My version: "There's nothing likes co-captain."
Model: "There's no such thing as a co-captain."

"No such thing as" is the fixed phrase for denying that something exists.
"Nothing likes" mixes "nothing" with a verb form, which English does not do
here.

### 1. The tag must match the subject

Original: "We didn't get any photos of those gnomes, did we?"
My version: "We've not got any photos of these gnomes, have me?"
Model: "We never got a single shot of those gnomes, did we?"

A tag question repeats the subject ("we") and the tense, and flips positive
to negative. "Have me?" uses the wrong person, and "have got" moves the
event from the past into the present.

## Patterns I Can Use Today

- "We never got a single shot of ..., did we?"
- "Is this going to be anything like ...?"
- "I might as well ..."
- "Let's figure this out."
- "There's no such thing as ..."

## How This Builds Speaking

This round forced active recall of fixed chunks, and the errors show where
automaticity is still missing: "might as well" and "no such thing as" fall
apart under pressure because they have only been met once. Read the model
versions aloud three times, then plant "might as well" and "no such thing
as" into two sentences of your own before the next round.
