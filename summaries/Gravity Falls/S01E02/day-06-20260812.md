## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 2. "Precise" is not "quality"

Original: "That's some quality family bonding!"
My version: "That's some precise family bonding!"
Model: "That's some real family bonding!"

"Precise" means exact and accurate; "quality" here means good and
meaningful. "That's some + adjective + noun" is the emphasis frame, and the
modifier has to describe how good the thing is.

### 6. "Fantastic" breaks the joke

Original: "Ya see, it's funny because marriage is terrible."
My version: "Ya see, it's fantastic because marriage is awful."
Model: "Ya see, it's funny because marriage is awful."

In "it's funny because ...", "funny" means humorous. "Fantastic" means
great, which reverses the deadpan logic of the joke. "Awful" for "terrible"
was a fine swap.

### 7. A challenge is not a clarification

Original: "Hey, there! What's the big idea?"
My version: "Hey,there! What do you mean?"
Model: "Hey, there! What do you think you're doing?"

"What's the big idea?" is an indignant challenge about what someone is
doing. "What do you mean?" politely asks for an explanation, so the function
changed. Also add the space in "Hey, there!".

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- "You call that + noun?" / "That's supposed to be + noun?": "You call that Ben Franklin?"
- "That's some + adjective + noun" emphasis: "That's some quality family bonding!"
- "A matter of grave danger": "No! That jig is a matter of grave danger!"
- "Who needs 'em?" dismissive rhetorical question: "Ingrates! Ah, who needs 'em?"
- "Say + clause" hypothetical: "Say bigfoot shows up."
- "It's funny because ..." deadpan: "It's funny because marriage is terrible."
- "What's the big idea?" indignant challenge
- "Pop into my head": "It just popped into my head."
- Bet frame "Five bucks says ..." / "I bet you five bucks ..." + "You're on!"
- "Why did ... because ..." joke frame with a pun: "Because he had a very big bill!"

## How This Builds Speaking

Jokes are the strictest test of word choice: swapping "funny" for
"fantastic" looks close on paper but kills the punchline, which is exactly
the risk synonym-hunting creates when you speak fast. The two taught idioms
("who needs 'em?", "pop into my head") are dismissive and storytelling
chunks you can drop into real conversation. Read the model versions aloud
three times, then plant "that's some + noun" and "You're on!" into two
sentences of your own before the next round.
