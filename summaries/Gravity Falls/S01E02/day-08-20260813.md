## Corrections Worth Remembering

### 4. "Ya see" is a filler, not a verb in progress

Original: "Ha ha! Ya see, the thing about that is--"
My version: "Ha ha! You are seeing the thing about that is..."
Model: "Ha! You see, the thing about that is--"

"Ya see" is just the casual "you see" used to hold the floor, like "you
know". "You are seeing" turns it into a progressive action and the frame
collapses. The structure "the thing about that is ..." is how you open an
explanation with a pause.

### 2. The "nothing ... except when ..." contrast

Original: "Well, I run away from nothing... Nothing except for when I ran away from my annoying Grunkle Stan who I ditched in order to pursue that lake monster."
Model: "Well, I never run away from anything... Except when I ditched my annoying Grunkle Stan to chase that lake monster."

The shape is a boast followed by its one exception: "nothing ... except for
when ...". The phrasal verbs carry the joke ("run away from", "ditch") and
"in order to" can shorten to "to" ("to chase"). Say the model aloud so the
rhythm of the two-part joke gets onto your tongue.

### 1. The sarcastic "you could ... or you could ..." offer

Original: "You kids could go waste your time on some epic monster-finding adventure, or you could spend the day learning how to tie knots and skewer worms with your great uncle Stan!"
Model: "You kids could waste your time on some epic monster hunt, or you could spend the day learning to tie knots and skewer worms with your great uncle Stan!"

This is a fake choice: both options sound unappealing, and the parallelism
"you could ... or you could ..." is what makes the sarcasm land. Rehearse it
in halves, the adventure half then the Stan half.

## Common Phrases and Fixed Structures

- Sarcastic "you could ... or you could ...": "You kids could waste your time on some epic monster hunt, or you could spend the day learning to tie knots..."
- "Run away from nothing ... except for when ...": "I run away from nothing... Nothing except for when I ran away from my annoying Grunkle Stan."
- "In order to + verb" / "to + verb" purpose: "...who I ditched in order to pursue that lake monster."
- "The thing about that is ...": "Ha! You see, the thing about that is--"
- Formal-comedy echo "Granted. Permission co-granted." / "Approved. Permission co-approved."
- "I like those odds!" / "I'll take those odds!"
- "Hang out with": "The only dinosaur we want to hang out with is right here."
- "Were off + -ing" reported thought: "I thought you two were off playin' spin the bottle with Zeus."
- Casual challenge "ever seen me + verb": "You knuckleheads ever seen me thread a hook with my eyes closed?"
- "Long story" / "It's a long story": "Whoa! What happened to your shirt? Long story, dude."

## How This Builds Speaking

Long lines went blank and short fixed phrases got copied, which means the
patterns are recognized but not yet producible. The fix is rehearsal in
pieces: say the long contrasts in halves, and swap one word in every fixed
phrase ("take" for "like", "figured" for "thought") so each one becomes
yours. Read the model versions aloud three times, then plant "you could ...
or you could ..." and "the thing about that is ..." into two sentences of
your own before the next round.
