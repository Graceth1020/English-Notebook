# Pattern 06 - fronted participle clause

Date: 2026-09-10 | Pattern: P006 | Status: done | Rounds so far: 3

Requested by the learner after meeting `Looking at his watch, he saw that it was
one o'clock` in NCE-02 and asking what the grammar was.

This is the same-subject half of `P001`, and it was already named in Drill 01's
summary - *"never With me sitting in the library..."* - but only as a footnote
explaining why `with me` was wrong there. It was never drilled on its own, so the
learner knows the prohibition without owning the alternative.

The two patterns are one operation split by a single test:

  subject of the extra fact DIFFERENT from the main clause  ->  with + X + doing
  subject of the extra fact SAME as the main clause         ->  bare participle

That makes this drill the natural sequel to 01 rather than a new topic, and it is
why Round 2 mixes the two: choosing between them is the actual skill.

Related error rows, all participle-vs-finite confusion: `E053` (With the change
touched), `E011` (Whenever working from home - a subject wrongly dropped, the
mirror image of this pattern's rule), `E005` (I usually learning), `E033`
(tired/tiring).

Round 1 (join two given sentences, participle up front) - can it be built.
Round 2 (mixed) - carries three decisions:
  - `-ing` vs `-ed` vs `having + -ed`
  - participle vs `with + X + doing` (the P001 boundary)
  - one dangling-participle trap, where the subjects differ and a bare participle
    would therefore be wrong
Round 3 (situation only) - 4 items.

The rule being taught:

  The participle has no subject of its own, so it BORROWS the subject of the
  main clause. Whatever comes after the comma is who is doing the -ing.

    Looking at his watch,  HE saw it was one o'clock.   he looked, he saw
    Sitting in the library, I can actually focus.        I sit, I focus

  Break that and the sentence says something absurd - "Looking at his watch, the
  meeting had already started" means the meeting looked at its watch. The test is
  mechanical: read the noun straight after the comma and ask whether it is doing
  the participle.

  Form marks the relationship, not the tense:
    -ing          the subject is doing it        Looking at his watch, he saw...
    -ed           it is done to the subject      Written in 1890, the book still...
    having + -ed  finished first, then the main  Having finished the ticket, I left.

Watch for: a dangling participle in Round 3 (the most likely failure once no
sentences are given), `having + -ed` avoided entirely in favour of `after I...`,
and the E053 instinct - a finite verb where a participle belongs.

## Round 1 - join two given sentences

**1.** I sit in the library. I can actually focus.
**中文：**坐在图书馆里，我才能真正专注。

**2.** He looked at the logs. He found the API key was wrong.
**中文：**看了日志之后，他发现 API key 是错的。

**3.** I work from home. I get more done than in the office.
**中文：**在家办公时，我比在公司做得更多。

**4.** She grew up in a traditional Chinese family. She has always been careful about her health.
**中文：**在传统中国家庭长大，她一直很注意健康。

## Round 2 - mixed, choose the right shape

**1.** I finished the ticket. Then I went home. (the first action completes before the second)
**中文：**做完 ticket 之后，我就回家了。

**2.** The change touches the core logic. I have got to test it properly. (careful - who is doing what?)
**中文：**改动碰到了核心逻辑，我得好好测。

**3.** The book was written in 1890. It still reads well today.
**中文：**这本书写于 1890 年，今天读起来依然很好。

**4.** Everyone books at the same time. Tickets are impossible to get. (careful - same subject or different?)
**中文：**大家同时抢票，票根本抢不到。

## Round 3 - free production

**1.** Describe what happens when you drive back to Jieyang and hit the holiday traffic.
**中文：**描述你国庆开车回揭阳遇上堵车的情形。

**2.** Explain how you feel after you have read for two hours in the library.
**中文：**说说你在图书馆读了两小时书之后的感受。

**3.** Tell a teammate what you noticed when you went through the logs on Saturday.
**中文：**跟同事说你周六查日志时发现了什么。

**4.** Say what you do first thing after you shut your laptop on a Friday.
**中文：**说说周五合上电脑之后你第一件事做什么。
