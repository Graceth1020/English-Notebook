# Pattern 14 - stacked time adverbials (date + clock time)

Date: 2026-10-17 | Pattern: P013 | Status: open | Rounds so far: 1

Learner-raised gap, from Drill 16 R3: "At midnight of last Saturday" - an of
inserted between the date and the clock time, where English chains the pieces
directly. Follow-up question the same day: how do date + morning/afternoon +
clock time combinations work in general?

The frame: English stacks time adverbials like an address, small unit first,
and each part keeps only its own preposition:

  at + clock time (at midnight, at 6:30, at noon)
  on + day-bound part (on Friday morning, on the evening of the 15th)
  in + bare part of day (in the morning)
  date/month/year chain on with no glue (last Saturday, October 15th, 2026)

Full: at 6:30 on Friday morning last week.

Rules being trained:
1. Small to large, zero glue - at midnight last Saturday, never "of".
2. Every part keeps its own preposition - at the clock, on the dated part,
   in the bare morning.
3. last/this/next/yesterday strip all prepositions - last Saturday, not
   "on last Saturday"; yesterday evening, not "in yesterday evening".
4. A specific date inside a part-of-day hangs on "of": on the morning of
   October 15th (this is the only legal "of" in the whole family).

Round 1: assemble from given pieces (the preposition is the discriminating
item, not the order). Round 2 will embed the stacks in full sentences.
Round 3: free production - narrate yesterday with three stacked times.

## Round 1 - build the time phrase

**1.** Pieces: at 6:30 / this morning
**中文：** 今天早上 6 点半

**2.** Pieces: at midnight / last Saturday
**中文：** 上周六半夜

**3.** Pieces: at 9 am / Monday
**中文：** 周一早上 9 点

**4.** Pieces: on the evening / October 15th
**中文：** 10 月 15 号晚上

**5.** Pieces: around 3 / yesterday afternoon
**中文：** 昨天下午 3 点左右

**6.** Pieces: at noon / the day of the release
**中文：** 发布当天中午

## Round 1 outcome

6/6 on first pass, zero retries. The discriminating cases all landed: item 2
(the original Drill 16 error, at midnight last Saturday, no "of"), item 4
(the one legal "of": on the evening of October 15th), items 1 and 5 (this /
yesterday stripping prepositions). Assembly is recognition-level clean - the
real test is Round 2's embedding inside full sentences, where frame-anchoring
(tense choice around the time stack) starts to matter.

## Round 2 - embed the stack in a sentence

**1.** 警报在周一晚上 9:40 响了。
**中文：** 警报在周一晚上 9:40 响了。

**2.** 我昨天下午 3 点左右把修复发到了生产环境。
**中文：** 我昨天下午 3 点左右把修复发到了生产环境。

**3.** 复盘会定在 10 月 15 号早上 10 点。
**中文：** 复盘会定在 10 月 15 号早上 10 点。

**4.** 到上周六半夜为止，我们已经回滚了版本。
**中文：** 到上周六半夜为止，我们已经回滚了版本。

## Round 2 outcome

1.5/4. The stacks themselves were correct in all four items - every error
lived AROUND the time phrase, which is exactly what Round 2 exists to expose:
item 2 redundant 3 pm + yesterday afternoon (pick one); item 3 "will be
holding" -> will be held / is scheduled for (hold active needs a human
subject), plus postmortem vs review meeting vocabulary; item 4 "by the
midnight" -> by midnight (midnight/noon as clock points are always bare) -
but the past perfect trigger by ... -> we'd rolled back was produced
correctly, the item's real target. Item 1 clean (went off, correct stack).

## Round 3 - free production

Narrate yesterday in 3-4 sentences with at least three stacked time
adverbials, one including a part-of-day. Tense frame consistent.

## Round 3 outcome

Structure 3/3: around 8 am yesterday, at 10:30 am, by 9 in the evening -
prepositions and ordering all correct, zero-glue rule held. One semantic
trap caught: "at 12 am" for midday - the classic meridiem flip (12 am is
midnight, 12 pm is noon); taught the avoidance strategy: say noon / midday /
midnight. Not counted: "After preparing all things" -> getting ready;
"We'd returned home by 9" -> we were back home by 9 (past perfect wants a
reference event; closing a narrative, were back / got back reads better).
Verdict: drilled. Owned must come from spontaneous chat use.
