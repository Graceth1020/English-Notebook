# Pattern 04 - no matter + wh- + clause

Date: 2026-09-08 | Pattern: P005 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 12 | Correct: 9/12

## The Gap

Zero uses in 55 exchanges over 11 days. The concession currently comes out one of
two ways, both correct, which is why no error row could ever exist for it:

- a separate sentence with `but` / `though` - Day 08's *The traffic can be brutal
  though*, Day 11's *A lot of people don't think learning counts as switching off
  at the weekend. But for me...*
- or it is dropped and only the conclusion survives - Day 08's *since then I've
  just been driving back*, with the "however bad it gets" left implied.

What it buys is conceding and refusing in one sentence. `but` concedes and then
changes direction; `no matter` concedes and holds position, which is a different
move and the one a standup or a negotiation actually needs.

Requested by the learner, overriding `pattern-next` (which offered `P002` for the
fourth drill running).

## The Rule

```
no matter + wh-word + a full clause  ,  then the main clause

no matter what   he says       ,  I'm driving back
no matter how    bad it gets   ,  I'm driving back
no matter when   he ships it   ,  I'll test it first
```

The wh-word does the conceding, so the clause after it is **ordinary word order** -
no inversion, no `will`. Only `how` reorders anything.

### The `how` slot - the whole drill in one line

**After `no matter how`, something must appear before the subject.** An adjective,
an adverb, or `many` / `much` + noun:

```
no matter how  bad         the traffic gets     ->  adjective
no matter how  fast        you want to ship it  ->  adverb
no matter how  many people book                ->  many + noun
no matter how  much work   wears me out         ->  much + noun
```

**If nothing can fill that slot, `how` is not your word.** Both misses in this
drill were the same one - `how` chosen where the thing being conceded was not a
degree at all:

```
no matter how  people say learning isn't relaxing   ->  content, not degree  ->  what
no matter how  the work wears me out                ->  a verb, not a degree ->  how much / how tired
```

`how` concedes a degree. `what` concedes content. A verb cannot be conceded by
`how` directly - either find the adjective behind it (`how tired work makes me`)
or quantify it (`how much work wears me out`).

### Where `no matter` is not available

```
Whatever you decide is fine.            -> the clause IS the subject. No `no matter`.
No matter what you decide, it's fine.   -> adverbial clause, `it` is the subject.
```

`no matter what` can never *be* the subject of a sentence; it only hangs off a
clause that already has one. Item 2.2 was set as a trap for this and the learner
walked over it by supplying `it` - see What Went Wrong, because the hint given was
worse than the item.

### `will`

Banned **inside** the `no matter` clause, expected in the main clause when the
main clause is a commitment:

```
No matter when he ships it, I'll test it properly first.
                ^ present                ^ will
```

## Round 1 - join two given sentences

**1.** The traffic gets bad. I still drive back home.

**中文：**不管路况多差，我还是开车回家。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how bad the traffic gets, I still drive back home.

**Answer:**
> No matter how bad the traffic gets, I still drive back home.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** `gets` rather than `is` was an improvement on the cue - traffic is a state that develops.

---

**2.** People say learning is not relaxing. It works for me.

**中文：**不管别人说学习算不算放松，对我来说有用。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how people say learning is not relaxing, it works for me.

**Answer:**
> No matter what people say, learning works for me.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** `how` with nothing before the subject. What is being conceded is the content of what they say, not a degree, so the word is `what`. Also `no matter what people say` is complete on its own - the original's `learning is not relaxing` was already carried by it.

**Verdict:** ok

## Round 2 - mixed, choose the right shape

**1.** He ships it on Monday or Wednesday. I test it properly first.

**中文：**不管他周一还是周三上线，我都要先好好测。

**You wrote:**
> No matter when he ships it, I test it properly first.

**Answer:**
> No matter when he ships it, I'll test it properly first.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The tense trap was the clause verb (`ships`, not `will ship`) and it was avoided. The main clause is a commitment about the future, so `I'll` there - the ban on `will` stops at the comma.

---

**2.** You decide. It is fine with me.

**中文：**你决定就好，我都行。

**You wrote:**
> No matter what you decide, it is fine with me.

**Answer:**
> No matter what you decide, it's fine with me.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Supplied `it`, which is exactly what makes `no matter` legal here. The trap only catches someone who writes `No matter what you decide is fine` - see What Went Wrong for the badly-set hint.

## Round 3 - free production

**1.** A teammate keeps pushing you to ship on Tuesday. Concede nothing on testing.

**中文：**同事一直催你周二上线，但测试这件事你不让步。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how fast you want to ship it, I've got to test it properly.

**Answer:**
> No matter how fast you want to ship it, I've got to test it properly.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The best item of the drill. `how fast` is an adverb in the slot, and this is precisely the Day 09 standup exchange where two flat clauses were used instead.

---

**2.** Explain why you go home over National Day even though it is eight hours in the car.

**中文：**解释你为什么国庆还是回家，即使来回八小时。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how long it takes, I'd go home over National Day.

**Answer:**
> No matter how long it takes, I'm going home over National Day.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Structure correct. `I'd` makes it hypothetical; this is a real plan, so `I'm going`. Recurring habit - `I'd` also appeared for a stated intention on Day 09.

---

**3.** Say what you do about your phone when you are working from home.

**中文：**说说在家办公时你怎么处理手机。

**You wrote:**
> Whenever I'm working from home, I shut off my phone.

**Answer:**
> No matter what comes in on my phone, I keep it off till I'm done.

**Verdict:** not the pattern

**Why:** Correct English, and it is `E011` produced right - the Day 02 sentence *Whenever working from home* with the subject now in place. But `whenever` states a condition where the task wanted a concession.

---

**4.** Tell someone why you keep reading at weekends, even when work has worn you out.

**中文：**不管工作多累，你周末还是要读书。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how the work wears me out, I'll read books at weekends.

**Answer:**
> No matter how tired work makes me, I'll still read at weekends.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** Same miss as 1.2. `wears me out` is a verb and `how` cannot concede a verb - either reach for the adjective behind it (`how tired work makes me`) or quantify it (`how much work wears me out`). Also `the work` -> `work` is `E031`, 8th hit, and `read books` -> `read`.

## What Went Wrong

- **The `how` slot, twice, at both ends of the drill** (1.2 and 3.4). Not two
  errors but one: `how` was treated as a general-purpose concession word rather
  than a degree word. The fix is mechanical and now written into the rule - if
  nothing can sit between `how` and the subject, the word is wrong. That this
  survived ten items in between is the finding: it was never corrected mid-drill
  because items 1.3, 1.4, 2.4 and 3.1 all happened to have a legal filler
  available, so the wrong model kept producing right answers.

- **Round 1 primed `how`.** Three of the four items genuinely wanted it, which is a
  flaw in the drill rather than in the learner. 1.2 was the only `what` item in the
  round and it came third. Future drills on a wh- family should spread the words in
  Round 1, not cluster them.

- **A hint that was worse than the item** (2.2). The learner was pointed at "what
  fills the subject slot" as though `no matter` were unavailable, when the real
  boundary is only that the clause cannot itself *be* the subject. He supplied `it`
  and the trap never triggered. Recorded because the hint could have induced an
  error that the learner was not going to make.

- **`I'd` for a real plan** (3.2), second time this has appeared for a stated
  intention rather than a hypothetical.

- **Non-structural, marked and not counted**: `tomorrow` for `somehow` (1.3),
  `can not` (2.4), `the work` (3.4, `E031` 8th hit), `read books` (3.4).

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

| Drill | Round 1 | Later rounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 `with X doing` | all four trailing the phrase | fronted unprompted from 2.3 |
| 02 purpose nouns | `a SMS`, an app read as a topic | both fixed with nothing said |
| 03 `the more X` | all four correct first attempt | one miss, heaviest item |
| 04 `no matter` | one wrong word choice | **the same wrong choice again, ten items later** |

The first three drills all showed self-correction: a wrong first instinct, then a
right second one with nothing said. This drill is the first where a miss
**repeated** rather than resolved - and the reason is visible. In Drills 01-03 the
wrong instinct produced wrong answers, so it got feedback every time. Here the
wrong model (`how` = 不管) produced correct answers on four intervening items
because a filler happened to be available, so nothing contradicted it until an
item came along with a bare verb.

Which sharpens the Drill 02 conclusion. More first instincts help only when a
wrong model fails visibly. When a wrong model is right by coincidence most of the
time, volume alone will not surface it, and the drill has to be built to attack the
boundary directly - 3.4 was the only item in twelve that could catch this one, and
it caught it.

Also worth logging: **first cross-drill transfer** (2.3 reached for `P004`,
drilled two days earlier, unprompted and correct).

## Carry Into Chat

Watch for these and log `pattern-used` on an unprompted `no matter`:

1. Standups and estimates - holding a testing line against a date is the exact
   move this pattern exists for, and 3.1 shows it is already available there.
2. Going home, the commute, tickets - the whole Day 08 cluster is built on
   concessions that were made as separate sentences.
3. Weekend reading and learning, where the concession is currently dropped entirely.
4. The boundary is worth watching too: `no matter how` with a bare verb after it is
   the error to catch, and `whatever you decide is fine` must stay without
   `no matter`.

`drilled`, not `owned`. Two unprompted uses in normal chat earn `pending`, and a
third in a different scene earns `owned`.
