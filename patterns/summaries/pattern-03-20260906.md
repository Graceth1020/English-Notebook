# Pattern 03 - the more X, the more Y

Date: 2026-09-06 | Pattern: P004 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 12 | Correct: 11/12

## The Gap

Zero uses in 55 exchanges over 11 days. Every proportional link came out as two
flat sentences or an `if` / `once` clause - Day 11's *Once I've got more
experience, I think I'll be able to handle it better*, Day 03's *depends on the
commute, but if it's a long one, definitely remote*. Both correct, so this could
never appear as an error row: it is absence, not fault.

What it buys is a single sentence for a relationship that otherwise needs two.
`if` states a condition and stops; this frame says the two things move together,
which is a different claim.

Requested by the learner ("more... more..."), overriding `pattern-next`, which had
offered `P002`.

## The Rule

```
the + comparative + [subject verb]  ,  the + comparative + [subject verb]
the longer the commute (is)         ,  the less I want to go in
```

Both halves front the comparative. **Nothing else moves** - no `do`, no `that`,
no `of`, and the subject follows the comparative directly. `is` can drop when a
half is just noun + be + adjective.

The comparative slot takes any of the shapes a comparative can have:

```
the longer      adverb       ->  the longer I work
the more time   + noun       ->  the more time I spend
the worse       adjective    ->  the worse the traffic
the more fluently  adverb    ->  the more fluently I can speak
```

### The one that is not this pattern

`more and more` is **one variable increasing on its own**, with nothing it is
proportional to:

```
AI is developing faster and faster        -> one variable, steady increase
the more I practise, the better I get     -> two variables, moving together
```

Item 2.1 was planted to see whether the frame would be over-applied to a
single-variable idea. It was not.

## Round 1 - join two given sentences

**1.** The commute is long. I want to go into the office less.

**中文：**通勤越长，我越不想去公司。

**You wrote:**
> The longer the commute is, the less I want to go into the office.

**Answer:**
> The longer the commute is, the less I want to go into the office.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** `is` is optional - *the longer the commute, the less I want to go in* is the tighter spoken version - but keeping it is never wrong.

**2.** I get more experience. I handle complicated tickets better.

**中文：**经验越多，处理复杂 ticket 就越顺手。

**You wrote:**
> The more experience I get, the better I handle complicated tickets.

**Answer:**
> The more experience I get, the better I handle complicated tickets.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The noun slot (`the more experience`) built correctly on the first attempt, which is the harder of the two shapes.

**3.** I read more guides. I unlock more hidden endings.

## Round 2 - mixed, choose the right shape

**1.** AI is developing quickly, and it keeps speeding up. (no second variable)

**中文：**AI 发展越来越快。

**You wrote:**
> AI is developing more and more quickly, and it keeps speeding up.

**Answer:**
> AI is developing faster and faster, and it keeps speeding up.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The trap item, and it was read correctly - `more and more`, not the frame. Not counted against the pattern: `faster and faster` is the spoken form; `more and more quickly` is correct but written.

**2.** I spend more time in the library. I get more done.

**中文：**在图书馆待的时间越长，做完的事越多。

**You wrote:**
> The more time I spend in the library, the more I get done.

**Answer:**
> The more time I spend in the library, the more I get done.

**Verdict:** ok

---

**3.** The traffic is bad. The commute is brutal.

**中文：**路况越差，通勤越难熬。

**You wrote:**
> The worse the traffic is, the more brutal the commute is.

**Answer:**
> The worse the traffic, the more brutal the commute.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Both `is`s droppable here, since each half is noun + be + adjective. Yours is grammatical; the shorter one is what gets said.

---

**4.** I work longer hours. I sleep worse.

**中文：**工作时间越长，睡得越差。

**You wrote:**
> The longer hours I work, the worse I sleep.

**Answer:**
> The longer I work, the worse I sleep.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The frame is right. `the longer hours` puts a noun in a slot that does not need one - drop it (`the longer I work`) or make it countable (`the more hours I work`).

## Round 3 - free production

**1.** A colleague asks why you would rather work remote. Link the commute to how you feel about going in.

**中文：**同事问你为什么想远程，把通勤和你想不想去公司连起来说。

**You wrote:**
> The longer the commute is, the less I want to go to office.

**Answer:**
> The longer the commute is, the less I want to go into the office.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Structure correct. `to office` needs `the` - or drop the noun entirely: `the less I want to go in`.

---

**2.** Explain why you keep reading Clean Code at weekends.

**中文：**解释你为什么周末一直在读《Clean Code》。

**You wrote:**
> The better I want to be at work, the more I have to read.

**Answer:**
> The better I want to get at my job, the more I have to read.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Idiomatic use of the frame with `want`, which was not taught. `at work` means physically present; `at my job` means skilled at it, which is the intended sense - and it lands `up to the job` from Day 11 in a new shape.

---

**3.** Tell a teammate that a change touching core logic takes longer to test.

**中文：**跟同事说，改动碰到的核心逻辑越多，测试就越久。

**You wrote:**
> The more complex of the file we change, the longer it takes to test.

**Answer:**
> The more core logic a change touches, the longer it takes to test.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** `of` cannot appear - after `the + comparative` the subject follows directly. `The more complex the file we change, the longer it takes to test` is the minimal fix; the answer above also reuses P001's own material.

---

**4.** Say what you have noticed about your own English after eleven sessions.

**中文：**说说十一次之后你对自己英语的观察。

**You wrote:**
> The more I practice in speaking English, the more fluently I can speak.

**Answer:**
> The more I practise speaking English, the more fluently I can speak.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Frame correct, including an adverb comparative (`the more fluently`) in the second half. `practise` takes the gerund directly - no `in`.

## What Went Wrong

- **One structural miss in twelve** (3.3): an intrusive `of`. It is the same
  instinct as `the more complex of the two` - a real phrase, where `of` is
  legitimate because a group is being compared. Here nothing is being selected
  from, so the frame takes the subject bare.

- **Three non-structural slips, all outside the drill's remit**: `guilds` for
  `guides` (1.3), `to office` (3.1), `practice in` (3.4). Marked but not counted,
  per the rule that only structure is scored here.

- **A noun in a slot that did not want one** (2.4): `the longer hours I work`.
  Not wrong, but it shows the noun-slot shape being reached for by default when
  the bare adverb is shorter.

- **Round 3 was clean structurally except 3.3**, and 3.2 applied the frame to
  `want`, which no item had modelled.

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

Third drill, and the shape from Drills 01 and 02 did **not** repeat:

| Drill | Round 1 | Later rounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 `with X doing` | all four trailing the phrase | fronted, unprompted, from 2.3 on |
| 02 purpose nouns | `a SMS`, an app read as a topic | both fixed with nothing said |
| 03 `the more X` | **all four correct, first attempt** | one miss, in the hardest item |

The previous two drills produced a wrong first instinct and a corrected second
one. Here the first instinct was right immediately, and the only miss came in the
free-production round on the item with the most content to hold at once. That is a
different failure mode: not habit interference, but load. Which supports the Drill
02 conclusion from the other direction - the frame was available at once, and it
broke only when the sentence around it got heavy.

Worth noting that the trap was refused (2.1). Over-application was the thing being
watched for, and it did not happen.

## Carry Into Chat

Watch for these and log `pattern-used` on an unprompted frame:

1. Anything about the commute or the office - the pairing he has already made
   twice in two different drills.
2. Effort and payoff: reading, practising, experience, getting better at the job.
   *The more I practise, the better I get* is one exchange away at any time.
3. Estimates and scope at work - *the more core logic a change touches, the longer
   it takes to test* is the shape a standup answer wants.
4. The trap side too: 越来越 stays `more and more` / `faster and faster`, and
   forcing the frame onto a single variable would be a new error, not progress.

`drilled`, not `owned`. Two unprompted uses in normal chat earn `pending`, and a
third in a different scene earns `owned`.
