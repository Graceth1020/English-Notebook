# Pattern 01 - with + X + doing/done

Date: 2026-09-02 | Pattern: P001 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 11 | Correct: 10/11

## The Gap

Across 45 exchanges and 8 days this structure never appeared once. The default
was two flat clauses - *The code change costs two days. But the change touches
some core logic* - or a `since` / `because` clause. Both are correct, which is
exactly why eight sessions of chat feedback could never surface it: there was
never an error to correct, only something absent.

What it buys is subordination. English keeps demoting secondary facts into
attached phrases; Chinese habit is to line them up one clause per idea. This is
the most common tool for doing that demotion.

## The Rule

Pull the noun out, make a small sentence, and take **whatever follows `be`**:

```
everyone  ->  everyone IS BOOKING            ->  with everyone booking
tickets   ->  tickets ARE SOLD OUT           ->  with the tickets sold out
provider  ->  provider IS BEING SWAPPED      ->  with the provider being swapped out
parents   ->  parents ARE four hours away    ->  with my parents four hours away
```

No need to name active or passive. `being` appears only when the action is both
passive and still in progress.

The fourth line is the one to notice: this is **not** a rule about adding a
participle. The second slot takes anything that can follow `be` - a participle, an
adjective (`with the window open`), a position (`with the boss away`), a
prepositional phrase (`with a coat on`), or an infinitive (`with so much to do`).

### Two things the rule above does not cover

Both came out of questions asked after the drill, and both are worth more than the
score.

**1. `have` collapses straight into `with`.** Item 1.4 had no `be` in it at all -
`I've got so much I'm trying to learn` - so the test does not run. `with` already
means `have`, so the verb just disappears and nothing is added:

```
I've got a rig              ->  with a rig
I've got so much to learn   ->  with so much to learn
I have two kids             ->  with two kids
```

Which is why `with so much I'm trying to learn` beats `with me trying to learn so
much`. The second is grammatical, but `with` exists to bring in a subject the main
clause does not already have, and `me` / `I` is the same person twice. When the
subject *is* the same, English drops `with` and fronts a bare participle instead:
*Sitting in the library, I can focus* - never *With me sitting in the library...*.

**2. A faster test than active/passive: is there a noun after the participle?**

```
with the change touching the core logic    -> an object follows  -> -ing
with the core logic touched                -> nothing follows    -> -ed
```

A past participle is passive, and passive cannot take an object, because the
object has already been promoted to subject. So a noun after the participle forces
`-ing`, with no thought about voice at all. Counting what comes next is mechanical
and survives speaking speed; working out who acts on what does not.

| Sentence | `with` subject | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| With **the core logic** touched | the logic | `touched` - nothing follows |
| With **the change** touching the core logic | the change | `touching` - object follows |

Same fact, two framings. Item 3.2 used the first and got it right.

## Round 1 - join two given sentences

**1.** Everyone books at the same time. It is impossible to get train tickets.

**中文：**大家都挤在同一时间订票，火车票根本抢不到。

**You wrote:**
> It's impossible to get train tickets with everyone booking at the same time.

**Answer:**
> With everyone booking at the same time, it's impossible to get train tickets.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Structure correct. Position is a separate habit - see What Went Wrong.

---

**2.** My parents are four hours away. A trip back eats the whole weekend.

**中文：**父母家四小时车程，回一趟就把整个周末吃掉了。

**You wrote:**
> A trip back eats the whole weekend with my parents four hours away.

**Answer:**
> With my parents four hours away, a trip back eats the whole weekend.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** No participle needed here - an adjective phrase attaches directly. Handled correctly.

---

**3.** Work and new things take up most of my time. Reading a novel is a luxury these days.

**中文：**工作和学新东西占掉了大部分时间，现在看小说算是奢侈了。

**You wrote:**
> Reading a novel is a luxury these days with work and new things taking up most my time.

**Answer:**
> With work and new things taking up most of my time, reading a novel is a luxury these days.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Structure correct. `most my time` -> `most of my time` is not the pattern and was not counted.

---

**4.** I have got so much I am trying to learn. I never get round to Red Alert.

**中文：**我想学的东西太多，红色警戒一直拖着没玩。

**You wrote:**
> I never get round to Red Alert with I trying to learn so much.

**Answer:**
> With so much I'm trying to learn, I never get round to Red Alert.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** `with` is a preposition, so a pronoun after it takes the object form - `with me trying`, never `with I trying`. Nouns hide this because they do not inflect, which is why only this item exposed it. Smoother still: `with so much on my plate`.

## Round 2 - choose doing / done / being done

**1.** The shorts were sold out. I ended up leaving with just the fruit.

**中文：**短裤卖完了，最后我只买了水果就走了。

**You wrote:**
> I ended up leaving with just the fruit with the shorts sold out.

**Answer:**
> With the shorts sold out, I ended up leaving with just the fruit.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Form correct. But this item forces the front position: the sentence already contains a real preposition `with just the fruit`, so trailing the pattern puts two unrelated `with`s in one sentence and the listener has to work out which is which.

---

**2.** Everyone was working from home. The office felt dead.

**中文：**大家都在家办公，办公室里冷清得很。

**You wrote:**
> The office felt dead with everyone working from home.

**Answer:**
> The office felt dead with everyone working from home.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Active, so `-ing`. Correct.

---

**3.** The SMS provider is being swapped out right now. I have got to test everything.

**中文：**短信服务商正在更换中，所以我得把每个地方都测一遍。

**You wrote:**
> With the SMS provider being swapped out right now, I've got to test everyting.

**Answer:**
> With the SMS provider being swapped out right now, I've got to test everything.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** The hardest item in the drill - passive and still in progress, so `being` is required. Identified unprompted.

---

**4.** The deadline has been moved to Wednesday. Marketing has stopped chasing me.

**中文：**截止时间改到了周三，市场部就不再催我了。

**You wrote:**
> With the deadline moved to Wednesday, marketing has stopped chasing me.

**Answer:**
> With the deadline moved to Wednesday, marketing has stopped chasing me.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Passive but finished, so no `being`. The contrast with item 3 is the whole point of the round, and both were judged correctly.

## Round 3 - situation only, no sentences given

**1.** Someone asks why you drive home for National Day instead of taking the train. Use the pattern, with "everyone squeezed into the same week" as the background.

**中文：**大家都挤在同一周抢票，所以我宁愿开车回去也不坐车。

**You wrote:**
> With everyone buying train tickets at the same week, I prefer driving car back rather than taking train.

**Answer:**
> With everyone buying train tickets in the same week, I'd rather drive back than take the train.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Pattern correct. `at the same week` -> `in the same week` and the missing article in `driving car back` are ordinary chat repairs, not the pattern, and were not counted here.

---

**2.** You are explaining to a colleague why the ticket needs a week, with "the core logic was touched" as the background.

**中文：**因为动到了核心逻辑，我估得得一周左右。

**You wrote:**
> With the core logic touched, I'd put it closer to a week.

**Answer:**
> With the core logic touched, I'd put it closer to a week.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Passive and finished, so no `being` - correct. `I'd put it closer to a week` is `C024`, produced with no prompting.

---

**3.** Describe your state after work once the phone or the devices are off.

**中文：**把东西都关了之后，我下班后能专心看书。

**You wrote:**
> With everything shut off, I can focus on reading books after work.

**Answer:**
> With everything shut off, I can focus on reading after work.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Correct. Chose the participle route over the simpler `with my phone off`, which is the harder of the two.

## What Went Wrong

- **`with I` for `with me`** (1.4) - the only outright error. `with` is a
  preposition, so a following pronoun takes the object form. Nouns never expose
  this because they do not inflect, so it surfaced on the one item with a pronoun.

- **Position, and then self-correction.** All four Round 1 answers put the phrase
  at the *end*, which means the sentence was built first and the structure hung on
  afterwards - the old habit wearing the new pattern. Then 2.3, 2.4 and all of
  Round 3 were fronted, unprompted. That shift matters more than the score:
  fronting requires deciding which fact is secondary *before* speaking, which is
  the actual skill this pattern buys. Trailing is not wrong, and item 2.1 shows
  when it is actively bad - a sentence already containing a literal `with` needs
  the pattern moved to the front.

- **The doing/done decision did not break at all.** 4/4 in Round 2 including the
  `being` case. Worth noting because that was the predicted failure point, and it
  means the rule (pull the noun out, look after `be`) is running.

## Carry Into Chat

Openings where this would be the natural thing to say, to watch for in later
sessions:

1. Anything about the commute or the office - *with everyone heading the same way*,
   *with half the team out*.
2. Estimating work - *with the core logic touched*, *with the tests still to write*.
3. Explaining why an evening or weekend went the way it did - *with the kids
   asleep*, *with my phone off*, *with nothing left in the tank*.

`drilled`, not `owned`. The structure was available while it was the announced
subject of the exercise, which is recognition. It counts as learned only when it
appears unprompted in a normal five-exchange chat, twice - logged with
`pattern-used`.
