# Pattern 06 - fronted participle clause

Date: 2026-09-10 | Pattern: P006 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 14 | Correct: 11/14

## The Gap

Raised by the learner, not by `pattern-next`: he met `Looking at his watch, he saw
that it was one o'clock` in NCE-02 and asked what the grammar was.

It was already half-known. Drill 01's summary had named it - *"never With me
sitting in the library..."* - but only as a footnote explaining why `with me` was
wrong there. The prohibition was owned; the alternative had never been drilled.

## The Rule

The participle has no subject of its own, so it **borrows the subject of the main
clause**. Read the noun straight after the comma and ask whether it is doing the
participle.

```
Looking at his watch,   HE saw it was one o'clock.    he looked, he saw
Sitting in the library, I can actually focus.          I sit, I focus
```

Break it and the sentence says something absurd - *Looking at his watch, the
meeting had already started* means the meeting looked at its watch.

### This pattern and P001 are one operation

```
subject of the extra fact DIFFERENT from the main clause  ->  with + X + doing
subject of the extra fact SAME as the main clause         ->  bare participle
```

Round 2 mixed the two and the boundary held on all four relevant items.

### Three forms

```
-ing          the subject is doing it        Looking at his watch, he saw...
-ed           it is done TO the subject      Written in 1890, the book still...
having + -ed  finished first, then the main  Having finished the ticket, I left.
```

`-ed` is the same Drill 01 test run backwards: `the book IS WRITTEN` -> take what
follows `be`. Several of these carry a fixed preposition that cannot be dropped:

```
I AM FACED WITH a deadline   ->  Faced with a hard deadline, I cut the scope.
```

Without `with`, `faced` reads as an active finite verb and the clause collapses.

### `having` takes the THIRD form

Not the past simple, not the `-ing`:

```
go    went    GONE   ->  Having gone through the logs
shut  shut    SHUT   ->  Having shut my laptop
read  read    READ   ->  Having read for two hours
```

**You wrote:**
> Sitting in the library, I can actually focus.

**Answer:**
> Sitting in the library, I can actually focus.

**Verdict:** ok

**2.** He looked at the logs. He found the API key was wrong.

**中文：**看了日志之后，他发现 API key 是错的。

**You wrote:**
> Looking at the logs, he found the API key was wrong.

**Answer:**
> Looking at the logs, he found the API key was wrong.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** `Having looked` would mark the sequence explicitly, but `-ing` covers "while/by doing" and is the more natural spoken choice.

**3.** I work from home. I get more done than in the office.

**中文：**在家办公时，我比在公司做得更多。

**You wrote:**
> Working from home, I get more done than in the office.

**Answer:**
> Working from home, I get more done than in the office.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Worth contrasting with `E011` (*Whenever working from home*, Day 02), which is the mirror-image error: `whenever` is a conjunction and needs a subject, a fronted participle is a non-finite clause and must not have one.

**4.** She grew up in a traditional Chinese family. She has always been careful about her health.

**Why:** The hardest item of the round - the participle spans a lifetime while the main clause is a present habit, and the borrowed subject still holds.

## Round 3 - free production

**1.** Describe what happens when you drive back to Jieyang and hit the holiday traffic.

**中文：**描述你国庆开车回揭阳遇上堵车的情形。

**You wrote:**
> With so many cars heading in the same direction, the traffic was brutal.

**Answer:**
> With so many cars heading in the same direction, the traffic was brutal.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Best item of the drill. Nothing cued `with` over a participle, and the subjects differ - cars head, traffic is brutal - so a bare participle would have dangled. Reuses his own Day 08 material.

**2.** Explain how you feel after you have read for two hours in the library.

**中文：**说说你在图书馆读了两小时书之后的感受。

**You wrote:**
> Having read in the library for two hours, I felt fullfilled.

**Answer:**
> Having read in the library for two hours, I felt fulfilled.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Not counted: `fullfilled` -> `fulfilled`. Correct only by accident on the form question - see What Went Wrong.

---

**3.** Tell a teammate what you noticed when you went through the logs on Saturday.

**中文：**跟同事说你周六查日志时发现了什么。

**You wrote:**
> Having went through the logs on Saturday, I found our service wasn't stable.

**Answer:**
> Having gone through the logs on Saturday, I found our service wasn't stable.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** `having` takes the third form. `went` is the past simple.

---

**4.** Say what you do first thing after you shut your laptop on a Friday.

**中文：**说说周五合上电脑之后你第一件事做什么。

**You wrote:**
> Having shutting off the computer, I went to the gym on Friday.

**Answer:**
> Having shut my laptop on Friday, I went straight to the gym.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** `shutting` is the `-ing`, primed by Round 1. `shut` is already the third form.

## What Went Wrong

- **`having` + wrong verb form, twice** (3.3, 3.4) - the finding of this drill, and
  the same shape as Drill 04's `how` miss. `Having finished` (2.1) and `Having read`
  (3.2) were both correct, so the model looked sound: `finished` and `read` have
  third forms identical to their past simple, so a wrong model produces right
  answers. The instant an irregular verb appeared it broke - and it broke in two
  different directions, `went` (past simple) and `shutting` (the `-ing` the drill
  had just spent a round reinforcing).

  This is the second time a wrong model has survived because the easy items could
  not distinguish it. Worth building into future drills: **an irregular verb in
  Round 2, not Round 3.**

- **A dropped fixed preposition** (2.5) - `faced` without `with`. The `-ed`
  branch's one real trap, and it was met on the first `-ed` item that had one.

- **Round 1 was all `-ing`, and the learner said so.** Four of four items wanted the
  same form, which is exactly the flaw logged in Drill 04 (three of four Round 1
  items wanting `how`) and reproduced here two drills later. He asked why there
  were no `-ed` examples before Round 2 reached them; two extra `-ed` items were
  added mid-drill in response. The rule for future drills is now explicit: **Round
  1 must spread the forms, not isolate one.**

- **Nothing dangled.** The one failure mode the pattern is named for did not appear
  in fourteen items, including four free-production ones.

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

| Drill | Wrong model | Why it survived |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 04 `no matter` | `how` = 不管 | four items happened to have a legal filler |
| 06 participles | `having` + past simple | `finished` / `read` have identical third forms |

Twice now the drill has failed to catch a wrong model until an item happened to
discriminate. Volume is not the fix - **one well-chosen item is worth four
redundant ones**, and the discriminating item belongs early, while there is still
drill left to retest it.

Against that, the P001 boundary was clean on all four items including an
unprompted one in Round 3, which is the strongest single result in six drills.

## Carry Into Chat

1. Sequencing at work - *Having gone through the logs, I found...* is the natural
   shape for the Day 09 incident report, which was told as flat clauses.
2. Reading and the library - *Sitting in the library, I can actually focus* is one
   exchange away on any weekend topic.
3. `Faced with...` and `Given...` are the two `-ed` openers that genuinely occur in
   speech; worth watching for either.
4. The boundary is the thing to check: if the participle's subject is not the main
   clause's subject, the sentence wants `with + X`, and getting that wrong is a
   dangling participle rather than a stylistic slip.

`drilled`, not `owned`.
