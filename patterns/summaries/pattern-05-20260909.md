# Pattern 05 - no matter + wh- + clause (Round 2, harder)

Date: 2026-09-09 | Pattern: P005 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 12 | Correct: 8/11

## The Gap

Drill 04 established that `no matter` was available as a structure but `how` was
over-applied as a general-purpose concession word. This drill was requested by the
learner to extend the pattern across all wh-words and add end-position
alternatives: `..., anyway` (C069), `..., though` (C052), and `either way`.

## The Rule

### Front-position: no matter + wh- + clause

```
no matter what   everyone says                 -> content
no matter where  I am                          -> place
no matter who    the PM assigns it to          -> person
no matter what   happened to the queue         -> event (NOT "what the queue happened")
```

The wh-word introduces its own clause. The subject of that clause follows the
wh-word (or follows the adjective after `how`).

### End-position: concession after the fact

```
The traffic is brutal. I drive back anyway.       -> action despite obstacle
The old provider was reliable. We swapped it out, though.  -> just the concession
Either way, I need testing time.                  -> the choice is irrelevant
```

### Concession markers do not stack

`but`, `still`, `anyway`, `though`, and `no matter` are all concession markers, but
they are not interchangeable and two of them pair up idiomatically. Three does not.
The Chinese habit behind the over-use is 虽然...但是...还是... anyway, which translates
each character into its own marker:

```
The traffic is brutal, but I drive back anyway.  -> `but` + `anyway`, idiomatic pair
No matter how tired I am, I still read.          -> `no matter` + `still`, idiomatic pair
The traffic is brutal, but I still drive back anyway.  -> three, redundant
No matter how tired I am, I still read anyway.   -> `no matter` + `anyway`, one is dead weight
```

**Two is normal when the markers do different jobs; the failure is the third, or
pairing two full concessions.**

- `but ... anyway` - `but` marks the turn, `anyway` marks the persistence. Canonical.
- `no matter ... still` - `no matter` concedes, `still` marks the persistence. Canonical.
- `no matter ... anyway` - both are complete concessions. Drop one.
- `but ... still ... anyway` - three markers for one idea.

## Round 1 - join two sentences, pick the right wh-word

**1.** Everyone says coding is not relaxing. It is how I switch off.

**中文：**不管别人怎么说，编程就是我的放松方式。

**You wrote:**
> No matter what everyone says, this is how I switch off.
> Everyone says coding is not relaxing, it is how I switch off anyway.

**Answer:**
> No matter what everyone says, coding is how I switch off.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Two versions, both structurally correct on the pattern. `this` for `coding` is slightly off because the referent sits in the conceded clause. The comma splice in the second version is a separate issue - see What Went Wrong.

**2.** The PM assigns the ticket to me or to someone else. I need to understand the context.

**中文：**不管 PM 把 ticket 派给谁，我都需要理解上下文。

**You wrote:**
> Either way, I need to understand the context.

**Answer:**
> No matter who the PM assigns the ticket to, I need to understand the context.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** `Either way` is a valid alternative and the cue was a binary choice. Not the `no matter` form but the task was to pick the right wh-word, and `either way` is arguably the better choice here.

**Answer:**

**Verdict:** ok

**4.** The queue was blocked or the provider was down. The monitor caught the error.

**中文：**不管是队列堵了还是供应商挂了，监控都抓住了错误。

**You wrote:**
> No matter what the queue happened, the monitor caught the error.
> The queue was blocked or the provider was down. The monitor caught the error anyway.

**Answer:**
> No matter what happened to the queue, the monitor caught the error.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** First version wrong - `what the queue happened` is not a clause; `what happened to the queue` is. The wh-word introduces its own clause, and `the queue` is not the subject of that clause. Second version correct.

## Round 2 - mixed, position and structure

**1.** The traffic is brutal. I still drive back. (with end-position `..., anyway`)

**中文：**路况很差，但我还是会开回去。

**You wrote:**
> The traffic is brutal, but I still drive back anyway.

**Answer:**
> The traffic is brutal. I drive back anyway.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Three markers (`but` + `still` + `anyway`) for one concession. Structurally English allows it but it feels crowded. The rule is embedded in the summary now.

---

**2.** I am tired after work. I still read at weekends. (BOTH ways: front and end)

**中文：**下班后很累，但还是周末读书。用前置和后置两种说法。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how tired I am after work, I still read at weekends anyway.

**Answer:**
> No matter how tired I am after work, I still read at weekends.
> I'm tired after work. I read at weekends anyway.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** The two versions were merged into one. `no matter` already concedes, so `anyway` is redundant. The `still` is fine with `no matter`.

**3.** The old SMS provider was reliable. We swapped it out. (with end-position `..., though`)

**中文：**旧的短信供应商很可靠，但我们还是换掉了。

**You wrote:**
> The old SMS provider was reliable, we still swapped it out though.

**Answer:**
> The old SMS provider was reliable. We swapped it out, though.

**Verdict:** wrong form

**Why:** Comma splice between two independent clauses. `though` belongs after a comma at the end, not after the verb.

## Round 3 - free production

**1.** Someone asks how you can read on weekends when you are already worn out from work.

**中文：**别人问你工作那么累怎么还周末看书。

**You wrote:**
> No matter how tired I am on weekends, I still read books.

**Answer:**
> No matter how tired work makes me, I still read on weekends.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** Structure correct. The fatigue shifted from "from work" to "on weekends," which changes the meaning. Not counted: `read books` -> `read`.

---

**2.** A PM asks whether you can handle the ticket alone.

**中文：**PM 问你一个人能不能搞定这个 ticket。

**You wrote:**
> I'll figure the ticket out anyway.

**Answer:**
> It's a big one, but I'll figure it out anyway.

**Verdict:** not the pattern

**Why:** `anyway` needs an obstacle to push against, and none was stated - alone it sounds dismissive. The learner supplied the fix himself after the drill (`It's a big one, but I'll figure it out anyway`), which is the correct version. The `either way` first given here was wrong: it needs a *choice*, and the cue had an obstacle.

---

**3.** You are deciding between going home over National Day and staying in Foshan.

**中文：**你在纠结国庆回不回家。两边都有代价。

**You wrote:**
> Either going home over National Day or staying in Foshan has a cost.

**Answer:**
> Either way, going home or staying has a cost.

**Verdict:** not the pattern

**Why:** `Either X or Y has a cost` is a different grammar (X or Y as subject). The task asked for `either way` as an adverbial at the front.

---

**4.** A teammate asks why you keep buying physical books when you can read digital.

**中文：**同事问你为什么还买纸质书。用 `..., anyway` 或 `..., though` 收尾。

**You wrote:**
> Digital books are convenient to read. I prefer reading physical books, though.
> No matter how convenient is to read Digital books, I prefer reading physical books, anyway.

**Answer:**
> Digital books are convenient to read. I prefer physical books, though.

**Verdict:** ok

**Why:** First version correct - `C052` produced for the first time. Second version has three issues: missing subject `it` after `how convenient` (`how convenient it is to read`), `anyway` redundant after `no matter`, and `Digital` capitalised.

## What Went Wrong

- **Marker stacking** (2.1, 2.2, 3.4 second version) - the dominant finding of this
  drill, though the first framing of it given during the drill was too blunt and the
  learner caught it. Saying "English uses one marker" is wrong: `but ... anyway` and
  `no matter ... still` are both canonical pairs, because in each the two words do
  different jobs - one marks the turn or the concession, the other marks the
  persistence. What actually breaks is a *third* marker (2.1's `but` + `still` +
  `anyway`) or pairing two *full* concessions (2.2 and 3.4's `no matter` + `anyway`,
  where either alone says everything). The Chinese habit behind the over-use is
  虽然...但是...还是... anyway, which puts a marker at every joint.

- **Comma splice** (1.1 second version, 2.3) - two independent clauses joined by a
  comma. This is a separate issue from the pattern and not structural, but it
  appeared twice in this drill and is worth logging.

- **`what the queue happened`** (1.4 first version) - wrong clause structure. The
  wh-word introduces its own clause, and `the queue` is not the subject of that
  clause. `what happened to the queue` is the correct form.

- **`how convenient is to read`** (3.4 second version) - missing subject `it`
  inside the `how` clause. The `how` slot was learned correctly (adjective in
  front of the subject), but the subject itself was dropped.

- **Two skipped/not-pattern items** (3.2, 3.3) - both are cases where the learner
  reached for a different structure that cannot carry the concession. 3.2's
  `anyway` had no obstacle to push against; 3.3's `either X or Y` was a subject
  noun phrase rather than a concessive adverbial.

- **`no matter` + `anyway` survived the drill** - the same miss pattern from
  Drill 04. A wrong model (`no matter` = concession, `anyway` = also concession,
  so together = more concessive) kept producing correct answers on items where
  only one appeared, so nothing contradicted it until the stacking items.

## Carry Into Chat

1. The `no matter` + `anyway` stacking is the thing to watch for - if either
   appears, the other is unnecessary.
2. `..., though` is now `C052` produced for the first time. One more unprompted
   use in a different context earns `pending`.
3. Comma splices are worth a separate note if they recur in chat.
4. The `either way` frame is available; watch for it in decision contexts.
