# Pattern 07 - none of + noun (total negation)

Date: 2026-09-12 | Pattern: P007 | Rounds: 3 | Items: 17 | Correct: 16/17

## The Gap

Born from a live error, not from absence. Day 14, exchange 3:

  "After the ticket shipped, all users couldn't pay their orders anymore."

Intended: 所有用户都没法支付 - zero users could pay. English reads `all ... not`
as partial negation: "not all users could pay". The sentence said the opposite of
what happened - in an incident report, the worst possible failure mode.

## The Rule

```
TOTAL      None of the users could pay.        (zero could)
PARTIAL    Not all users could pay.            (some could, some couldn't)
TWO        Neither provider worked.            (exactly two, both failed)
UNCOUNT    None of the work is done.           (singular verb)
```

The forbidden shape is `all ... not-verb`, where `not` scopes over `all` and flips
the meaning. The escape hatch: `all` + a verb that carries its own negation is
fine - `All the tests failed` means every test failed.

**After `neither` / `none of`, the verb stays affirmative.** The negation lives in
the subject, not the verb:

```
Neither coffee shop was open.    ->  neither + affirmative verb
Neither coffee shop closed.      ->  means BOTH were open - the meaning flips
```

**`no + noun` is a legitimate sibling**, not a shortcut:

```
None of the alerts got out.      -> the group is named, then negated
No alert got out.                -> the noun carries the negation itself
```

Both mean zero. `no + noun` is tighter; `none of the...` is needed when the group
must be explicit (*none of the alerts we set up*, *none of them*).

Pronouns after `of` are object pronouns: `none of us`, `neither of them` - the
P002 boundary, held on every item.

### Verb agreement after `none of` / `neither of`

Asked by the learner the day after the drill (about 3.5's `None of the cars was
moving` - correct as written).

```
None of the cars was moving.     -> traditional / formal
None of the cars were moving.    -> the more common spoken choice
```

Both are correct. `none` is historically *not one*, which is where the singular
rule comes from, but when a plural noun sits right before the verb, most native
speakers follow the noun. Only an **uncountable** noun fixes the verb, because
the noun itself has no plural:

```
None of the traffic was moving.      -> only "was"
None of the work is done.            -> only "is"
None of the information is current.  -> only "is"
```

| After `none of` / `neither of` | Verb |
| --- | --- |
| plural noun (`the cars`, `the users`, `them`) | `was` or `were` - both fine |
| uncountable noun (`the work`, `the traffic`) | singular only |

The same logic covers `neither of` + plural: `Neither of us has replied` is the
traditional choice, `have replied` is common in speech. Singular is the safer
default in writing.

**When `neither` takes `of`** - look at the first word after it (added 2026-09-12,
from a side question during this drill):

```
neither + bare noun              ->  Neither provider worked.        (no of)
neither of + the/these/my/us...  ->  Neither of the providers worked.
                                     Neither of us went.
```

`neither` is itself a determiner, so it modifies a bare noun directly. Anything
that already carries a determiner or is a pronoun needs `of` to attach. Pronouns
have no bare form at all - `Neither us went` is impossible, only
`Neither of us went`. Same distinction applies to `none`: `none of` is the only
form, because `none` never modifies a noun directly.
**And the noun after bare `neither` is always singular** (added 2026-09-20, from a
side question during Drill 11 - triggered by the Drill 11 retest item
`Neither coffee shops were opened`):

```
Neither coffee shop was open.       ->  bare neither + SINGULAR noun
Neither coffee shops was open.      ->  wrong
Neither of the shops was/were open. ->  of + plural, verb either way
```

Mnemonic: `neither` = **neither one**. The noun that follows it directly is "that
one", so it is singular, and the verb follows suit. The plural only ever appears
after `of` (where it must also carry a determiner: `the shops`, `these shops`,
`my teammates` - never `neither of coffee shops`).

## What Went Wrong

- **One miss in seventeen, and it is the mirror image of the original error.**
  3.4's `Neither coffee shop closed` is the negative-polarity version of Day 14's
  `all users couldn't pay`: in both, the negation is attached to the wrong element
  and the sentence means its opposite. Day 14 put `not` where it swallowed `all`;
  3.4 put `closed` where it cancelled `neither`. The scope confusion is one thing,
  not two, and it has now surfaced at both ends of the negation family. The rule
  that covers both: **the negation lives in the subject; the verb stays
  affirmative** - and `all` is never that subject.

- **The uncountable decision was tested three times and hit twice** (2.3 and 3.9
  clean; 3.5 sidestepped by converting `traffic` to `cars`). The sidestep is worth
  noting because it is a recognition of difficulty, not a failure - the learner
  reached for a noun he could control rather than the one the cue specified.

- **Round 1 and Round 2 were clean across all eight items**, including both
  total-vs-partial discrimination items. This is the first drill where the
  decision the pattern turns on was never missed in a structured round.

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

The one miss arrived where misses always arrive in this track: Round 3, the item
with the most content to hold at once (3.4 had the coffee shop, the closure, and
the consequence). Drills 03, 04 and 06 all showed the same thing - the structure
is available when it is the announced subject, and it breaks under load.

But this drill is also the cleanest evidence yet that the drill-design fixes are
working: Round 1 spread four forms instead of clustering one (no priming effect),
and the discriminating item sat at 1.2 rather than Round 3 (partial negation was
chosen correctly every time it came up - four separate decisions, zero misses).

## Carry Into Chat

1. Incident and status talk - `none of the alerts got out`, `none of the tests
   passed`, `not all the docs are up to date` are all one exchange away on any
   work topic, and Day 14's incident story alone contains three openings.
2. The `neither` + affirmative verb rule - if `neither` appears with a negative
   verb in chat, log it; it means 3.4 was not a one-off.
3. `no + noun` vs `none of the + noun` is a register choice, not an error -
   either production counts as the pattern used.
4. Watch for the original error recurring: any `all ... not` in chat should be
   logged as a recurrence of the Day 14 shape, with `none of` as the fix.

`drilled`, not `owned`. Two unprompted uses in normal chat earn `pending`, and a
