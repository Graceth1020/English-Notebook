## The Rule

on = surface/line, in = container, of = part-whole. Platforms are SURFACES:
on Rednote / on GitHub / on the platform - never "in". The off-pair of on is
off: I got this off Rednote. Which pair fires follows the VERB: see/read
something on a platform; get something off it.

by + bare noun = transport/means only (by bus, by hand). A tool takes `with`
(speed it up with the cache); a method takes `by -ing` (by caching).

Fixed collocations ignore the metaphor and are learned one by one: rely on,
depend on, lie in, thanks to, down to, based on, scheduled for.

**5.** 会议定在十月二十号周一早上六点半。(P013 stack tie-in)

**中文：**会议定在十月二十号周一早上六点半。

**You wrote:**
> The meeting is scheduled for 6:30 am Monday on October 20th.

**Answer:**
> The meeting is scheduled for 6:30 am on Monday, October 20th.

**Verdict:** wrong

**Why:** Every individual preposition is right (`scheduled for`, `on` + date), but the stack broke: `Monday` hangs bare and `on` landed on the wrong unit. `on` governs the day+date as one unit, small-to-large, no extra prepositions between. This is P013 territory, not P014 - the one miss of the drill, and it belongs to the other pattern.

## What It Showed

All three flagged misses from Drill 15 cleared: platform-as-surface repaired at
the first ask (1.1) and held through Round 3, `rely on` reached unprompted
(2.2), and the `with`-for-tools rule applied without prompting (1.6). The one
miss (2.5) was not a P014 error at all - every preposition choice was correct
and the P013 time-stack broke instead (`on` landing on the date instead of
governing day+date). That is a useful negative result: the preposition layer
is now stronger than the stacking layer above it, which is exactly what P013
(`drilled`, 0/2 used) is for. Two non-pattern slips surfaced for the log,
neither new: `get discount` (E031 family) and `new added` (adverb before
participle).
