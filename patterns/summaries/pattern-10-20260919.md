## The Rule

Three types, three mechanisms:

```
1. who/which + be + X        ->  drop who/which + be      the engineer responsible
2. who/which + lexical verb  ->  verb becomes participle  the change touching core logic
3. which/that + S + V        ->  pronoun vanishes          the API we replaced
```

**The type-2 boundary** (the new content of this drill): the participle must be
one English actually uses as an adjective. `touching`, `working`, `heading` are
fine. `knowing`, `having`, `being` are stative and resist the reduction. Test:
can you put the participle before the noun? `the touching change` is odd but
parses; `the knowing engineer` does not mean `the engineer who knows`.

And the constraint that covers everything: **after the reduction, no finite verb
may be left standing.** Modals (`can run`) and finite lexical verbs (`works`)
cannot reduce at all.

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

Drill 08s one miss was the object-relative drop - a type that had never been
taught, so the miss was a drill-design finding, not a learner one. The type then
appeared unprompted in chat the next day (Day 18s `a project you arent familiar
with`), and in this drill it was produced five times across five opportunities
without one error. The sequence is the strongest evidence yet for the tracks
founding bet: **show the structure once, name the test, and recognition converts
to production fast** - the bottleneck was never learning, it was exposure.

The other finding is the boundary work. Day 18 produced the over-application
(`knowing much`) and this drill refused it twice, once flagged and once not. The
refusal in free production (3.2) is the one that counts: the boundary held with
no `careful` in the prompt.
