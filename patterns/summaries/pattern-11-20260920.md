## The Rule

```
TOTAL      None of the users could pay.     No user could pay.
PARTIAL    Not all users could pay.
TWO        Both providers were expensive.    Neither provider worked.
UNCOUNT    None of the work is done.         None of the traffic was moving.
GROUP      Everyone on the team is responsible.   (never "all the team")
```

Two hard rules:

1. **The negation lives in the subject; the predicate stays affirmative** - and
   "affirmative" covers adjectives too. `unreliable` is a negative in disguise:
   *Neither provider was unreliable* forces the reader to cancel two negatives,
   the same shape that flipped *Neither coffee shop closed* in Drill 07. If the
   predicate carries its own negation, reach for `both` or a plain affirmative
   verb.
2. Exactly two is `both` / `neither`, never `all two` / `none of the two`.

## What Went Wrong

- **The Drill 07 miss shape returned in disguise, twice** (1.3 partially, 2.3
  fully). 1.3 kept the verb affirmative but broke the noun (`neither coffee
  shops`); 2.3 moved the negation from the verb into the adjective (`unreliable`)
  and stacked it against `neither`. The rule as stated - "the verb stays
  affirmative" - was too narrow; the real rule is the predicate stays affirmative,
  adjectives included. The rule section above now says so.

- **The GROUP row (`all the team` shape) is the new live gap** - three occurrences
  in three days: Day 20 (`all the team have got to be responsible`), Round 2 item
  1 (repaired), Round 3 item 4 (`all people heading`, produced fresh). One repair
  in a structured round, one fresh production of the error. This is the thing to
  watch in chat.

- **The `both` branch is solid** - items 1.4, 2.2, 3.2 all correct, including the
  free-production one.

- **`up to the date`** (3.3) - the Day 14 `kept to date` shape recurring with the
  article on the wrong side. Logged as `chat:E064`.

## The Pattern Behind The Pattern

Eighth drill scored, and the shape from Drill 05 repeats: a wrong model survives
on items that cannot distinguish it, then breaks on the one that can. In Drill 07
the miss was `neither` + negative verb; this drill kept the verb affirmative on
every item - and then moved the negation into the adjective instead, because
"verb stays affirmative" had been learned as a verb rule rather than a negation
rule. The boundary moved; the model followed it and broke one step further out.

Also worth noting: 2.3's error was preceded by a meta question asking for the
antonym of `reliable`. The antonym was supplied, and it was used to build the
double negative. That is not an argument against answering meta questions - but
it is a reminder that a new word is safest used in an affirmative frame.
