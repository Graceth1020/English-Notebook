## How to review this

Reading this file is recognition, and recognition is the problem, not the
cure. The only action that moves a chunk into recall:

1. Pick 3 unchecked chunks. Three minutes, not thirty - spacing beats volume.
2. For each one, say a sentence **out loud** about your own real life,
   not the example. The example is already understood; your own sentence is not.
3. Next time, put the same chunk in a different situation. Repeating
   yesterday's sentence is memorisation; changing the context is recall.

Do not read this file before a chat session - the seeding is meant to be
silent, and previewing it invalidates the only honest measurement here.

## Day 01

- [ ] **couldn't put it down** - a book too good to stop reading
  - I started it Friday night and couldn't put it down.
- [ ] **be really into (something)** - be passionate about it
  - I was really into suspense novels.
- [ ] **flush out your system** - the health idea of clearing the body
  - A big glass of water flushes out your system.
- [ ] **get through (a book/task)** - finish it at pace
  - I got through it in three nights.
- [ ] **a luxury** - something you can rarely afford the time for
  - Reading for two hours straight is a luxury now.
- [ ] **it comes down to (X)** - name the real cause, casually
  - Honestly, it just comes down to time.

## Day 02

- [ ] **stay focused** - keep concentrating (fixed phrase)
  - I turn off my console to stay focused.
- [ ] **I get more say over (X)** - claim control in natural speech
  - I get more say over my own hours.
- [ ] **work from home** - remote work - never 'at home'
  - I worked from home all through COVID.
- [ ] **switch off** - stop thinking about work
  - The hard part is switching off at the end of the day.
- [ ] **my commute** - the trip to work, already includes its length
  - My commute is 40 minutes door to door.
- [ ] **right away** - the neutral spoken 'immediately' - default over 'at once'
  - If you need me, I'll respond right away.

## Day 03

- [ ] **never get round to it** - keep meaning to do something and never do
  - I've always meant to play it, I just never get round to it.
- [ ] **I've always meant to (do X)** - a standing intention never acted on
  - I've always meant to pick that game back up.
- [ ] **get something off (a platform)** - find/take content from an app or site
  - I got this recipe off Rednote.
- [ ] **all sorts** - spoken replacement for 'various' / 'all kinds'
  - People post all sorts on there.
- [ ] **sold out** - no stock left - always with 'be', always plural for shorts etc.
  - I went back for them but they were sold out.

## Day 04

- [ ] **get in the zone** - hit deep focus while working
  - Once I get in the zone I lose three hours.
- [ ] **talk it through** - work something out by discussing it
  - Even a team of experts has to talk it through first.
- [ ] **that's what pays** - name the thing that earns the money
  - The business side is what pays.
- [ ] **buy that** - believe a claim - spoken 'be convinced by'
  - People say coding is dead, but I don't buy that.
- [ ] **heads-down time** - uninterrupted solo work
  - I'd protect my heads-down time over meetings.
- [ ] **the business side / the technical side** - an ASPECT needs an explicit noun (side/part/end); Chinese leaves it implicit
  - The business side, I'd say - not the coding side.

## Day 05

- [ ] **I'd put it closer to (a week)** - counter an estimate with your own number
  - I'd put it closer to a week.
- [ ] **that's after the fact** - it only tells you once the damage is done
  - Monitoring helps, but that's after the fact.
- [ ] **talk something through** - discuss it properly, to a conclusion
  - We talked it through last week.
- [ ] **all in** - totalling everything up
  - So all in, I'd put it at a week.
- [ ] **let's aim to (ship it Wednesday)** - propose a date without ordering anyone
  - Let's aim to ship it next Wednesday.
- [ ] **for what it's worth** - soften an opinion you know may not land
  - For what it's worth, I still think it's overkill.
- [ ] **still hasn't (done X) by then** - the negative-already: English uses still + perfect, never already + didn't
  - By then the customer still hasn't got their code.

## Day 06

- [ ] **it makes no difference to** - right preposition: difference takes to, not in; also means 'doesn't affect'
  - Twenty seconds makes no difference to my evening.
- [ ] **company culture** - fixed compound - never 'the company's culture'
  - That comes down to company culture.
- [ ] **work-life balance** - fixed compound, that order, hyphenated
  - Switching off is a big part of work-life balance.
- [ ] **sit on something** - hold it without acting on it
  - He's been sitting on my PR for three days.
- [ ] **read something into (it)** - infer a meaning that may not be there
  - Don't read too much into it.
- [ ] **how big a deal it is** - gauge importance - the spoken alternative to 'how difficult/serious'
  - It depends on how big a deal it is.
- [ ] **that's down to (X)** - attribute a cause or responsibility, casually
  - That's down to how my leader plans it.
- [ ] **be up to the job** - have the ability the role needs - usually used in the negative
  - Am I just not up to the job?
- [ ] **out of my depth** - beyond what you can handle
  - I was completely out of my depth in that meeting.

## Day 07

- [ ] **That's on me** - take responsibility, without the weight of 'duty'
  - I'm the one who let it slide - that's on me.
- [ ] **wired** - too stimulated to sleep
  - I'm still wired an hour after playing.
- [ ] **wear me out** - drain your energy - the spoken 'exhausting'
  - Playing games wears me out.
- [ ] **the thing is, ...** - spoken frame for raising the real issue - replaces 'the question is'
  - The thing is, I can't sleep properly afterwards.
- [ ] **unwind** - wind down after effort - stronger than relax
  - An hour of gaming helps me unwind.
- [ ] **get in the way of (X)** - the spoken 'affect' when something interferes
  - It never gets in the way of my sleep.
- [ ] **a weeknight thing** - confine something to weeknights in three words
  - That's only really a weeknight thing.
- [ ] **a rig / my setup** - what people call a gaming PC - never 'devices'
  - I've got a rig that can run anything.
- [ ] **know it inside out** - know something thoroughly from long use
  - I know the game inside out by now.

## Day 08

- [ ] **over (a holiday)** - across the whole of a holiday - 'during' needs 'the' with 'holiday'
  - I go back over National Day.
- [ ] **the traffic is brutal** - spoken replacement for 'traffic is a serious problem'
  - The traffic was brutal the whole way.
- [ ] **heading in the same direction** - direction takes 'in'; 'heading' beats 'going'
  - Everyone was heading in the same direction.
- [ ] **..., though (sentence-final)** - the spoken 'However' - goes on the end, never the front
  - The traffic can be brutal, though.
- [ ] **my parents' place** - where your parents live - not 'where my parents live'
  - I'd work from my parents' place.
- [ ] **I only get back twice a year** - frequency in the verb, not in 'chances'
  - I only get back twice a year.
- [ ] **that doesn't hold up** - the reason sounds fine but collapses under one question - 站不住脚
  - He said it was a budget issue, but that doesn't really hold up.
- [ ] **that's a symptom, not the reason** - distinguishes what you can see from what is causing it
  - The retries are a symptom, not the reason.
- [ ] **that's downstream of (X)** - caused by something further up - the root-cause phrase natives use at work
  - All three of those are downstream of the same constraint.
