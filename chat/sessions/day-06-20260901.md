**Codex:** Something I keep seeing argued about. Your manager messages you at 9pm - not an emergency, just a question that could have waited until morning. Some people answer immediately, some leave it till the next day on principle. What do you actually do?

**You:** I will answer it until morning. Switching off after work is a key part of balancing life and work. It's just a question which means whether I reply immediately or not make no sense.
[learner added in Chinese: the last sentence was meant to be "更别说这只是一个问题，我是否立刻回都不影响进度"]

/ [awkward] "That might be the company's culture" -> "That comes down to company culture" - company culture is a fixed compound, no possessive.

REVISION of exchange 5, offered by the learner after reading the summary.

**You (redo):** That's on me to manage. If I have to work in the evening, I'll figure out the reason. Does it come down to my low efficiency, or my unreasonable working schedule? Or even worse, could my ability match my position?

/ [wrong] "could my ability match my position?" -> "am I just not up to the job?" - he means his ability does NOT match; as written it asks whether it does. Negation dropped, meaning inverted.
/ [bookish] "my low efficiency, or my unreasonable working schedule" -> "I'm not working efficiently, or my schedule's unrealistic" - two nominalisations where English uses a verb and an adjective.
/ [awkward] "That's on me to manage." -> "That's on me." - the phrase is complete on its own; adding to manage makes that both subject and implied object.
