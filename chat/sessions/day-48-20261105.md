[meta] **Q:** C089 it really matters to a manager to manage project progress. Is this sentence right?

[meta] **A:** No - that stacks two structures. `matter to` is "X matters to someone", where X is the thing that matters and to only introduces who it matters to: *Being familiar with computer science really matters to us.* The `it ... to do` extraposition can't ride on top, because the to in `matter to` already holds the "who" slot, so a second to-infinitive reads as "important when doing it to a manager". Two legal forms: *Managing project progress really matters to a manager.* or *It matters to a manager that the project keeps moving.*

[meta] **Q:** What's the meaning of "turned down twice,"?

[meta] **A:** turn down means reject - a request, a proposal, an application, an offer. Not "lower". So "turned down twice" = we've said no to it twice: *We turned down their request twice.* It only means "lower the volume" when the object is music or heat, and as a separable phrasal verb the pronoun must sit in the middle: *turned it down*, never *turned down it*.
