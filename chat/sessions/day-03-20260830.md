**Codex:** Happy to be the plan - though I'll warn you, phrases stick better when they come out of something you actually did. So: anything at all this weekend, even if it was just lying around?

**You:** I went to shop yesterday afternoon. I bought some kinds of fruits like avocados and grapes. I also went to buy a new basketball short which I've bought before, but unfortunately it's been sold out.

↪ [wrong] "I went to shop" → "I went shopping" / "I went to the shops" — bare "shop" needs either the -ing form or an article.
↪ [awkward] "some kinds of fruits" → "some fruit" — "fruit" is uncountable here; "kinds of" adds nothing.
↪ [wrong] "a new basketball short which I've bought before, but ... it's been sold out" → "the same basketball shorts I'd bought before, but they were sold out" — shorts is always plural; and the tense should sit in the past with the story.
Native: wrong

> 3 sentences → 3 fixes.
> Skipped, carry to summary: "unfortunately" is a written connector; speech uses
> "but they were sold out" with no signpost, or "sadly" at most.

**Codex:** Annoying when that happens. Avocados though, are you actually into them or is that a health thing?

**You:** Right. Avocados have much cellulose which helps flush out our system. Also, that's delicious when you mix them with yogurt.

↪ [wrong] "have much cellulose" → "have a lot of fibre" — "much" in the positive needs "a lot of"; and "cellulose" is the lab word - food is "fibre".
↪ [wrong] "that's delicious when you mix them" → "they're delicious mixed with yogurt" — "that" does not agree with the plural avocados.
Native: wrong

**Codex:** Avocado with yogurt is a new one on me - I'd have guessed that goes savoury. Is that a normal combination where you are?

**You:** Yeah, I found this combination from Rednote. Rednote is really an amazing platform where we can get various recipes.

↪ [wrong] "I found this combination from Rednote" → "I got this off Rednote" / "I found it on Rednote" — you find things ON a platform, not FROM it.
↪ [bookish] "Rednote is really an amazing platform where we can get various recipes" → "It's great for recipes - people post all sorts" — "platform / various / we can get" is app-store copy, not how you describe an app to a friend.

**Codex:** Rednote's turned into everyone's cookbook. Last one - is there anything you've been meaning to get to on weekends but never actually do?

↪ [wrong] "I'd have always planed to play" → "I've always meant to play" — "I'd have" is a conditional about an unreal past; you want present perfect. ("planned" also has two n's.)
↪ [awkward] "there is no time left for me" → "I never get round to it" — the English idiom for intending something and never doing it.
↪ [bookish] "I have a tight schedule for learning new things" → "I've got so much I'm trying to learn" — "tight schedule for learning" is calendar language.
