**Codex:** Here's one every dev has lived through. You've looked at a ticket properly and it's a week's work. You say so, and your manager comes back with "really? I'd have thought two days, tops." He's not being hostile - he genuinely thinks it's smaller than it is. What do you say back to him?

**You:** The code change costs two days. But the change touches some core logic where I have to test carefully. So three days covers the testing. A total week for development is a good fit for this ticket.

/ [wrong] "The code change costs two days" -> "The code itself is about two days" - cost frames time as a loss; work takes time.
/ [awkward] "core logic where I have to test carefully" -> "core logic, so I've got to test it properly" - where can't attach to logic.
/ [bookish] "A total week for development is a good fit for this ticket" -> "So all in, I'd put it at a week" - a good fit is for people and jobs, not estimates.
Native: bookish

**Codex:** Splitting the coding from the testing is the right instinct - that's what makes an estimate hard to argue with. Your manager nods, then says: "Hm. Wei did something pretty similar last month, took him two days." What now?

**You:** I reviewed the change of that ticket before. It's similar to mine, but the core logic is different. He added a new notification provider which doesn't touch current logic. But my ticket is migrating current SMS provider to a new one. I have to test carefully to make sure complatibility.

/ [wrong] "make sure complatibility" -> "make sure nothing breaks" - typo aside, make sure wants a clause, not a bare noun.
/ [awkward] "I reviewed the change of that ticket" -> "I looked at his change" - the change of that ticket is a chain of nouns; just name whose it is.
/ [bookish] "doesn't touch current logic" -> "doesn't touch anything that's already there" - current is documentation English here.
