# Day 52 - The mess somebody else left you

Date: 2026-11-09 | Category: work | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 14 | Chunks owned: 92/128 | Chunks credited: 8 | Patterns owned: 6/13

## What We Talked About

A project taken over from a colleague who left a month later, with a feature
already scheduled to ship onto it. The docs turned out to be the good part, the
PM got the half-finished pieces, and the reason nobody argued about the timeline
was - in his own words - the culture. Five exchanges, and the interesting part was
not the story but how much of it he could say with banked expressions already in
hand.

**1. Me:** Have you ever been handed code from another team that was already broken - and had to decide whether it was even worth fixing?

**You said:**

Moreover, a new feature was scheduled to ship after I took over the project. I wanted to push back on it, but the schedule was so tight. Imagine my dismay that I had to push things forward. I spent a lot of time mapping out the business process. I didn't mean to make any bugs, so I'd put it closer to a month.

**Say it like this:**
> Last year I took over a new project from a colleague who was leaving a month later. Since the project had the same concept as the other projects, it was hard to develop. On top of that, a new feature was scheduled to ship after I took over the project. I wanted to push back on it, but the schedule was so tight. Imagine my dismay when I found out I had to push things forward. I spent a lot of time mapping out the business process. I didn't want to ship anything buggy, so I'd put the timeline closer to a month.

## Worth Stealing

> Imagine my dismay when I found out I had to push things forward.

Dismay is a mild, slightly literary word, and it is exactly right here: not angry,
not surprised, just quietly put out. `when I found out` is what makes it land,
because the dismay arrives at the moment of discovery, not at the fact itself.

## Patterns Showing Up Again

None of the drilled structures surfaced. The one worth noting is the abstract
preposition territory: "it was down to the PM" is P014's subject arriving in the
wrong direction - the responsibility sense of "on" was needed, and the cause
preposition came out instead. Same gap, seen from the other side.
