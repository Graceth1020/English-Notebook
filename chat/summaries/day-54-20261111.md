# Day 54 - The ticket nobody owns

Date: 2026-11-11 | Category: work | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 7 | Chunks owned: 100/128 | Chunks credited: 4 | Patterns owned: 7/13

## What We Talked About

A ticket that falls between two teams, and the question of who ends up holding
it. He answered with a PM as owner, `the right to push back` as the team's move,
and a leader conversation as the escape from saying no outright - which is a
better answer than most meetings produce. The session also closed the row that
had been resisting since Day 52, and promoted a pattern that had been drilled
seven times without ever appearing in speech.

## Worth Stealing

> ... it's on the PM, I'd say.

Three words that end an ownership argument. It is the same `on` as `it's on them`
and `that's on me`: the responsibility sits on a surface, and naming the surface
names the owner. This is also the row that failed on Day 52 as "down to the PM",
which is why it was worth the whole session's first question.

## Patterns Showing Up Again

`P014` abstract prepositions - **owned**, on the second unprompted use.

`it's on the PM` is the canonical case: `on` as the surface a responsibility rests
on. The same session also produced the failure mode of the same family, "the
schedule matters **to** the project", where the preposition was right-ish and the
noun on the other side was not a party that can care. One pattern, two outcomes,
one promotion.

## New Errors

- `E099` speak up, not speak it out (fixed-phrase)
- `E100` bare verb after "has the right to"; a later date is pushed back (grammar)
- `E101` who owns it, not "the division" (calque of 分工)
- `E102` no article before a how-clause (grammar)

`E100` is the second time condition/tense drift has appeared right after a modal
("I'd ... I will ..." was the first). The pattern is not the tense itself but
losing the modal's grip one clause later, and it will show up again.
