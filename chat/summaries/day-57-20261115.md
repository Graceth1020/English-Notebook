# Day 57 - The message you read wrong

Date: 2026-11-15 | Category: culture | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 11 | Chunks owned: 104/128 | Chunks credited: 4 | Patterns owned: 7/13

## What We Talked About

A two-word "ok." with no emoji, whether a flat reply means cold or busy, and then
the week's real messages: a leader asking why the testing was slow. The topic
left books alone on purpose - `read something into it` had failed four times in
reading conversations where he could always answer with the book's content - and
it worked in the first exchange, in a text message from a friend.

The second half drifted to work, as it usually does: how fast to answer a
leader, and then a Saturday of basketball, 王者荣耀 and a big meal he did not
give up for overtime.

## The Conversation

**1. Me:** Sunday. Someone sends you a reply that is two words long, no emoji - just "ok." Do you take that as cold, or as a busy person typing with one thumb?

**You said:**
> I won't read something into it, I'd say. It might be a busy person sending a short message.

**中文：**我不会多想，我觉得可能就是人家忙，就发了个短消息。

**Say it like this:**
> I wouldn't read anything into it, I'd say. It might just be a busy person sending a short message.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I won't read something into it | I wouldn't read anything into it | wrong | A negative needs `anything`, and 不至于 is `wouldn't` | 否定要用 anything，不至于用 wouldn't |

**Native:** slightly off

**3. Me:** Pick one of those messages from last week and tell me the whole thing - when it came in, what it looked like on the screen, and how long you sat on it before answering.

**You said:**
> That was a message about the project test progress. My leader asked me why the progress was so slow and whether it could ship in time. It took me half an hour to catch up on the reason. And then I spent ten minutes answering the message, especially expressing how big a deal it is.

**中文：**那是关于项目测试进度的一条消息。我 leader 问我为什么进度这么慢、能不能按时上线。我花了半个小时才弄明白原因。然后我用了十分钟回复，主要是说明这事有多大。

**Say it like this:**
> That was a message about the project test progress. My leader asked me why it was so slow and whether it could ship in time. It took me half an hour to work out why. Then I spent ten minutes answering it, mostly to explain how big a deal the delay was.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | to catch up on the reason | to work out why it was so slow | wrong | `catch up on` is 补上进度 or 了解近况, not 弄清原因 | catch up on 是补进度/了解近况，不是弄清原因 |
| 2 | especially expressing how big a deal it is | mostly to explain how big a deal it was | wrong | `especially` is 尤其; 主要是 is `mostly`, and the tense should match | especially 是"尤其"，这里要的是"主要是" |

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I probably round some friends up | I'd usually round some friends up | wrong | `probably` is 大概会 and pairs with `would`; a habit is `usually` | probably 配 would；习惯用 usually |

## Worth Stealing

> And as a PM, I've got to push things forward. Last week a feature was postponed, and it was on the tech team.

Two work sentences that between them name a role and assign a cause without
hedging - which is the shape a PM actually talks in.

- `C110` **"push things forward"** - "I've got to push things forward."
- `C128` **"it's on them"** - "A feature was postponed, and it was on the tech team."
- `C039` **"be up to the job"** - "The leader thinks you're up to the job."

## New Errors

- `E111` catch up on is 补进度/了解近况, not 弄清原因 (collocation)
- `E112` so far takes the perfect continuous (grammar)
- `E113` especially (尤其) where mostly (主要是) is meant (other)
- `E114` plenty takes no article (a plenty of) (grammar)
