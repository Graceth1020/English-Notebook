# Day 08 - Holidays, and the ones nobody enjoys

Date: 2026-09-02 | Category: culture | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 22 | Chunks owned: 2/57

## What We Talked About

National Day and the country-wide travel rush. It turned into something more
specific: you go home to Jieyang twice a year, four hours each way, with a car
sitting outside. The three reasons you gave - no tickets, brutal traffic, eight
hours round trip - are all true, but all three turn out to be **downstream of the
same constraint**, which only surfaced in exchange 4: you need a full block of
leave, and only a public holiday gives you one. The tickets are hard to get
*because* everyone is squeezed into that same week; the traffic is brutal for the
same reason; and eight hours of driving is only worth it when the trip is longer
than a weekend.

(The first version of this summary said those reasons "fell over when pushed". That
was too strong - they did not collapse, they were incomplete. Nothing you said was
wrong; it was all a symptom of the constraint rather than the constraint itself.)

First session with no fix cap. Twenty-two repairs plus five optional upgrades,
where the old rules would have shown fifteen at most and hidden the rest.

**1. Me:** Something I've never got my head around about Chinese holidays: everyone gets the same week off, so the whole country travels at once. National Day is coming up. Are you going anywhere, or is that exactly the reason you're staying put?

**You said:**
> I usually go back home during National holiday. Since I works in Foshan and my parents are in Jieyang, I just have two chances to go back home every year. It is kind of a luxury for me.

> I usually go back home over National Day. I work in Foshan and my parents are in Jieyang, so I only get back twice a year - it's kind of a luxury for me.

**2. Me:** Hang on - Foshan to Jieyang is four hours at most, and you still call twice a year a luxury? That's the part I want to understand. But first: what happens when you actually try to book for that week?

**中文：**那周的火车票很难抢，因为大家都在同一时间买。不过去年我买了车，从那之后就自己开车回去。但堵车很严重，因为大家都往同一个方向跑。

## Worth Stealing

- `C049` **"over (a holiday)"** - "I go back over National Day." Use *during* only with *the ... holiday*.
- `C050` **"the traffic is brutal"** - the spoken *traffic is a serious problem*. Also *a nightmare*, *horrendous*.
- `C051` **"heading in the same direction"** - direction takes *in*; *heading* beats *going*.
- `C052` **"..., though"** at the end of a sentence - the spoken *However*. "The traffic can be brutal, though."
- `C053` **"my parents' place"** - not *where my parents live*.
- `C054` **"I only get back twice a year"** - frequency in the verb, not in *chances*.
- `C055` **"that doesn't hold up"** - 站不住脚. A reason that sounds fine until one question lands on it. Also *that doesn't hold water*.
- `C056` **"that's a symptom, not the reason"** - separates what you can see from what is causing it.
- `C057` **"that's downstream of (X)"** - the root-cause phrase natives use at work. "All three of those are downstream of the same constraint."

These last three came out of a Chinese question about the word 站不住, not out of the
chat itself, so they are recognition until you produce one unprompted. They belong
in a work argument more than a holiday one - the natural home is a design review or
an incident post-mortem, where the whole job is separating a symptom from its cause.

## Patterns Showing Up Again

- **Missing article on a singular countable noun** - *Remote job means*, now **3 hits**
  and three consecutive sessions (`chat:E031`). This is no longer an occasional slip;
  it is the one mechanical gap that survives every topic. Pair with `coach:E008`,
  the mirror case where an article gets added to an uncountable.
- **Tense drifts on the first sentence of a hypothetical** (exchange 5, new as
  `chat:E037`) - *I'll go* / *means* / *I can get*, then correct by the third
  sentence. The rule is known; it does not survive the moment of starting to speak.
- **Chinese frame nouns carried across** - *chances* for opportunities (exchange 1),
  *once* for 每次 (exchange 3). Same habit as *the change of that ticket* on Day 05:
  the Chinese noun gets a dictionary equivalent instead of the English verb.
- **Report register is topic-driven, confirmed again** - the only bookish lines came
  when you were explaining a system to an outsider (*a serious problem*, *However*,
  *I need to work in the office*). Across eight sessions: 27 of 45 exchanges.
- **A phrase from the question gets echoed back** - *I don't buy that*, lifted from
  my previous turn and used with the wrong meaning. Second instance after *secret
  storylines* on Day 07. Borrowing my wording is the recognition trap in miniature.
