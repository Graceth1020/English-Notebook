# Day 04 - AI and whether coding is a dead end

Date: 2026-08-31 | Category: opinion | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 15 | Chunks owned: 0/23

## What We Talked About

Whether AI makes software careers a dead end. You said no, then had to defend it
twice as the counter-arguments got harder, and landed on domain knowledge as the
thing that keeps you employable. The last two turns turned personal: you'd rather
be heads-down in the zone, but if forced to choose, you'd give up the coding.

First opinion topic. It was picked because register climbs highest when a
sentence has to carry a position - and it did, immediately.

## The Conversation

---

**1. Me:** A lot of people your age are being told that learning to code is a dead end now, that AI will write it all in a few years. You're a full-stack dev. Do you actually buy that?

**You said:**
> No, I don't think so. Though AI's ability to write code has developed quickly, we still got to check the result. And this is based on our basic knowledge. Learning to code helps us build solid knowledge.

**中文：**不，我不这么认为。虽然 AI 写代码的能力发展很快，但我们还是得检查结果。而这要靠我们自己的基础知识。学编程能帮我们打下扎实的基础。

**Say it like this:**
> No, I don't buy it. AI's got a lot better at writing code, but you've still got to check what it gives you - and you can only do that if you actually know the language yourself.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Though AI's ability to write code has developed quickly, we... | AI's got a lot better at writing code, but... | bookish | A fronted *Though X, Y* is essay structure. Speech states the thing, then swings back with *but*. | 前置的 Though 从句是作文结构；口语先说事实，再用 but 转折。 |
| 2 | we still got to check the result | you've still got to check what it gives you | wrong | *Got to* needs *have*. Spoken: *you've still gotta check it*. | got to 前面要有 have，口语常缩成 you've still gotta。 |
| 3 | based on our basic knowledge / build solid knowledge | you actually know the language yourself | bookish | *Knowledge* twice in two sentences. English usually turns 知识 into a verb - *know X*, *know how to X*. | 两句里出现两次 knowledge；英语通常把「知识」变成动词 know。 |

**Native:** bookish

**2. Me:** Let me push on it. Nobody says AI writes perfect code today. The claim is about five years out - reviewing output becomes a much smaller job than writing it, and companies need three developers where they needed ten. "You still have to check it" doesn't touch that. Checking is cheaper than building. So if you're one of the ten, what makes you one of the three?

**You said:**
> I have to say business. AI's got better at writing, but the business is different and complicated on different industries. Having an engineer understanding the business deeply is kind of a luxury for the company.

**中文：**我觉得是业务。AI 写代码是变强了，但不同行业的业务千差万别、很复杂。一个真正懂业务的工程师，对公司来说算是一种奢侈品。

**Say it like this:**
> The business side, I'd say. AI's got better at writing code, but the business is different in every industry - and an engineer who really understands it is kind of a luxury for a company.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I have to say business | The business side, I'd say | awkward | *I have to say X* sounds like a reluctant admission. You meant *my answer is X*. | I have to say 听起来像勉强承认；你想说的是「我的答案是」。 |
| 2 | different and complicated on different industries | different in every industry | wrong | Wrong preposition, and *different* is already in the sentence. | 介词错；而且 different 前面已经有了。 |
| 3 | Having an engineer understanding the business deeply | an engineer who really understands it | wrong | *Having someone doing* doesn't work here - it needs a relative clause. | having sb doing 结构不成立，要用关系从句。 |

**Native:** wrong

**3. Me:** That's the right answer. But there's an obvious comeback: domain knowledge isn't protected either. A model with your company's entire Slack history, every ticket, every postmortem - that's a lot of business context, and it doesn't quit. Some would say the engineer who "understands the business" is the first to go, because that understanding is written down somewhere. Where's the part of your job that genuinely isn't written down?

**You said:**
> Still the business, I'd say. Whenever a totally new business model emerges, it's hard for LLM to design the process, the logic or that kind of things. Even the professional team have got to communicate to design.

**中文：**我还是觉得是业务。每当出现一个全新的业务模式，LLM 很难设计出流程、逻辑这类东西。就算是专业团队，也得先沟通才能设计出来。

**Say it like this:**
> Still the business, I'd say. Whenever a totally new business model comes along, it's hard for an LLM to design the process, the logic, that kind of thing. Even a team of experts has to talk it through first.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | it's hard for LLM to design | it's hard for an LLM to design | wrong | A countable singular noun needs an article. | 可数名词单数必须带冠词。 |
| 2 | that kind of things | that kind of thing | wrong | Fixed singular. Casual alternative: *that sort of stuff*. | that kind of thing 固定用单数。 |
| 3 | Even the professional team have got to communicate to design | Even a team of experts has to talk it through first | wrong | Generic *a*, singular agreement after *team*, and *communicate to design* is a calque. | 泛指用 a；team 用单数；communicate to design 是中式直译。 |

**Native:** wrong

**4. Me:** You didn't fold, and "it isn't written down because it doesn't exist yet" is sharper than where you started. But say you're right - the job becomes domain thinking plus talking it through with people. Less time in an editor, more time in meetings. Does that sound like a job you'd want?

**You said:**
> Actually, I'd rather avoid that kind of thing. I am really into coding especially when I code in a silent environment like a library. I shut off everything which I might get distracted by to stay focused. I fall into a state called zone.

**中文：**其实我更想躲开那类事。我是真的很喜欢写代码，尤其是在图书馆那种安静的地方写。我会关掉所有可能让我分心的东西来保持专注。我会进入一种叫「心流」的状态。

**Say it like this:**
> Honestly, I'd rather avoid that. I'm really into coding, especially somewhere quiet like a library. I shut off anything that might distract me so I can stay focused - I get in the zone.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | in a silent environment like a library | somewhere quiet, like a library | bookish | *Environment* is questionnaire English, and *silent* is stronger than you meant. | environment 是问卷用语；silent 语气过强，日常说 quiet。 |
| 2 | everything which I might get distracted by | anything that might distract me | awkward | Correct but takes the long way round - passive plus a stranded preposition. Also *that* for restrictive clauses. | 语法对但绕：被动+介词后置；限定从句用 that。 |
| 3 | I fall into a state called zone | I get in the zone | wrong | Fixed phrase, and it takes *the*. You were explaining the term; native speakers just use it. | 固定说法，必须带 the；你在解释这个词，母语者直接用。 |

**Native:** slightly off

**5. Me:** You said what keeps you employable is understanding the business - which is mostly talking to people. And the part you'd protect is heads-down time in the zone. Those pull in opposite directions. If you had to give one up, which goes?

**You said:**
> Coding, honestly. Understanding business keeps me employable, and this is how I get money. If I want to be able to get a higher salary, I've got to be focused on learning more business knowledge. I can kind of doing some coding things whenever I am free.

**中文：**说实话，放弃写代码。懂业务才让我有饭吃，钱是从这儿来的。想拿更高的工资，我就得专注在多学点业务上。写代码这种事，有空的时候还能弄一弄。

**Say it like this:**
> Coding, honestly. Understanding the business is what keeps me employable, and that's what pays. If I want a higher salary I've got to focus on learning more about the business - I can still do a bit of coding whenever I'm free.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | this is how I get money | that's what pays | awkward | Grammatical but blunt - closer to how a child would put it. | 语法没错但太直白，像小孩说话。 |
| 2 | learning more business knowledge | learning more about the business | bookish | Second *knowledge* of the session. Same fix as exchange 1. | 本场第二次 knowledge，和第 1 轮同一个问题。 |

## Patterns Showing Up Again

- **Report register under argument** (exchanges 1, 4, 5): the fronted *Though*
  clause, *environment*, and *knowledge* twice. Predicted before the session and
  it landed in the first sentence. Now 17 of 25 exchanges across four days.
- **知识 rendered as `knowledge`** (exchanges 1, 5): *basic knowledge*, *solid
  knowledge*, *business knowledge*. Three times in five turns - the single most
  repeated item today. English almost always turns it into a verb: *know the
  language*, *learn more about the business*.
- **Articles on countable singulars** (exchanges 2, 3): *for LLM*, *the
  professional team*. Matches the long-standing coach row about articles.
- **Verb + to + verb as a purpose calque** (exchange 3): *communicate to design*,
  the same shape as Day 03's *couldn't help stopping myself to read it*.
