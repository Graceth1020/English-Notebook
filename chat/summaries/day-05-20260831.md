# Day 05 - Estimates and pushing back on a deadline

Date: 2026-08-31 | Category: work | Exchanges: 5 | Fixes: 20 | Chunks owned: 0/29

## What We Talked About

A single scene, held for the whole session: a manager thinks your one-week ticket
is a two-day ticket. He pushed four separate times - a flat "two days, tops", a
named colleague who did something similar faster, a client deadline already
promised, and finally the claim that monitoring makes the testing unnecessary.

The estimate was defended well and the week was won. Four coach rows retired,
three of them on things that had failed three lessons each.

Two Codex mistakes are recorded below rather than quietly fixed, because both
were caught by the learner and both distort the record if left in: a rewrite in
exchange 3 that invented content, and a turn in exchange 5 that ended without a
question and then scored the resulting confusion as an error.

## The Conversation

---

**1. Me:** Here's one every dev has lived through. You've looked at a ticket properly and it's a week's work. You say so, and your manager comes back with "really? I'd have thought two days, tops." He's not being hostile - he genuinely thinks it's smaller than it is. What do you say back to him?

**You said:**
> The code change costs two days. But the change touches some core logic where I have to test carefully. So three days covers the testing. A total week for development is a good fit for this ticket.

**中文：**代码本身两天就够了，但这个改动碰到了核心逻辑，得仔细测，测试大概要三天。加起来一周比较合理。

**Say it like this:**
> The code itself is about two days. But it touches some core logic, so I've got to test it properly - that's another three days. So all in, I'd put it at a week.

**What changed:**

| # | You wrote | Natural | Label | Why | Why 中文 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The code change costs two days | The code itself is about two days | wrong | Work *takes* time; *cost* frames it as a loss and sounds like money. | 工作是 take time；cost 把时间说成损失，而且像在说钱。 |
| 2 | core logic where I have to test carefully | core logic, so I've got to test it properly | awkward | *where* attaches to places, not to *logic*; the link here is cause, so use *so*. | where 接地点，不接 logic；这里是因果关系，用 so。 |
| 3 | A total week for development is a good fit for this ticket | So all in, I'd put it at a week | bookish | *a good fit* is for people and jobs. An estimate is a number you *put it at*. | a good fit 用于人和岗位；估时用 put it at。 |

**Native:** bookish

**2. Me:** Splitting the coding from the testing is the right instinct - that's what makes an estimate hard to argue with. Your manager nods, then says: "Hm. Wei did something pretty similar last month, took him two days." What now?

**You said:**

**中文：**我看过他那个改动，看着像，但核心逻辑不一样。他是新加一个通知渠道，不动现有的；我是把现在的短信渠道换掉，得测到不出问题。

**Say it like this:**

## Worth Stealing

- `C024` **"I'd put it closer to a week"** - counter an estimate with your own number rather than rejecting theirs.
- `C025` **"that's after the fact"** - the exact tool missing in exchange 5. "Monitoring helps, but that's after the fact."
- `C026` **"talk something through"** - "We talked it through last week."
- `C027` **"all in"** - "So all in, I'd put it at a week."
- `C028` **"let's aim to..."** - proposes a date without ordering anyone: "Let's aim to ship it next Wednesday."
- `C029` **"for what it's worth"** - softens an opinion you know may not land. Used by the manager in exchange 5; worth stealing back.

## Patterns Showing Up Again

- **Register climbs when the sentence carries a position** (exchanges 1, 2, 3) -
  *a good fit*, *current logic*, *third-party*. Now 20 of 30 exchanges across five
  sessions. Unchanged, and still the dominant pattern.
- **Nouns where English uses verbs** (exchanges 2, 3) - *the change of that
  ticket*, *make sure complatibility*. Day 04 had three `knowledge` in five turns.
  This is the same habit: a Chinese noun phrase carried straight across instead of
  being turned into a verb.
- **Withdrawn:** a third pattern was claimed here - that the position was
  surrendered once the pressure came off in exchange 5 - and logged as
  `chat:E023`. It was wrong. That turn ended with narration and no task, so a
  different question was answered. Row deleted, and the real fault is now
  Non-Negotiable 4 in the skill: every turn must end with an unmistakable ask.
  Holding a position against a *friendly* challenge therefore remains untested.
