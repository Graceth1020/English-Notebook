## What We Talked About

New week: the business colleague will be busy with a more urgent project, and the taken-over project is low-priority - so he sets aside more time for the spec as the deadline approaches. The grind is reading an unfamiliar C# codebase to extract business processes, and that is too much of a hassle because he is not familiar with C#. His method: the C# framework shares the same concept as SpringMVC (what he used before), so he finds the web pages, visits them, reads through the logs to see how each layer handles a request, and when he runs into a problem he debugs the request context and steps into the core logic. He knows he has read enough when the whole process is mapped out - after a small meeting with the business side to get clear on what each business part does. The pipeline from spec to Java: tech team reviews the spec and flags unclear parts, developers write a system design doc, a review meeting reads through every doc, designs that do not hold up get rejected, and once all leaders approve, coding starts. For a startup this is too heavy - with a tight schedule, the tech team should join the requirements stage early and kick off system design the moment the spec is done.
