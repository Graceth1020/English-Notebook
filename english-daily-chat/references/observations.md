## "Reached but broken" - recognition transfers, assembly does not (2026-09-20)

The learner reaches for chunk/pattern language under production pressure and the
fixed form comes out damaged, even though the chunk was clearly recognised and
aimed for. This is the forward signal - recognition is transferring - and it is
scored `missed` (never credited), because the damaged form is not ownership.

Instances so far:

- C001 `couldn't put it down` -> "I'll not to put it down" (Day 22, 6th try; modal + to garbling)
- C017 `all sorts` -> "post sorts" (Day 20; came out whole Day 22)
- C039 `be up to the job` -> "be up to my job" (Day 20)
- C040 `out of my depth` -> "people out of our depth" (Day 20; idiom applied to others)
- C056 `that's a symptom, not the reason` - missed 4x before landing whole (Day 21)
- C091 `high-spec` -> "has a high-sec" (Day 44, 1st try; spec->sec + adjective used as a noun)
- C045 `get in the way of` -> "gets ON the way" (Day 44; preposition broke, pending row so no off-scene confirm either)
- C124 hint contamination (Day 44): named in Day 43's Two To Use, produced correctly next day - credit REVERTED. Two To Use naming = one-day hint; a chunk named there can only be credited from a later session.
- C126 `get involved in (something)` -> "which different teams are get involved in" (Day 46; auxiliary stack broke: are + get cannot coexist; reached-but-broken, logged missed).
- C089 `matter to (someone)` -> "it really matters for the developers" (Day 46; preposition slot broke, to -> for; reached-but-broken, logged missed).
- C127 `write things down` -> "write any plan down" (Day 47; fixed part broke: things -> any plan, plus my own question had hinted the slot; reached-but-broken, logged missed).
- C086 `a one-day (X) pass` / C116 `so far` (Day 47) - both were named in Codex's own opening/question, then produced back next turn; same-session echo, no credit (same rule as the C124 case below).

Pattern in the damage: the fixed parts break - the particle, the article, the
modal+to glue - never the content words. The learner knows the chunk; the
assembly slot is what gives way under load.

What to do: never penalise the reach. Log `missed` when the form breaks, credit
when it comes out whole (C017 and C056 both landed whole the next time, so the
reach is a real step). When one mechanism recurs, promote it to a pattern drill.
Current candidate: **P009 modal + bare infinitive** - "I won't put it down",
never "I won't to put it down" (one instance, Day 22). Not worth a drill until
it recurs.


## "Form intact, wrong slot" - the chunk reaches the wrong concept (2026-10-31)

Distinct from fixed-part damage above. The fixed phrase comes out whole, but
its meaning slot does not match what the sentence talks about:

- C125 `high-priority / low-priority` -> "a high-priority drink" (Day 45). The
  adjective ranks tasks and projects, not beverages; he meant the default/go-to
  drink. Form was perfect, concept wrong - logged `missed`, same as a broken
  form, because the learner has not owned the collocation's boundary yet.

Watch for the next occurrence: if a chunk keeps landing on a nearby but
wrong concept, the gap is semantic scope, not assembly - a contrast card
(high-priority task vs default drink) may be more useful than another seed.

## Prompt comprehension: abstract options stall, concrete A/B does not (2026-10-31)

Day 45 exchange 4 opened with "settle everything in one go / sort it out piece
by piece" - he could not parse it after one plain-language unpacking, and asked
for a second round. A concrete two-option example (one message with the whole
plan vs several messages, one decision each) unblocked it immediately. When a
planning or process question stalls twice, skip the abstract vocabulary and
write the two situations as labeled mini-examples before asking again. Those
clarification rounds are `[meta]`, never counted as exchanges.


## "I + verb" every time - the fronted subject never comes out (2026-09-20)

Every sentence expressing a thing or a wish opens with "I + verb"; the object or
idea is never put in subject position. Not grammar - nothing ever errors - it is
the fronted-subject frame (passive, gerund subject, "X is the plan") that cannot
be recalled. Same mechanism as C031, one level up: C031 is a noun slot, this is
a whole-sentence frame.

- "I follow the rule strictly." -> "The rule gets followed strictly."
- "I want to read books before bed." -> "Books before bed are still the plan."

Handling (in chat, C031-style mechanism test): when a non-I subject appears
naturally ("The rule holds" / "Doing X helps" / "A day of work wears me out"),
mark `[good]` - any correct subject word counts, not a fixed phrase. When a run
of sentences all opens with I, say one gentle line - never a formal correction.

Logged as **P010** (P009 stays reserved for modal + bare infinitive). Status
`owned` as of Day 47: "Whether I skip a day depends on..." (exchange 3) was the
unprompted non-I-subject production; `pattern-used` logged it (used=9/2). Keep
praising the frame on sight, but no further logging needed.

## A general label is not a different context (2026-11-08)

Six pending rows were produced correctly and unprompted today - C040, C064, C073,
C091, C125 (all home `work`) and C001 (home `reading`) - and none was confirmed.
The day was labelled `general`, so the command would have accepted all six. They
were counted as uses and held, because every one of them arrived inside the
retelling of the same junior-project story they were banked from, and C001 arrived
inside a book recommendation, which is literally its home territory. A re-telling
is not portability, and a false `owned` removes a row while it is still
recognition-only. Test to apply: not the day's scene label, but whether the
situation being described is new. Day 50 confirmed two work/family rows because
they surfaced in a travel answer where they had never been used before; today's
did not.

- The consequence for scheduling: this learner's default repertoire for any open
  question is engineering anecdotes. Ask "when were you thrown in" and the answer
  is first-year coding even though the day is labelled general. Any day intended
  to confirm a work-homed pending has to be a topic with no technical surface at
  all - food, health, family, shopping - otherwise it silently becomes a work chat
  with a general label. Five rows now sit at 3 uses each waiting for exactly that.
- C092 "push back on" was missed for the second time, and the question asked for it
  almost verbatim ("did you actually say something about it?"). He reached for
  "I spoke up my opinion" instead, which is both a wrong construction and a
  different verb. When a direct opening still does not pull the chunk, the chunk
  is not available for that function - consider whether C092 should be replaced by
  the plainer "say something" alongside it.
- Instrument-versus-agent appeared twice in one session: "a framework which can
  integrate into every business system" and "when the application deployed on the
  cloud server". Two occurrences in one day is row-worthy if it shows up again -
  the Chinese habit is to let the inanimate subject act, and English requires the
  passive.
- Fixed phrases rebuilt from the middle: "take no for my answer" for "take no for
  an answer". He has the first half and the concept, and re-grows the ending each

### Correction, same day (2026-11-08)

The block above held all six pendings. On review with the learner, the test it
used was too strict. The ledger's rule is scene, not narrative: C001 sits home
`reading` and C040 C056 C064 C074 C090 C091 C109 C111 C125 C126 all sit home
`work`, while a `daily` topic runs on scene `general` - so the off-scene condition
is met the moment the phrase comes out in a session that is not those scenes, and
the day being about code does not change the recorded scene. Eleven rows were
confirmed. Keep the narrower caution for a different case only: a chunk produced
while quoting back Codex's own wording, or the same sentence from the same
session, is still not evidence. The remaining five (C089, C101, C102, C127, C077)
are home `general` and now need a session in some other scene.

### Typing slips vs assembly failures (2026-11-08)

C030, C112 and C120 were first logged `missed` and the learner reclassified them
as typing slips; they are now `used` and sit at pending. The line drawn: where the
damage is a word inside an otherwise intact frame ("hasn't happen" for "hasn't
happened", "for my answer" for "for an answer"), take the learner's call and
credit the use - the morphology there is already tracked as E090, and quarrelling
over it double-charges the same error. Where the damaged part IS the row's subject
(C120 exists solely to install the "as"), credit the use but do not confirm:
pending is the right resting place until a clean production appears.

### Retro audit of days 48-50 (2026-11-08)

Scanning the transcripts against all 128 rows turned up four productions that were
never credited, all from long answers where only the sentence being corrected got
noticed. C053 "my parents' place" (day 48), C092 "push back on" (day 48), C107

The frame he has is "the same as", not "the same X as Y", so seeding it produces
the short form every time.

## The prompt that gets answered by the thesis

C058 "in a way that sticks" was asked for as "what made that one land when the
others did not", and the reply was "It's about patience" - the book's argument
rather than the mechanism. C036 went the same way: a question about inventing a

## Day 54 - The row that closed with a different noun

C128 "it's on them" was seeded three times and produced nothing: on Day 52 the
answer was "it was down to the PM". Today the same situation pulled "it's on the
PM", which is the mechanism with the slot filled by a different person - and that
is exactly what the bank-the-mechanism rule says to credit.

The lesson is about the seeding question, not the learner. Day 52 asked "whose
problem was that?", which is a question about causality as much as ownership;
"down to" answers it plausibly. Today's opening was "who ends up chasing it" -
a question that only has owners as answers. When a chunk refuses to appear, check
whether the opening can be answered correctly without it.

## When the frame is right and the slot is wrong

"The schedule really matters to the project" - `matter to` needs a party that can
care, and a project cannot. C089 has been pending at 3/3 for months and this is
its failure mode: the frame is solid, the noun after `to` is a thing instead of a
someone. Marking it missed rather than used is the honest call, because crediting
it would have promoted a row that is being used wrong.

Same family, same session: P014 was promoted on "it's on the PM". Abstract
prepositions are a two-part skill - which preposition, and what kind of noun it
accepts - and the ledger has only been tracking the first half.
