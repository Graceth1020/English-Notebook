# Form Drill 12 - action chains II: connectors upgraded, first-mention-the earns a row

Date: 2026-10-03 | Axes: tense, article, form, object, prep | Rounds: 3 | Clean: 10/15
Variant: action-chain (scenario + chunk bank + word list), learner-designed in Drill 11. Results not strictly comparable to Drills 01-10.

## Why This Was Run

Learner-requested continuation of Drill 11's action-chain format, with new daily
scenes (brushing teeth, showering, washing dishes). The format gained two pieces
this drill: per-round word lists (nouns alongside the verb bank, learner's idea),
and a connector quota set mid-drill by the learner's own question about Then-
overuse - max one `then`, at least one subordinator (once/after/before/while),
at least one purpose or causal link. Also new this drill: scene chunks are now
recorded in `form/chunks/` (per-scene files with original + optimized passages),
kept out of the chat chunk bank, which stays general-purpose.

## Round 1 - easy (one main object: the toothbrush)

**Prompt:** Walk me through brushing your teeth, step by step - from picking up the toothbrush to putting everything back. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把刷牙的完整流程按顺序讲一遍，从拿起牙刷到把东西放回去。每个动作都写到。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> Let me walk you through how I brush my teeth. First, I switch on the faucet to wash my cup and toothbrush. Then I fill up the cup, squeeze some toothpaste onto the toothbrush. I drink some water and spit it to wash my mouth first, and begin to brush my tooth up and down. After brushing, I put all things back and tide up my appearance looking in the mirror.

**tense:** ok
**article:** "brush my tooth" -> "brush my teeth" (the fixed phrase takes plural; number judgement, second instance after Drill 10's "the job markets")
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** "spit it to wash my mouth" -> "spit it out" (particle dropped; `spit` + object alone means something else)

**Note:** Not counted. The first-mention rule from Drill 11 held: "my cup"
introduced with possessive, correctly switched to "the cup" on second mention.
Out of scope, all recorded in `form/chunks/bathroom.md`: "switch on the faucet"
-> "turn on the tap" (water turns, devices switch); "wash my mouth" -> "rinse
my mouth (out)"; "tide up" -> "tidy up" (real-word slip); "put all things back"
-> "put everything back"; "give myself a once-over in the mirror" banked as the
natural version of his closing move.

## Round 2 - medium (two-plus containers)

**Prompt:** Walk me through taking a shower, step by step - from turning the water on to toweling off. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把洗澡的完整流程按顺序讲一遍，从开水到擦干。每个动作都写到。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> I step in the bathroom to take a shower. I turn on the shower head to test the water. After setting the water temperature to the right temperature, I wet my hair and put some conditioner onto it. Then I grab the shower head to rinse off my hair. Then I use body wash to lather up. At last, I dry myself with a towel.

**tense:** ok
**article:** ok
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** "I step in the bathroom" -> "step into the bathroom" (movement into a space takes `into`; `step in` alone = intervene or pop in)

**Note:** Not counted, and mostly good news. "turn on the shower head" had the
definite article right (own bathroom's fixture), "a towel" was correctly
indefinite on first mention - the Drill 11 rule firing on both hard cases.
"rinse off my hair" kept its particle, the thing that broke in Round 1. Logic
slip: conditioner applied before any shampoo and rinsed straight off; the
sequence wet -> shampoo -> rinse -> conditioner -> rinse is now in the
optimized passage. Also "the shower", not "the shower head".

## Round 3 - hard (a sink full of small things)

**Prompt:** Walk me through washing the dishes after dinner, step by step - from clearing the table to everything being on the drying rack. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把晚饭后洗碗的完整流程按顺序讲一遍，从收拾桌子到所有东西放上沥水架。每个动作都写到。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> I fill up the sink and put plates, bowls and chopsticks in it with dish soap. Then I wet the towel to clean the table. After that, I begin to wash things in the sink with a sponge. Once I clean them all up, I put them on the drying rack. At last, it's the greasy pan's turn. I squirt some dish soap to it, scrub it and rinse off the soap.

**tense:** ok
**article:** "I wet the towel to clean the table" -> "wet a towel / a cloth" (first-mention definite: the towel came from his own head, not the word list, so the listener has not met it; drying rack and greasy pan were list-supplied and correctly definite)
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** "squirt some dish soap to it" -> "squirt ... onto it" (the bank itself said `onto`)

**Note:** Not counted. The pronoun stress test passed: `it` tracked sink then
pan, `them` tracked dishes, three switches all clean. The self-set connector
quota was met in full: one `then`, "Once I clean them all up", a purpose link
("to clean the table"), and "After that" / "At last" used once each as stage
markers. "it's the greasy pan's turn" is genuinely idiomatic - and the pan
last is the correct real-world order.

## What It Showed

**1. First-mention `the` earned its own row: `chat:E072`, opened at 3 hits.**

`the bowl` (Drill 11 R2), `the cup` (Drill 11 R3), `the towel` (Drill 12 R3) -
three instances, one mechanism: the object is definite in the narrator's head
because he can see it, and the listener's first-mention perspective is never
checked. Distinct from `chat:E056` (definite for a *hypothetical class*) and
from `chat:E031` (slot empty) - this is the third article subtype in the
matrices, and the one the action-chain format is uniquely good at exposing,
since every round introduces fresh objects. The towel miss came with a built-in
control: the same round used list-supplied objects (`the drying rack`, `the
greasy pan`) correctly definite, and Drill 12 R2 used "a towel" correctly
indefinite. The rule is available; the perspective check is what is missing.

**2. `prep` misses narrowed from prepositions to particles.**

Round 1 `spit out`, Round 2 `step into`, Round 3 `squirt onto` - all phrasal-
verb particles, zero preposition errors. The glue problem is not gone, but its
center of gravity moved one notch: whole chunks carry the right preposition
when the chunk is known ("rinse off my hair", "put some conditioner onto it"),
and what drops is the particle of a verb he is building on the fly.

**3. `tense` and `form` clean across the whole drill** - with the variant caveat
(chunk bank supplies support) already on record from Drill 11. The connector
quota, set mid-drill in response to his own question, was met completely in
Round 3: one `then`, one subordinator, one purpose link. This is the first
time a self-imposed style constraint has been set and met inside a drill.

**4. The scene-chunk library is now the drill's second output.**

`form/chunks/` holds bathroom (12 + 11 chunks) and kitchen (19 + 10), each
round stored as original + optimized passage plus a status-tagged chunk table.
The learner memorizes the optimized passages; the statuses show which chunks
were produced, corrected, or supplied.
