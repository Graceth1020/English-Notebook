## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** You've decided to try an unfamiliar open-source project on your own machine - say something like JeecgBoot. Walk me through what you actually do, in order, from the moment you have the repository to the moment the thing runs in your browser. Include the part where something doesn't work the first time.

**中文：**讲讲你在本地跑一个陌生开源项目的完整过程——从拿到代码库到浏览器里能打开，按顺序说，包括第一次没跑起来时你怎么处理。

**He wrote:**
> First of all, whenever I want to use a new open-source project, I'll visit its website and read the "get start" section. I can sort out where to start by it. Second, I'll read the readme file on its GitHub. Readme can always help developers get my head around the project through the functions. At last, I'll clone it and run it past the guild.

**tense:** ok

**article:** "Readme can always help developers" -> "The readme can always help developers" (bare singular countable, `chat:E031`)

**form:** the "get start" section -> the "Getting Started" section (bare `get` where the fixed name takes `-ing`, `start` where it needs `-ed`)

**object:** ok

**Note:** Not counted. `object` was clean throughout - `visit its website`, `read the readme file`, `clone it`, `run it` all carry their objects, which is the axis that missed here in Drill 03. Out of scope: "help developers get my head around" mixes the person, and with `developers` as subject needs "get their heads around it"; "sort out where to start by it" should be "from it"; "run it past the guild" is two errors in three words, since `guide` is the intended word and "run something past someone" means to seek their approval rather than to follow instructions.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** A team is starting a system that they will own and maintain for the next five years. They can build it on a low-code platform, or write it themselves on a plain framework. Pick one - actually pick, don't say it depends - and defend it. Then finish by saying what would have to be true about the project for you to choose the other option instead.

**中文：**一个团队要做一套未来五年都由他们自己维护的系统。用低代码平台，还是自己从零写？必须选一个并说明理由，最后再说：项目具备什么条件时你会改选另一个。

**He wrote:**
> I'd pick up a low-code platform as our basic framework. Since our website has many management pages, and most of them are simple, the low-code design pattern is really a good fit for us. If the pages focused on interaction, we've got to develop it on our own rather than use a low-code platform. The more complicated the page is, the less effective we use a low-code platform.

**tense:** "If the pages focused on interaction, we've got to develop it" -> "If the pages were interaction-heavy, we'd have to develop them" (past-tense hypothetical with a present-tense main clause, `chat:E037`)

**article:** "If the pages focused on interaction" -> "if pages are interaction-heavy" (definite article for a hypothetical class of thing; article present but wrong, not missing - now `chat:E056`)

**form:** "the less effective we use a low-code platform" -> "the less effective it becomes" (wrong verb shape in the second half of the correlative comparative; the first half, "The more complicated the page is", is built correctly)

**object:** ok

**Note:** Not counted. "the pages focused on interaction" omits the finite verb, since `focused` here is a past participle and needs "the pages are focused on interaction". "develop it on our own" uses a singular pronoun for the plural antecedent `pages`. "pick up" for `pick` adds a particle the verb does not take in this sense. The round did take a side and did answer the closing question, which Drill 03's Round 2 did not.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** A new developer joins your team with no context on how your system handles a typical request. Pick one real interaction flow - say, a user clicking "save" on a form, or a search that returns results - and describe every piece of the architecture that touches it, in the order they fire. Include the database, the cache, the front-end component, the API layer, and any middleware or background job. Your goal is to give someone who knows nothing about your system a mental map of what talks to what.

**中文：**新同事对你系统的架构一无所知。选一个实际请求流程（比如用户点「保存」或搜索），按顺序说出每个环节——前端组件、API、缓存、数据库、中间件、后台任务——让新人能画出整个请求的路径图。

**Handed back, not marked:** Let's break a request process step by step. First, open your network tab and click the save button within the user form, you can see a http post request whose URL is "/api/user/save". Then, open the user project and find the function which is related to this url. Step in the save function to next layer. In this code snippet, you can clearly see how it calls the database api to insert data.

**He wrote:**
> See this section on the page, this component is an adding user form. Then we fill up the form and click the save button. It'll post an http request to our backend API layer. Before it is entering the real handler, a filter will get the privileges from the Redis cache to check whether the current user has right to assess the URL. After checking, the business handler will handle it and call database api to insert it. Finally, a background job will be hired to record the operation logs.

**tense:** "Before it is entering the real handler" -> "Before it enters the real handler" (present continuous for a punctual transition in a sequence)

**article:** "has right to assess the URL" -> "has the right to" (bare singular countable, `chat:E031`)

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**Note:** Not counted. `form` and `object` were both clean across six sentences: `will post`, `will get ... to check`, `will handle`, `will be hired`, `to insert` are correctly shaped, including the switch to passive voice for the background job, and two chained `it` references each resolve. Out of scope: "assess the URL" should be "access the URL", a real word in the wrong slot that no spellchecker catches; "a background job will be hired" needs "triggered" or "kicked off", since `hire` applies to people; "an adding user form" stacks modifiers on the Chinese pattern where English takes "an add-user form". The handed-back attempt above also contained "a http post request" for "an http" and "to next layer" for "to the next layer", neither counted, since only the completed version is marked.

## What It Showed

**1. `article` missed in all three rounds - the first axis to do so.**

Three drills had produced scattered article misses; this one produced a clean sweep,
across all three difficulty levels. Two were bare singular countables (`chat:E031`:
*Readme can help*, *has right to*) and one was the wrong article present (*the pages*
for a hypothetical class). Control 01 scored 12/12 on this same material with the
subject announced in the heading, four days earlier.

The strongest single piece of evidence sits in the handed-back attempt rather than
the marked one. Control 01 tested the /eɪtʃ/ vowel rule as a discrete item and he got
it right. The first pass at Round 3 produced "a http post request" while he was busy
describing an architecture; the redo produced "an http request" correctly. Same rule,
same learner, three days apart, and the variable is how much attention was left over.
That is the premise of this whole track, now shown on one specific rule instead of an
error category.

**2. `object` was clean in all three rounds, immediately after missing twice.**

Drill 03 was `object`'s first outing and it missed in two rounds of three, including
the easiest. Drill 04 has it clean everywhere, in a drill whose Round 3 was dense
with transitive verbs and chained pronoun references - `call database api to insert
it`, `record the operation logs`, `handle it` - which is the shape that produced three
misses in one session on Day 05.

One clean drill does not resolve a 7-hit row, and the round that best tested it was
written after a hand-back, with more attention than a first pass. Verify in Drill 05
before concluding anything.

**3. The hand-back rule worked, and the failure mode was content, not length.**

Drill 03's Carry Forward said to hand back short rounds before marking. Round 3 came
in at five sentences - long enough - but answered a different question: it described
how a new joiner could *discover* the architecture using devtools, naming two of the
six required components. The redo named all six in firing order.

Round 3 exists to force an article judgement at every noun, and a devtools
walkthrough contains few nouns needing judgement. So the measurement rule needs
widening. Sentence count is a proxy for the thing that actually matters, which is
whether the answer contains enough of the required *kind* of clause. Drill 03 lost
data to short rounds; Drill 04 nearly lost data to a long-enough round that dodged
the task.

**4. Tense produced a third distinct shape.**

Drill 03 was present perfect with a definite past time. Round 2 here is the
`chat:E037` hypothetical. Round 3 is continuous aspect for a punctual event. Three
drills, three different mechanisms, all filed under one axis - which is fine for the
matrix but means "tense: miss" is not one repeating error being tracked toward
resolution.

**5. Round 1 broke its streak - upward.**

Three drills had produced exactly one Round 1 miss each. This one produced two. Not a
curve, just the end of the only stable regularity the track had, which is worth
recording because Drill 03's Carry Forward called it out specifically as evidence
against low stakes being protective.

## Carry Forward

1. **Widen the hand-back rule from length to task fit.** A round gets handed back if
   it misses the stated sentence count *or* fails to produce the kind of content the
   round was designed to elicit - named components for an architecture round, named
   time points for a chronology round. Judge before marking, and mark only the
   completed version. Round 3 here is the case that motivates it.
2. **`chat:E031` needs a different instrument.** Ten drill rounds across four drills
   plus a 12/12 control have not moved it; it is now at 10 hits and missed in every
   round of this drill. Marking it in arrears is not producing transfer. Try
   pre-committing him to checking his own articles before submitting, which tests
   whether self-monitoring can be trained even where externally supplied attention
   cannot be.
3. **Wrong-definiteness is now its own row: `chat:E056`, opened at 3 hits.**
   `a correct API key` (02), `the file` (03), `the pages` (04). It separates from
   `chat:E031` because the mechanism differs: not an omitted function word but a wrong
   judgement about definiteness with the article present. Both rows now review on
   their own schedules, so a future drill can tell which of the two directions is
   moving.
4. **Verify `object` in Drill 05 on a first-pass answer.** Its clean sweep came
   partly from a redo. Build Round 2 around doing a series of things to one named
   thing, the Day 05 shape, and mark the first attempt.
5. **`make sure` + bare noun did not recur.** Flagged in Drill 03 as needing a third
   instance before splitting into its own row. Still at two. Leave it.
6. **Stop reporting the Round 1 regularity.** It held for three drills and broke on
   the fourth.
