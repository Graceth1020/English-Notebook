## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** A dish you can cook from memory - something you've made enough times that you don't need a recipe. Walk me through making it, from taking the ingredients out to plating it. The real sequence, including the step everyone else does differently from you.

**中文：**一道你不用看菜谱就能做的菜。从拿出食材到装盘，按真实顺序讲一遍，包括那一步你和别人做法不一样的地方。

**He wrote:**
> Cole chicken wings is a dish which I get off RedNote. I cook it for many times, so cooking it has become my muscle memory. At first, I should clean the wings with starch water. Then I pour some oil into an iron pot and put it on the gas stove. After heating the oil to a sufficient temperature, the wings are poured into the pot. I fry the wings for three minutes, and pour an appropriate amount of cola into the pot according to the amount of the wings. Finally, cover the pot and cook over medium heat for more than five minutes. You can get a delicious dish.

**tense:** "a dish which I get off RedNote" -> "which I got off RedNote" (finished past event in present tense; the E049 family, logged as a hit)

**article:** ok

**form:** "After heating the oil to a sufficient temperature, the wings are poured into the pot" -> "After heating the oil, pour the wings in" / "Once the oil is hot, the wings go in" (the -ing clause's implied subject is the cook, but the main clause's subject is the wings; dangling participle, the `chat:E011` family)

**object:** ok

**prep:** "I cook it for many times" -> "many times" (occurrence counts take no preposition; second instance after Drill 06's "over one time")

**Note:** Not counted, three cross-track positives in one round: "got off
RedNote" carries the exact `chat:E016` target preposition (wrong tense, right
glue); "cook over medium heat" is correct - the `over` pair that broke as
"over one time" in Drill 06 works in its proper habitat; "muscle memory" is a
well-used chunk. Out of scope: "Cole" for "Cola" (braised cola chicken wings;
the final verb is "braise"); "I should clean the wings" drops the `should`
(应该 transferred into a steps narration); person drifts from I to "You can
get..." at the end.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** Should junior engineers in their first year be *encouraged* to use AI coding assistants, or *restricted* from them? Some say it accelerates learning; others say it produces developers who can ship code but can't debug it. Pick one side - actually pick - defend it, then give the other side's strongest argument and your rebuttal.

**中文：**初级工程师头一年应该被鼓励用 AI 编程助手，还是应该被限制？必须选一边并辩护，然后给出对方最强论点和你的反驳。

**He wrote:**
> I encourage the junior engineers to use AI coding assistants, which not only helps us find out solutions accurately, but also reviews our code effectively whenever we finish a feature. I'm not agree with the idea that AI produces developers who can ship it but can't debug it. Before LLMs were released, I found out how to deal with a problem on websites. The thing is, many blogs published on the internet were providing wrong solutions which reduced the efficiency to solve problems. Now we have LLMs, and we can find out anything quickly whenever we need. This helps us learn things efficiently.

**tense:** ok

**article:** ok

**form:** "I'm not agree with the idea" -> "I don't agree with the idea" (`agree` is a verb and negates with `do`; `I'm not` goes before adjectives - "I'm not sure")

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted. The round's structural win: a past era ("Before LLMs
were released... blogs were providing") switched to present on the named
anchor "Now" - exactly the frame management that has been failing, done
right. Cross-track: "The thing is" is the `chat:E034` target phrase,
unprompted; "can ship it but can't debug it" both carry objects. Out of
scope: "find out solutions" -> "find solutions" (`find out` discovers facts,
`find` obtains things); "the junior engineers" for a general policy should
be bare plural. Content: the rebuttal answers answer-quality (blogs were
worse) instead of the actual objection (skill atrophy); the engaging
rebuttal is reps - AI gives juniors more attempts per week.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** Tell the story of one technical decision you made that turned out to be wrong. Cover it in order: what you decided and when, why it seemed right at the time, when and how you realized it wasn't, what it cost, and - finishing the story - what you would do differently if you were facing the same decision today.

**中文：**讲一个你做过、后来发现是错的技术决策：什么时候决定的、当时为什么觉得合理、什么时候怎么发现不对劲、代价是什么，最后说如果今天重新面对同样的决定你会怎么做。

**He wrote:**
> When I graduated from university, I developed a personal website. I finally decided to use SpringMVC as the base framework. At that time, SpringMVC was very popular and many projects were using it to develop. But after I finished my first version, I found the MVC model was hard to maintain. I had to develop each service for a URL, and this was a very troublesome matter. If I had had one more choice, I would have picked up a gateway to build my feature.

**tense:** ok

**article:** ok

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted - and this is the round of the drill. The closing
counterfactual "If I had had one more choice, I would have picked up a
gateway" is the `chat:E037` family's hardest shape - past perfect in the
if-clause, would-have + participle in the main clause - built correctly at
maximum load, after missing in Drills 02, 04 and 06. "had had" is the
correctly formed past perfect of `have`. The chronology holds the past frame
throughout, including the "At that time" anchor. Out of scope: "picked up"
for `pick` (same slip as Drill 04); "one more choice" is a calque for "if I
had it to do over"; "a troublesome matter" is 事情 transferred - natives say
"a pain". Content: a gateway sits in front of a framework rather than
replacing it, so the fix does not map to the stated pain (per-URL services);
the counterfactual that follows from his own story is a framework with
routing, or a static site.

**5. Occurrence-count glue is now two instances.** `over one time` (Drill 06),

negates with `do`. One instance; watch alongside the E063 cluster - if it
