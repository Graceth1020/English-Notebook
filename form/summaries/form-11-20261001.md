# Form Drill 11 - action-chain narration, learner-designed variant

Date: 2026-10-01 | Axes: tense, article, form, object, prep | Rounds: 3 | Clean: 12/15

## Why This Was Run

Requested by the learner with a specific target: narrating physical action chains
(take the rice out of the fridge, put it in a bowl, microwave it) - the genre where
four of the five axes fire at once. `object` and `prep` are stressed by every
verb + it + preposition slot, `tense` by the frame that has to hold across a whole
sequence, `article` by every container that gets introduced.

**Variant format, and the results are not strictly comparable to Drills 01-10.**
The learner redesigned the drill mid-run: instead of free prompts, each round
supplies a scenario plus a chunk bank (boil water, pour, fill up, screw the lid
on), and he organizes the language. Difficulty climbs by object count - one main
object, two containers, a bench full of small things - which stresses pronoun
switching instead of question complexity. The axes were visible (the chunk bank
itself taught the shapes), so this measures assembly with support, closer to the
pattern drills than to Drills 01-10. Recorded honestly; the format is his to
design, as Day 11 established.

## Round 1 - easy (one main object)

**Prompt:** Walk me through your morning hot-water routine, step by step - from the moment you decide you want the water to the moment you leave home with it. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把早上烧水、倒水、装瓶、出门的完整流程按顺序讲一遍，每个动作都写到。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> When I wake up in the morning, I've got to drink a bottle of water. I plug in the kettle and switch it on to boil water. When it comes to boil, I fill up my water bottle and drink it. Then I continue to pour water into my bottle. After filling it up again, I screw the lid on tight. Finally, I grab it on my way out the door.

**tense:** ok
**article:** "When it comes to boil" -> "When it comes to a boil" - the fixed phrase keeps its article
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** ok

**Note:** the `it`-tracking the drill was built around held completely, including the switch from water back to bottle in "fill it up again". Not counted - "I fill up my water bottle and drink it" reads as drinking the bottle (`it` points at the nearest object); `drink some` fixes the reference. Word choice, chat territory. The kettle/bottle noun split from the chunk bank was used correctly throughout.

## Round 2 - medium (two containers)

**Prompt:** Walk me through heating up your leftover fried rice, step by step - from opening the fridge to eating it. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把加热剩炒饭的完整流程讲一遍，从开冰箱到开吃。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> I take the leftover fried rice out of the fridge and put it in the bowl. Then I cover it with a lid and microwave it for three minutes. I take it out halfway and give it a stir. After heating it up, I should be careful while the bowl is hot. I put the bowl on the table and let it sit for a minute. Then I dig in. What a delicious dish!

**tense:** ok
**article:** "put it in the bowl" -> "put it in a bowl" - first mention of the bowl is indefinite
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** ok

**Note:** not counted - pronoun switching held: `it` tracks the rice for four sentences, then correctly switches to `the bowl` once the bowl is the hot object, and "let it sit" points at the bowl without ambiguity. "After heating it up" repeats what the microwave step already said (logic, chat).

## Round 3 - hard (a bench full of small things)

**Prompt:** Walk me through making your afternoon cup of tea with milk, step by step - from deciding you want one to taking the first sip. Every action, in order. At least five sentences.

**中文：**把泡一杯加奶红茶的完整流程讲一遍，从想喝到喝第一口。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> Whenever I want to have a cup of tea, I've got to put the kettle on. After heating it up, I warm the cup with hot water. Then I put a tea bag in the cup, pour the water over it and let it steep for three minutes. Later I add a splash of milk and give it a stir. A delicious cup of tea with milk is done.

**tense:** ok
**article:** "I warm the cup with hot water" -> "I warm a cup" - first mention again; the listener has not met the cup yet
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** ok

**Note:** not counted - "After heating it up" is the Round 2 redundancy repeated, and the `it` wobbles between kettle and water. The tea bag never comes out; "...is done" is a calque ending. All chat territory.

## What It Showed

**One miss per round, all on `article` - but the subtype split is the finding.**
Round 1 was a missing `a` inside a fixed phrase (`comes to a boil`). Rounds 2 and
3 were the same subtype twice: **first-mention definite article** - `the bowl`,
`the cup` for objects the listener has not met. The mechanism is visible: the
object is definite in the narrator's head because he can see it, so `the` comes
out; the listener's perspective - first mention gets `a` - is not being checked.
Two rounds running is a pattern, not a slip, and it is the E056 direction
(article present but wrong), not the E031 bare-noun signature. That continues
the shift Drill 06 first noticed: the bare-determiner gap has gone quiet while
wrong-choice articles surface instead.

**`object` and `prep` were flawless across 20+ action verbs** - every `it` landed,
every phrasal-verb particle and preposition was glued on. Caveat: the chunk bank
supplied the glue, so this run cannot tell supported assembly from independent
production. The test for that is a later run without the bank.

**`tense` held three frames** (habitual present x2, whenever-habitual) with zero
drift - welcome after Drill 06's 3/3, though habitual present is the gentlest
frame there is.

**"After heating it up" appeared in two rounds** - a redundant connector phrase
used as a crutch between stages. Not an axis, but if it appears a third time it
is a habit worth naming in chat.

## Carry Forward

1. The first-mention-`the` subtype is the live article gap now. If it appears in
   chat, log it against E056. The rule to carry: *first mention gets `a`, every
   mention after gets `the`* - and the check is the listener's knowledge, not
   the narrator's.
2. Repeat this drill format in a few weeks **without the chunk bank** - same
   scenarios, no support. That is the only way to tell whether `object`/`prep`
   were owned or borrowed.
3. The format itself works and is his: scenario + chunk bank + object-count
   climb. Keep it as the designated practice for action-chain narration; the
   scene verb banks (kitchen done; morning routine done; commute/gym TODO) can
   grow as chunks.
