# Form Drill 03 - the first run with `object`, and short answers as a confound

Date: 2026-09-06 | Axes: tense, article, form, object | Rounds: 3 | Clean: 8/12

## Why This Was Run

First drill since `object` became the fourth axis. `chat:E055` (object dropped
after a transitive verb) had opened at 7 hits across Days 04, 05 and 11, found by
scanning all 255 correction rows rather than by noticing it live - so the axis had
never actually been tested in a drill.

Prompts were built around doing a series of things to something already named,
because Day 05 was one session about a single ticket and produced three object
drops in a row. That is the shape that seems to trigger it.

Also carried forward: `chat:E031` (articles, 8 hits), `chat:E037` (tense in
hypotheticals) and `chat:E053` (verb form selection).

## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** A colleague sends you a PR to review. Talk me through your process - what you look at first, what you focus on, and when you send it back for changes.

**中文：**讲讲你 review 同事代码的流程——先做什么，看什么，什么情况下要求返工。

**He wrote:**
> When I get a new review, I'll check the file list to see what it changes. Then I'll check the core logic and the important configuration files. If the change touchs something unrelated, I'll check whether this change is needed. Also, the tests are Indispensable.

**tense:** ok

**article:** ok

**form:** ok

**object:** 1 miss - "to see what it changes" leaves the transitive verb without an object. Either go intransitive with "to see what's changed", or supply one with "to see which files it changes". This is `chat:E055` appearing in the most relaxed round of the drill.

**Note:** Not counted. "touchs" is a spelling slip for "touches". "Indispensable" is written register - speech says "tests are a must". And "a new review" is the wrong noun: what arrives is a PR, the review is what he does to it.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** Your team is deciding on a rule: any PR over 500 lines must be split before it can be merged. Someone objects that some changes are naturally large - renaming an interface used everywhere, for instance, where splitting is more dangerous because the intermediate states do not compile. Take a position and say how your rule would actually work.

**中文：**对"PR 超过 500 行必须拆分"这条规矩表态，并回应"有些改动天然就大、拆开更危险"的反驳。

**He wrote:**
> Whether to split the PR depends on how big the change is. If the file is important and highly affected, we need not split it. The more complex the logic of this file, the greater the risk we will have to modify it.

**tense:** ok

**article:** 1 miss - "If the file is important" introduces a hypothetical file not previously mentioned, so it needs "a file". Same axis as `chat:E031` but the opposite direction: not a missing article, a wrong one. Second instance of this direction after "a correct API key" in Drill 02.

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**Note:** Verb shapes were all correct - "need not split" is correct if formal, and the correlative comparative "The more complex ..., the greater ..." is built correctly on both halves. Not counted, but it shaped the round. He was asked to take a side and answered "it depends", then contradicted himself: important files should not be split, yet more complex logic means more risk - which is an argument *for* splitting. "need not" is also written register. The answer came in at three short sentences, which is the minimum, and short answers give the axes less to fail in.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** The 500-line rule has been in place three months. Explain the whole thing to a new joiner who knows none of the background: why it was introduced, what the objections were at the time, how it was rolled out, how it has gone over three months, and what problems remain.

**中文：**给新人按时间顺序讲清楚这条 500 行规矩：起因、当初的反对声音、怎么落地的、三个月的效果、现在还存在的问题。

**He wrote:**
> We've set a rule three months ago that a merge is forbidden whenever a PR changes over 500 lines. We set it to make sure the quality of review. If a PR contains a lot, we might lose our attention to the key files. During the execution, we've found this rule puts pressure on the engineers because one development sometimes need to be merged many times.

**tense:** 1 miss - "We've set a rule three months ago" pairs the present perfect with a definite past time, which English does not allow: "We set a rule three months ago". Same for "During the execution, we've found" - either "we found" or drop the time phrase and use "we've since found". This is the Day 10 "I've checked the log" error again.

**article:** ok

**form:** 1 miss - "one development sometimes need to be merged" needs "needs"; the subject is third-person singular.

**object:** 1 miss - "to make sure the quality of review" gives `make sure` a bare noun phrase where it needs a clause: "to make sure review quality stays high". Second occurrence of this exact shape after "make sure complatibility" on Day 05 (`chat:E025`).

**Note:** Not counted. The prompt asked for five or six sentences covering five time points; he wrote four sentences covering three, omitting the objections and the three-month outcome entirely. "lose our attention to" should be "lose sight of".

## What It Showed

**1. The new axis earned its place immediately.**

`object` missed in two of three rounds, tying `tense` and `form` for the most
misses in the drill, and one of those was in Round 1 - the easiest, most relaxed
round. Seven corpus occurrences justified adding it; the first drill confirms it is
live rather than historical.

Worth noting the two misses are different shapes. Round 1 is a bare transitive
verb with nothing after it. Round 3 is `make sure` taking a noun phrase where it
needs a clause - which is the same shape as `make sure complatibility` from Day 05,
now twice. The second may deserve its own row if it recurs.

**2. Three drills, three different curves - the retraction holds.**

| Drill | Shape | Misses by round |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | inverted | 1, 3, 0 |
| 02 | humped | 1, 5, 3 |
| 03 | monotonic | 1, 1, 3 |

Drill 02 retracted the "stakes not difficulty" theory read off Drill 01. Drill 03
confirms the retraction rather than replacing it with a new story: difficulty does
not predict accuracy in any stable way across drills. The only consistent thing is
that Round 1 has produced exactly one miss every single time.

**3. Short answers are a measurement problem, not just a content problem.**

Rounds 2 and 3 both came in under the requested length - three sentences where the
brief said three to four, and four sentences where it said five to six with five
specified time points. Round 3 skipped two of the five outright.

This matters for the data, not just the answer quality. Fewer clauses means fewer
opportunities for each axis to fail, so a short round understates the error rate
while looking like a normal result in the matrix. Round 2 shows it plainly: three
sentences, and one of them still carried an article error.

There is also a content-side cost that this track is explicitly not supposed to
mark but cannot ignore: Round 2 asked for a position and got "it depends", followed
by two sentences that argued against each other. When the thinking is unresolved,
the sentences get short and hedged, and short hedged sentences are the ones where
form errors have nowhere to appear.

## Carry Forward

1. **Enforce the length before marking.** A round that comes in under the stated
   sentence count gets handed back to be completed, and only then marked. Drill 03
   has two rounds of discounted data because this was not done.
2. **`object` stays.** Two misses in its first outing, one of them in the easiest
   round. Do not fold it into `form`.
3. **Watch `make sure` + bare noun specifically.** Two occurrences now (Day 05,
   Drill 03). If it appears a third time it is a distinct row from `chat:E055`,
   because the mechanism is different - not a dropped object but a complement type
   the verb cannot take.
4. **Stop looking for a degradation curve.** Three drills, three shapes. Report the
   misses; do not explain them. The one durable observation is that Round 1 always
   produces exactly one miss, which is itself evidence against the idea that low
   stakes are protective.
5. **Wrong-article is now its own sub-pattern.** `a correct API key` (Drill 02) and
   `the file` for `a file` (Drill 03) are both article-present-but-wrong, which is
   a different failure from `chat:E031`'s missing article. Two instances; watch for
   a third before splitting the row.
