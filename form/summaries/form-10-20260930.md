# Form Drill 10 - the self-check variant finally runs, and the result is partial

Date: 2026-09-30 | Axes: tense, article, form, object, prep | Rounds: 3 + self-check | Clean: 9/15

## Why This Was Run

A clean measurement drill one day after Drill 09's best-ever score, with no
dedicated round owed to any axis. It became the most important drill of the track
so far for a reason nobody planned: two article misses in the first two rounds
armed the self-check variant, which had been reserved since Drill 04 waiting for
exactly this material. It ran as Part 4 - its first execution.

## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** Describe your actual desk as it is right now - what's on it, where things sit, what's always there, and what shouldn't be there. Not the ideal setup; the real one you're looking at.

**中文：**描述一下你此刻真实的桌面——上面有什么、东西都摆在哪、什么是常驻的、什么其实不该在那儿。不要理想版，就讲你现在看到的。

**He wrote:**
> I have two laptops sitting on my desk. One is used to working, and another one is my personal laptop. A computer monitor connecting to the working laptop is behind the laptop. Honestly, my personal laptop shouldn't be here. Since I can't visit internet on my working laptop, I have to use my personal laptop to search something.

**tense:** ok

**article:** "I can't visit internet" -> "the internet" (bare determiner, `chat:E031`; and "visit the internet" itself is a calque of 上网 - natives "get online" / "access the internet")

**form:** "One is used to working" -> "One is used for work" (`be used to + -ing` = accustomed; purpose is `be used for`); "a monitor connecting to the working laptop" -> "connected to" (passive participle; the monitor does not connect itself)

**object:** ok

**prep:** "to search something" -> "to search for something" (`search` + object examines a place; searching *for* a thing takes `for`; the `wait for` / `listen to` family)

**Note:** Not counted, two cross-track positives: "two laptops sitting on my
desk" is the `chat:E004` target, unprompted; "Honestly" is a natural discourse
opener. Out of scope: "the working laptop" -> "the work laptop" (`working`
describes what it is doing, not what it is for).

**His revision (self-initiated, before Round 2):** all four marked errors fixed,
plus the not-counted "work laptop" note - and one survivor, "another one" for
"the other one" with exactly two items, which had never been flagged. Second
formal instance of the pattern: flagged errors fixed at 100%, unflagged not
found.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** As an engineer's career grows, is it better to be a *specialist* - deep in one stack, one domain - or a *generalist* who can work across many? Pick one - actually pick - defend it, then give the other side's strongest argument and your rebuttal.

**中文：**工程师的职业发展，做专精一个技术栈一个领域的 specialist 好，还是做跨多领域的 generalist 好？必须选一边，辩护，然后给出对方最强论点和你的反驳。

**He wrote:**
> It's better to be a generalist, I'd say. You say a specialist can be in demand in the job markets. I don't agree with it. This is an AI era. No matter how difficult the problem is, an LLm can resolve it. A generalist who knows a lot can better match the current business market.

**tense:** ok

**article:** "in the job markets" -> "in the job market" (the labour market as a whole is singular; plural means multiple national or regional markets)

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted, three items. "I don't agree" is Drill 09's "I'm not
agree" corrected inside 24 hours. "No matter how difficult the problem is" is
`P005` - the concession pattern drilled on 09-08 - produced perfectly under
load, first unprompted use in this track. "an AI era" and "an LLM" are both
vowel-rule cases, correct. Content: the rebuttal engages for once (AI shrinks
specialist scarcity), though "an LLM can resolve any problem" overclaims.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** A relative who has never programmed - an aunt, say - asks you at a family dinner: "So what do you actually do all day at work?" Explain your job to her: what the system you work on does for its users, and what your own part in it is. Every technical word you use, you have to explain in plain language as you go.

**中文：**家庭聚会上，完全不懂编程的长辈问你："你上班到底每天都在干嘛？"用她能听懂的话解释你的工作：你做的系统对用户是干什么的，你自己在其中负责什么。用到的每个技术词都要顺手用大白话解释。

**He wrote:**
> I'm a software engineer. My main job is developing applications like APP, website. I'm responsible for the features of Mobile Bank APP. Every day I add new features to the APP. For example, recently I built a coupon feature for our pay module. After paying for a order, customers can get coupons. I designed the backend logic and the database schema. Whenever an order is paid, the system saved the order information.

**tense:** "Whenever an order is paid, the system saved the order information" -> "the system saves the order information" (general rule with `whenever` needs the present frame; second instance of this exact subtype after D05's "whenever a review request had been received")

**article:** "applications like APP, website" -> "like apps and websites" (bare singular countables; examples go plural); "paying for a order" -> "an order" (vowel-sound rule - produced correctly twice in Round 2 the same day)

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted. "After paying for an order, customers can get coupons"
keeps the -ing clause subject-aligned, the thing that broke in Drill 09 R1.
"database schema" correct after "table scheme" twice in Drill 05. The audience
constraint failed: "backend logic", "database schema", "pay module" all
unexplained, so the aunt is lost even though the facts are right - the
plain-language restructuring this round was built to elicit did not happen.

## Part 4 - The Self-Check Variant (first run)

**Setup, and a necessary modification.** The variant spec hands back all three
answers unmarked and says only "determiners". Immediate per-round marking had
already revealed Round 1 and 2's article misses verbatim, so those two were
contaminated by recall. The clean measurement was therefore: everything *else*
he could find - in practice, Round 3's two article errors, which he had not
seen marked. R3's article line was withheld until after the check.

**What he found:**

| His find | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| R1 "visit the internet" | already marked - recall, not a find |
| R2 "in the job market" | already marked - recall |
| R3 "for an order" | caught - one of two unmarked errors |
| R3 "of the Mobile Bank APP" | not an error; bare product name was fine, the addition defensible - neutral |
| R3 "like APP, website" | missed - the other unmarked error |

**Catch rate on unmarked material: 1/2, zero harmful false positives.**

**The reading, and it is more informative than the rate.** He caught the
*phonetic* determiner error - `a` vs `an`, the rule Control 01 proved he knows
cold - and missed the *structural* one: bare singular countables, the
`chat:E031` core signature itself. The error that has resisted four drills and
24 chat hits is also the one his own monitoring does not see. `chat:E031` is
not merely weak under production pressure; it is partially invisible to his
second pass as well. Per the variant's outcome table this is the "some"
branch: partial, worth repeating to see whether the rate climbs. The practical
consequence: a submit-time scan cannot be "check your articles" - it has to be
concrete ("find every noun, ask what is in front of it"), and even then the
structural subtype should be expected to transfer last.

## What It Showed

**1. The self-check variant works as an instrument, and its first result
refines the `chat:E031` problem.** Partial catch (1/2 unmarked), with the catch
on the phonetic rule and the miss on the structural one. Combined with the two
revision data points (Drill 05, and Round 1 today: flagged errors fixed at
100%, unflagged at ~0%), the picture is now consistent across three
measurements: his monitoring operates on *flagged or explicitly known* items
and does not sweep for the class.

**2. `whenever` general-rule frame is now two exact instances.** D05 R2
("whenever a review request had been received") and today ("whenever an order
is paid... saved"). The wider family (frame breaks at passage boundaries) has
more, but this subtype is specific: a `whenever` rule stated in present with
one past verb inside it. Third instance earns a row.

**3. Cross-track retentions are accumulating.** This drill alone: `chat:E004`
("sitting on my desk"), `chat:E034` ("The thing is" in Drill 09, revision
quality today), `P005` ("No matter how difficult the problem is" - first
unprompted use, ten days after the pattern drill), "database schema" (Drill
05's "scheme"), and "I don't agree" (Drill 09's "I'm not agree"). The
24-hour survival failures of earlier drills are slowly being replaced by
24-hour-plus survivals.

**4. `prep` keeps finding the `for` family.** "search for" today, after
"travel to" and "integrate into" (Drill 09) and "scroll through" (Drill 08) -
still no repeats of logged pairs, still no drill action warranted.

## Carry Forward

   structural subtype ever gets caught.
2. **The concrete scan instruction goes to the learner as advice:** "find every

3. **`whenever`-frame subtype at two instances;** third earns a row.
4. **`the other one` vs `another one` (exactly two items)** - one instance,

5. **Article number judgement** ("the job markets" for "the job market") is a
   third article subtype in the matrices after bare-determiner and wrong-
   definiteness. One instance; watch before it earns a row.
