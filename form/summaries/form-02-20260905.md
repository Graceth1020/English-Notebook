# Form Drill 02 - a timeline, and the article gap that ignores difficulty

Date: 2026-09-05 | Axes: tense, article, form | Rounds: 3 | Clean: 3/9

## Why This Was Run

Form Drill 01 produced an inverted curve - the hardest round clean, the easiest one
carrying the error - and that was read as evidence that a drop in perceived stakes,
rather than content difficulty, is what costs accuracy. Three rounds is not enough to
support a claim like that, so Drill 02 was partly a replication: an easy, low-stakes
round 1 to see whether the slip reproduced.

It did not reproduce the way it was predicted to, and the drill found something else.

`chat:E031` (singular countable noun with no article) stood at 5 hits across 5
consecutive chat sessions and had already survived a conventional 12/12 drill.
`chat:E037` (tense in hypotheticals) and `chat:E053` (verb form selection) were the
other two axes carried over unchanged.

**Round 3 was withdrawn and reissued mid-drill.** See `What It Showed`.

## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** Your gym routine. Walk me through how it actually came about - when you started, what you tried first that didn't stick, and what the routine looks like these days.

**中文：**讲讲你的健身习惯是怎么形成的：什么时候开始的，一开始试过什么但没坚持下来，现在的 routine 是什么样。

**He wrote:**
> I always went to gym within a year after graduating from university. But now I have other exercise habits like playing basketball and running. Since there is no gym near my home, I don't go to the gym.

**tense:** ok

**article:** 1 miss - "went to gym" should be "went to the gym". Singular countable noun, `chat:E031`. But see the note: this is the most informative single miss in the track so far.

**form:** ok

**Note:** Verb shapes were all correct - "after graduating", "habits like playing basketball and running". Out of scope and not counted: "within a year after graduating" reads as *before a year had passed*, where the duration sense is "for about a year after I graduated"; and "I have other exercise habits" is noun-heavy where speech would say "I've got into other things". Neither is a form error.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** Your company is scrapping the wellness benefit - the gym subsidy, the annual health check, all of it - and putting that money into base salary instead. Most of your team is quietly pleased. You are the one asked to make the case against it in a team meeting, to a room that already disagrees with you.

**中文：**公司要取消健身/体检福利，把钱折进基本工资。你要在会上论证公司这么做是个错误。

**He wrote:**
> I think this a bad decision. If the company keep the benefit, we'll go to gyms and do annual health check because they are one part of our salary. If that money is put into base salary instead, some people would not do these healthy things regularly. Health is even more important than money.

**tense:** 1 miss - two parallel conditionals with mismatched modals. "If the company keep ... we'll go" then "If that money is put ... some people would not". Both if-clauses are present, so both main clauses need "'ll"/"won't". Mixing in "would" makes the second scenario sound less real than the first, which undercuts the argument, since that is the outcome being warned about. Same family as `chat:E037`.

**article:** 2 misses - "do annual health check" should be "do the annual health check" (singular countable, `chat:E031`). And "they are one part of our salary" should be "part of our salary"; "one part" means one component among several enumerated ones.

**form:** 2 misses - "I think this a bad decision" drops the copula, needs "this is a bad decision". And "If the company keep" needs "keeps"; `the company` is third-person singular, and subject-verb agreement is a verb-shape choice.

**Note:** Not counted. "go to gyms" is grammatical but English treats it as a fixed singular, "go to the gym", even when speaking generally. Collocation rather than form.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** Explain the whole SMS provider migration to a new colleague who starts next week and knows none of the background - why you switched, what you did, what broke on Saturday, how it was fixed, and where it stands now. Five or six sentences, in chronological order.

**中文：**给一个没参与过的新人，按时间顺序完整讲一遍这次 SMS 供应商迁移。

**He wrote:**
> We swapped SMS provider out on the 24th of last month. The ticket went out on Saturday afternoon. But an error occurred which caused that no verification went out. After going through the logs, we found that the change touched the SMS sending logic. The problem was the application wasn't picking up a correct API key. A fix went out on that evening. I've written a report about this incident.

**tense:** ok

**article:** 2 misses - "swapped SMS provider out" needs "the SMS provider" (`chat:E031`, third time in this drill). And "a correct API key" should be "the correct API key": the article is present but wrong, since there is exactly one correct key, so it is definite.

**form:** 1 miss - "caused that no verification went out". `cause` does not take a that-clause; it takes object plus infinitive. Either "meant no verification codes went out" or "caused no verification codes to go out".

**Note:** The tense result is the finding of this drill: six sentences across five distinct time points, no drift anywhere, then a correct switch to present perfect for "I've written a report" because the report exists now. That is the exact distinction he got wrong on Day 10 with "I've checked the log", made correctly here in the round built to stress it. Not counted: "on that evening" should be "that evening" - English drops the preposition before that/last/next. That is preposition, not one of the three axes. Also worth recording positively though outside the axes: "the SMS sending logic" and "wasn't picking up" are both Day 10 corrections, reproduced unprompted a day later.

## What It Showed

**1. The Drill 01 curve finding was wrong, and was retracted.**

Drill 01 was inverted. Drill 02 is humped - 1 miss, then 5, then 3, peaking in the
middle. Two drills, two different shapes, so neither shape is a property of the
learner. The "stakes, not difficulty" theory was over-read from three data points and
should not have been presented to him as a finding. Difficulty does not predict his
accuracy in any consistent way, and this track should stop generating narratives from
single drills.

**2. Articles failed in every round; nothing else did.**

Five of the nine misses were articles, and they were the only axis to miss in all
three rounds. Tense was clean in both rounds where it was not specifically loaded -
and clean *again* in round 3, which was purpose-built to break it across five time
frames. Form missed only where the sentence needed an unusual complement.

**3. Round 1 is the strongest evidence in the track, and it rules out the last
remaining knowledge explanation.**

In one three-sentence answer he produced the same noun three times:

| | | Article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | went to __ gym | dropped |
| 2 | there is no gym near my home | correct - bare after `no` |
| 3 | I don't go to the gym | correct |

Two correct out of three, including the harder case where `no` makes the bare form
right, and one dropped - within thirty seconds, on the same word. A 12/12 drill could
be dismissed as recognition. This cannot: nobody lacks knowledge of a rule they apply
correctly twice in the same breath.

So the article gap is not degrading under load. It drops at roughly a constant rate
regardless of round, difficulty, or what else the sentence is doing. That is a
different problem from the one this track was designed for, and the honest conclusion
is that **the form-drill instrument probably cannot fix it either** - it was built for
errors that appear under content pressure, and this one is not sensitive to content
pressure at all.

**4. The round 3 design was wrong and was changed mid-drill, at the learner's request.**

The original round 3 was an adversarial challenge: a colleague pointing out two holes
in his round 2 argument and demanding a finance answer. He stopped and said the
question was too hard to answer, and that the drill was supposed to be about speaking
English rather than about handling that kind of question.

He was right, and round 2 is the proof: it produced five misses while he was merely
stating a position, not defending one. The load that matters comes from the content
being complex, not from having to win an argument. An adversarial round makes him
solve a business problem first and produce English second, and when the first step
fails there is no data - the answer comes out short and cautious, which is exactly
when form errors hide.

That design was inherited from the daily chat's pushback style, where holding a
position under challenge is the actual goal. It does not belong here. `form-drills.md`
has been updated.

## Carry Forward

1. **Stop reporting a degradation curve after one drill.** Two drills have now given
   contradictory shapes. Say what the misses were; do not explain them with a theory
   until there are five or six drills to look at.
2. **`chat:E031` needs a different instrument, and it may not exist.** A conventional
   drill scored 12/12. Conversational seeding failed six sessions running. Content-load
   drilling now shows it failing at a flat rate independent of load. What has not been
   tried is a production habit rather than a test - for instance, having him re-read
   his own answer for nouns only before sending it, which trains the checking step
   instead of the rule.
3. **The article miss in round 3 was a wrong article, not a missing one.** `a correct
   API key` for `the correct API key` is the first of its kind in the corpus and is
   worth watching; if it recurs it is a separate row from `chat:E031`.
4. **Round 3 is now a high-information-load task, not an adversarial one.** Chronology,
   noun-dense technical description, or multi-clause comparison. See `form-drills.md`.
5. **Tense may be closer to solved than the error log suggests.** `chat:E037` has now
   survived a purpose-built five-time-point narration. It still missed in round 2 on
   parallel conditionals, so the remaining weakness looks specific to hypotheticals
   rather than to time management in general.
