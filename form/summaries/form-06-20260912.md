# Form Drill 06 - `prep` debuts with two misses, `tense` fails every round

Date: 2026-09-12 | Axes: tense, article, form, object, prep | Rounds: 3 | Clean: 7/15

## Why This Was Run

First drill with the five-axis set. `prep` was added this week on 9 corpus
occurrences across 9 sessions (E016, E029 x3, E039, `similar with`, `work for
tickets`, `spend in commute`, `protect over working hours`, `add for a
module`), meeting the same bar `object` did. Background numbers: `chat:E031` at
16 hits, `chat:E037` open, and a same-week chat correction (`At first` + past
tense, Day 14) available for a 24-hour survival test.

## Round 1 - easy

**Prompt:** You play basketball when you can. Walk me through a typical session - where you play, who you play with, how a game usually goes, and how you feel afterwards. At least four sentences.

**中文：**聊聊你平时打篮球：在哪打、和谁打、一般怎么进行、打完什么感觉。至少四句。

**He wrote:**
> I am really into playing basketball, especially after work. I always play it at the court near my home. At first, I play it myself. And over time, more and more neighbors have found the court. Now we have a Wechat group. Whenever we want to play, we send a message to gather teammates.

**tense:** "At first, I play it myself" -> "At first, I played by myself" - `At first` anchors the past; identical error was corrected in chat the previous day and did not survive 24h of production
**article:** ok
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** "play it myself" -> "play by myself" - `do it oneself` = without help; `by oneself` = alone. His contrast was with neighbours joining later, so alone. He asked mid-drill why; the oneself / by-oneself / on-my-own distinction was explained with the "swap in alone" test.

**Note:** not counted - `over time, more and more neighbors have found the court`. C070 was banked the previous day and is already in use with the correct present perfect. Also `an`-before-vowel was not tested this round.

## Round 2 - medium

**Prompt:** Some companies let engineers pick their own tickets; in others the team lead assigns them. Which works better, and why? Take a side and support it. At least four sentences.

**中文：**有的公司让工程师自己挑工单，有的由组长分配。哪种更好？选一边并给出理由。至少四句。

**He wrote:**
> It's better for the team lead to assign. An engineer can pick his own tickets will cause a real issue. If there is a hard ticket and another one is easy, all engineers have got to pick the easy one. And finally this will create an atmosphere of vicious competition. But the team lead knows whose workload is heavy and whose is light, so he can assign the tickets rationally.

**tense:** "all engineers have got to pick the easy one" -> "everyone will go for the easy one" - `have got to` = forced; he meant a natural tendency, which is `will`. The modal flips the argument
**article:** ok
**form:** "An engineer can pick his own tickets will cause a real issue" -> "Letting engineers pick their own tickets will cause a real issue" - two finite verbs stacked; the subject needs an -ing clause
**object:** "It's better for the team lead to assign" -> "to assign tickets" - dropped object, despite `assign the tickets` appearing correctly one sentence later (E055 family)
**prep:** ok

**Note:** first article-clean round at medium difficulty; drills 04/05 missed articles every round. Not counted - `a real issue` is the Day 13 correction of `a serious problem`, applied. `vicious competition` -> `cut-throat competition`; `assign rationally` -> `balance the load`. Both belong to the daily chat.

## Round 3 - hard

**Prompt:** A user taps "pay" on an order in your app. Walk me through everything that happens from that tap until the payment succeeds - every system the request touches, in order, and what each one does. At least five sentences.

**中文：**用户在你的应用里点了"支付"。从这一下点击到支付成功，中间经过哪些系统、每个系统做什么，按顺序讲一遍。至少五句。

**He wrote:**
> After the "pay" is tapped, the app will send an http request to our backend server with the paying parameters. Then the request goes through our filters, where we check if the request is valid, if the order is expired, that kind of thing. We also have a Redis cache to check if the request is received over one time. At last, the pay handler will deal with the order. It'll set the order's status and write it to the database, then call the bank API to deduct money. After all these is done, the server changes the order's status and sends a successful response to the client.

**tense:** "will send ... goes through ... will deal ... changes" -> pick one frame for a general process - natives narrate a process in present throughout: the app sends, the request goes through, the handler deals with it
**article:** "After all these is done" -> "After all this is done" - this/these number agreement. Not the E031 bare-determiner signature; see What It Showed
**form:** ok
**object:** ok
**prep:** "received over one time" -> "received more than once" - `over` takes quantities (over 100 people), not occurrence counts; and `once`, not `one time`

**Note:** not counted - `an http request` correct on the /eItS/ vowel rule, the Drill 01 trap item. `the paying parameters` -> `the payment parameters` is word choice, belongs to chat.

## What It Showed

**`tense` is now the weakest axis - three misses in three rounds, three
different subtypes.** `At first` + present (a chat correction from the previous
day, not surviving 24h of production), `have got to` for `will` inside a
hypothetical (the E037 family), and will/present drift in process narration. No
single rule covers all three, which suggests the problem is frame selection
under load, not a specific gap.

**`article` may be changing shape.** First drill with no bare-determiner miss -
the E031 signature - in any round. The one miss (`all these is`) is number
agreement, a different subtype. One drill is not a trend; the next two will say
whether the bare-noun gap is actually closing or just had a quiet day. The
self-check variant was not run: with only one article miss and that of a
different type, there was too little to catch, and the variant is reserved
rather than routine.

**`prep` earns its place on debut: misses in two of three rounds.** Both were
glue errors on otherwise correct sentences (`play it myself`, `over one time`),
which is exactly the profile the axis was built for - he knows the facts,
produces them wrong when busy.

**`object` holds.** Two clean rounds of three, consistent with drills 04/05.
The one miss (`to assign` bare) self-corrected a sentence later.

Difficulty curve: 3/5, 2/5, 2/5 - roughly flat with slight degradation. Per the
standing rule, the numbers are reported, not explained: three rounds do not
support a curve reading.

## Carry Forward

1. `tense` now has the worst record of any axis (3/3). It has an error row
   (E037, 3 hits) but no dedicated instrument. If the next drill repeats a
   3/3, design a tense-stressing round deliberately: narration across named
   time points, the Drill 02 format that once produced a clean run - a repeat
   would show whether that clean run was real.
2. Watch whether the `article` miss subtype shift (bare determiner -> number
   agreement) persists. If E031 goes quiet in chat as well, that is the first
   evidence anything has moved it.
3. `prep` stays marked. The interesting question is whether its misses
   cluster on the same pairs the corpus already logged (`reply to`, `work
   on`, `add to`) or find new ones.
