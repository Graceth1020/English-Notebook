## Round 1 - Simple

**Prompt:** Your first thirty minutes at work. Walk me through the actual sequence: what you open first, what you check, what you deliberately skip, and in what order. Not the ideal version - what you really do.

**中文：**上班头三十分钟你实际做的事：先打开什么、看什么、刻意不碰什么，按顺序讲。不要理想版，讲真实版。

**He wrote:**
> When I arrived at my workplace at 8:45, I eat breakfast. While having breakfast, I browse international news at the same time. Then I put down my phone. Entertainment apps get me distracted so I set the phone to the focus mode. At 9.00, I get in the zone. I reply to the emails which I left yesterday. Then I check the to-do list and handle things according to their priority.

**tense:** "When I arrived at my workplace at 8:45, I eat breakfast" -> "When I arrive at 8:45, I eat breakfast" (one past verb breaking a present habitual frame; "emails which I left yesterday" is correctly past inside the frame - a genuinely past event)

**article:** ok

**form:** ok

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted, two cross-track positives. "reply to the emails" is the exact
target form of `chat:E029` (reply + missing to, 3 hits), produced unprompted in a
busy sequence. Also correct: "arrive at", "according to", "in the zone". Out of
scope: "the focus mode" should be bare "focus mode" like "dark mode"; "my workplace"
is a calque for "the office". Six sentences against a 3-4 brief - over-length does
not hide errors, so no hand-back.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** Your company offers a choice: the classic five eight-hour days, or four ten-hour days with a three-day weekend, same total hours. Pick one - actually pick - and defend it. Then name the strongest argument for the side you didn't pick, and say why it doesn't win you over.

**中文：**公司让你二选一：传统的五天八小时，或者每天十小时、每周四天、三天周末，总工时一样。必须选一个并辩护，然后说出对方阵营最强的一个论点，并解释为什么它说服不了你。

**He wrote:**
> I would prefer the four ten-hour one because a long weekend means I can go to travel other cities in my province. A pleasure tour help me unwind after work. You say that the classic one makes people more effective since they don't need to work long hours. I disagree with it because a long weekend gives us more choice to relax. As I said before, people can have a nice trip during a three-day weekend.

**tense:** ok

**article:** ok

**form:** "A pleasure tour help me unwind" -> "helps" (third-person singular agreement)

**object:** ok

**prep:** "go to travel other cities" -> "travel to other cities" (travel + destination takes `to`; same family as Day 08's "go to the same direction")

**Note:** Not counted. "I'd prefer" was correctly conditional and the argument held
a present frame throughout - tense's clean round here is part of the verdict. The
rebuttal does not engage the objection: the other side argues effectiveness, the
rebuttal repeats the relaxation point. A real rebuttal concedes the tiredness and
counters with recovery. "A pleasure tour" is a calque; natives say "a weekend trip".

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** Pick a system or project you've been involved with for a long time. Tell its story from the beginning to today, and into the future: how it started and when, what it looked like at first, what the major changes were and when they happened, what state it's in now, and what's planned for it next. Every stage needs its time anchor.

**中文：**挑一个你长期参与的系统或项目，从头讲到尾：什么时候开始的、最初什么样、中间有哪些重大变化、现在什么状态、接下来有什么计划。每个阶段都要带时间标记。

**He wrote:**
> I've developed a chrome plugin since two months ago. At first, it only had a sentence or word translation function. Whenever you click the plugin icon, a message box with an input pop up. You can input your sentence or world and it displays the translation. These days, I've been adding new features which supports the plugin to save words or sentences you like. I will have integrated LLMs to the plugin by the end of this quarter.

**tense:** "I've developed a chrome plugin since two months ago" -> "I've been developing it for two months" / "I started it two months ago" (`since` and `ago` are two anchors glued into one; pick one)

**article:** ok

**form:** "a message box with an input pop up" -> "pops up"; "features which supports" -> "support" (agreement, both directions in one round); "supports the plugin to save" -> "features that let you save" (`support` does not take object + to-infinitive; calque of 支持……做)

**object:** ok

**prep:** "integrated LLMs to the plugin" -> "integrated into" (new pair, not one of the corpus pairs)

**Note:** Not counted, and the round's real result is in this column. Across five
named time points, everything held except the `since`-frame: "At first, it only
had" is the exact Drill 06 Round 1 error, produced correctly this time; "These
days, I've been adding" is the right aspect for ongoing work; and "I will have
integrated LLMs by the end of this quarter" is a correctly built future perfect -
the most advanced tense construction attempted in any drill to date. The tense
column says "miss", but the round as a stress test mostly passed.

## What It Showed

**1. `tense` did not repeat 3/3, and the dedicated round mostly held.**

Two misses of three rounds, but the composition matters. Round 3 was built to the
Drill 02 format - narration across five named time points - and produced one miss
(the `since` + `ago` double anchor) against four correct frames, including a future
perfect with `by` + deadline, the hardest construction attempted in the track.
Drill 06's `At first` + present error was produced correctly. The carry-forward
question - was Drill 02's clean run real - now leans yes. The remaining tense
weakness is frame selection at passage boundaries (Round 1's single past verb in a
present frame), not tense morphology.

**2. First all-clean article drill on record - and the same caution as last time.**

Seven drills, and this is the first with no article miss in any round, one week
after `chat:E031` reached 21 hits in unmarked chat. Drill 06 taught the lesson
already: one quiet drill is a quiet day, not a trend, and chat is where the
verdict lives. If Days 21+ show E031 hits slowing, this drill becomes the first
candidate for when something moved. Until then it is recorded, not celebrated.

**3. The self-check variant stayed reserved - as a null run.**

The variant tests whether he can find his own determiner errors in a second pass.
Today there is nothing to find: all three rounds were determiner-clean, and Rounds
1-2 had already been marked as such in his view, so the exercise would have
degenerated into confirming zeros. It stays reserved for a drill where article
misses exist to be caught.

**4. Third-person agreement earned its own error row.**

`the company keep` (D02 R2), `one development need` (D03 R3), `a pleasure tour
help` (D07 R2), `a message box pop up` and `features which supports` (D07 R3) -
five instances across four drills, in both directions (singular verb on plural
subject and vice versa). Filed under `form` in matrices until now, but the
mechanism is specific and repeatable: number agreement between subject and verb,
lost under load. Logged as `chat:E063` at 5 hits.

**5. `prep` misses are finding new pairs rather than repeating corpus ones.**

Drill 06's question was whether prep misses cluster on the logged pairs
(`reply to`, `work on`, `add to`). Today: `travel to` and `integrate into`, both
new. `reply to` was produced correctly in Round 1. Early signal: the axis is
measuring a general glue problem, not a small set of memorizable exceptions.
