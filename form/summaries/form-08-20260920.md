If I go to play basketball, I drive back home and change into sportswear. Otherwise I walk along the river for three kilometers. After getting back home, I always scroll down short videos or play games to relax.

## Round 2 - Medium

**Prompt:** Some companies offer "unlimited vacation" - no fixed number of days off, take what you need as long as the work gets done. In practice, people at those companies often take *fewer* days than before. Is unlimited vacation a real benefit or a trap? Pick one, defend it, then give the other side's strongest argument and your rebuttal.

**中文：**有些公司推行「无限假期」——不限天数，活干完就行。但实际上员工反而休得更少。无限假期是真福利还是陷阱？选一边，辩护，然后给出对方最强的论点和你的反驳。

**He wrote:**
> In my opinion, it's a trap. If there is no fixed number of days off, we can't make the boundary clear between work and life. Somebody might say companies offer "unlimited vacation" means employees can get more say over their own hours, but assigning workload will be difficult at the same time. And some employees may spend less time doing things in order to get more days off. This causes their output low quality.

**tense:** ok

**article:** ok

**form:** "Somebody might say companies offer "unlimited vacation" means..." -> "companies offering unlimited vacation means" (two finite verbs stacked; the subject of `means` needs an -ing clause, same shape as D06 R2); "This causes their output low quality" -> "causes their output quality to drop" / "leads to low-quality output" (`cause` takes object + to-infinitive, not an adjective complement; same complement error as D02's "caused that no verification went out" - and he built "make the boundary clear" correctly two sentences earlier)

**object:** ok

**prep:** ok

**Note:** Not counted, two cross-track positives. "get more say over their own
hours" is the target phrase of `chat:E037's correction, produced unprompted with
the right preposition; "assigning workload" carries its object, which failed bare
in Drill 06. Content: the rebuttal drifts - he argues employees will slack to bank
days off, but never engages the prompt's actual phenomenon (people take *fewer*
days because nobody wants to be seen taking the most), which is the argument his
position needed.

## Round 3 - Hard

**Prompt:** Compare your current job with your previous one across exactly these four dimensions: the tech stack, how work gets assigned, the pace, and the people. On each dimension, say which side is better and roughly by how much. End with the one dimension you'd transplant from the old job into the current one if you could.

**中文：**从四个维度对比你现在的公司和上一家：技术栈、任务分配方式、节奏、人。每个维度说哪边更好、大概好多少。最后说：如果能从上一家搬一样东西过来，你搬哪个。

**He wrote:**
> Let's begin from the tech stack. Honestly, there isn't a big difference between both companies. Since the business side are similar, the base framework is nearly same. Then it's how the work gets assigned. It's also similar of this dimension. Whenever a new ticket comes, the team lead reads the spec and assigns it based on our workload. As for the work pace, it is slower in my current company because getting a stable system is the company culture. The colleagues on two companies are all kind and professional. Whenever a colleague is in need, the others help him actively. When it comes to the dimension to transparent, that must be the time to get off. In my previous company, I can get off work at 5:00 pm and cook things for dinner at home, but now I have to eat outside.

**tense:** "In my previous company, I can get off work at 5:00 pm and cook things" -> "I could get off work at 5:00 and cook dinner" (a finished past situation narrated in present, with "but now" making the contrast explicit; the E049 family)

**article:** "the base framework is nearly same" -> "nearly the same" (`same` never appears without `the`; determiner slot empty)

**form:** "the business side are similar" -> "is" (agreement, `chat:E063` instance 6); "the dimension to transparent" -> "the dimension of transparency" (adjective where the preposition needs a noun)

**object:** ok

**prep:** "begin from the tech stack" -> "begin with"; "colleagues on two companies" -> "at both companies"; "similar of this dimension" -> "similar on this dimension" (three new pairs)

**Note:** Not counted. "the others help him actively" is a calque - natives "pitch
in". Content slip the axes cannot mark: the transplant dimension is named
"transparent" but the answer describes leaving work at 5pm; dimension and example
do not match, so the closing answer never lands.

## What It Showed

**1. Drill-article and chat-article are now two different behaviors.**

Last six drill rounds: one article miss (`nearly same` for `nearly the same`,
today's Round 3). Same week's chat: `chat:E031` from 16 to 23 hits, including
two the morning after the all-clean Drill 07. The divergence is no longer a
one-drill curiosity - it is the most stable pattern in the track. Whatever is
suppressing the error in drills (a marked setting, familiar format, slower
production) is present every drill and absent every chat. This strengthens the
case for the self-check variant's premise: the fix, if one exists, is a
submit-time habit in unmarked settings, not more drill accuracy.

**2. `chat:E063` (agreement) keeps finding subjects at a distance.**

Instance 6: "the business side are similar" - a singular subject with a modifier
between it and the verb. The cluster hypothesis from Drill 07 (relative clauses)
broadens: it is not relatives specifically, it is *any material between subject
and verb*. `features which supports`, `the business side are`, `a message box
with an input pop up` - in every instance a noun closer to the verb than the
actual subject steals the agreement.

**3. `prep` confirms: a general glue problem, not memorizable exceptions.**

Drill 07 asked whether misses cluster on logged pairs. Two drills later: seven
misses, seven different pairs (`travel to`, `integrate into`, `scroll through`,
`begin with`, `at a company`, `on this dimension`, plus Drill 06's two), while
logged pairs keep coming out right (`reply to` again clean in Round 1). The
practice implication, already given to him: learn frames as whole words, and
scan verbs for their glue before sending. Lists of his past misses would not
have caught a single one of these.

**4. `object` posts a second consecutive all-clean drill.**

Including two verbs that failed bare in earlier drills (`assigning workload`,
`assigns it`). Combined with the chat carrying `chat:E055` at 9 hits, the
clearance from drill duty in W37 continues to look correct.

**5. Round 3 difficulty is real again - but `tense` and `article` missed only
there.**

The comparison task (four named dimensions, parallel structures across nine
sentences) produced four of the drill's five axis misses. Round 1 over-length
(six sentences against 3-4, second drill running) continues; it does not distort
measurement, but it is now a reliable feature of his easy rounds: more material
than asked for, no more errors than a shorter answer would carry.
