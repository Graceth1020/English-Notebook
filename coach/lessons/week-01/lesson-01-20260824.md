# Lesson 01 - Disagree and reframe without saying "no"

**Today's single goal:** when someone's question or claim is built on a premise
you don't accept, push back while sounding collaborative.

This is the highest-value move in your target job. In a design review or an
interview, "No, that's wrong" ends the conversation and costs you the room.
Lisa Su does it four times in five minutes without ever saying no.

<details><summary>中文说明</summary>

今天只练一件事：别人的问题或说法建立在一个你不认同的前提上时，你要既表明不同看法、
又让人觉得你在合作，而不是在对抗。这不是"礼貌客套"，而是外企里保护你专业信誉的核心技能。
中文里直译过来的 "I don't think so" / "This is wrong" 在英语里比你想象的要生硬得多。

## Dialogue

Turn-by-turn practice. Learner replies are verbatim.

### Turn 1 - Code review (composition vs inheritance)

**Coach (senior engineer):** "Hey, quick thought on this — I'd use inheritance here
rather than composition. We've got a clear base class already, so it'd cut down the
boilerplate. Any reason you went the other way?"

**You (attempt 1):** "In my opinion, in Java, composition is more flexible than
inheritance. So, I think it'd be better to use composition here that we can maintain
this class easily."

**Fix:**
- Function (P1): opened with own verdict; the other person's point is never
  acknowledged, so they have to defend it. Lead with their point, then differ.
- Grammar: `here that we can maintain` -> `here, so that we can maintain`.
- Register: `In my opinion` is heavy for a PR comment; `Fair point` / `I'd lean toward`.
- Asked for a concrete win instead of the abstract word "flexible".

**Model:** "Fair point - inheritance would cut the boilerplate if this hierarchy stays
stable. My thinking here was a little different: this provider has to integrate with
several third-party payment APIs, so composition lets us swap one out without touching
the base class."

**You (attempt 2):** *(copied the coach's model sentence back verbatim)*

**Fix:** logged as E007 `copies the prompt verbatim` (hit 2). Reading a good sentence
feels like learning; only rebuilding it is. Copying also removes the signal needed for
correction. Rebuilt with constraints: no reused words, name a real varying thing.

**Model:** *(none - this reply was the coach's own sentence, so there was nothing of the
learner's to repair. The rebuild is attempt 3.)*

**You (attempt 3):** "That's fair, inheritance would be clearer if this hierarchy is
stable. However, my opinion is the payment provider here can be integrated with
different third-party payment Apis. If we prefer inheritance over composition, this
provider will lose its flexibility. If you stick to your thought, I'll happy to accept it."

**Fix:**
- Grammar: `I'll happy to accept it` -> `I'd be happy to` (`will/would` + `be` before
  an adjective). Logged E013.
- Tone (P1, matters more): `If you stick to your thought, I'll happy to accept it`
  surrenders an argument already won. Keep the position standing:
  `Happy to talk it through if you still prefer inheritance.` Logged E014.
  Also `stick to your thought` -> `stick to your guns` / `if you still prefer`.
- Smaller: `my opinion is ... can be integrated` -> `the thing is, this provider has to
  integrate with`; `Apis` -> `APIs`; `However` -> `That said` / `but` in a PR comment.

**Model (learner's own version, minimally repaired):** "That's fair — inheritance would
be clearer if this hierarchy stays stable. The thing is, this provider has to integrate
with several third-party payment APIs, and inheritance would lock us into one shape.
Happy to talk it through if you still prefer it."

**Outcome:** won the exchange. The concrete example (multiple payment APIs) carried it
where "more flexible" did not. Carry forward: **lead with their point, close without
surrendering.**

### Turn 2 - Design meeting (microservices, 4-person team)

**Coach (tech lead):** "I'm thinking microservices for this one. It's how we'd build it
if we were doing it properly — everyone's moving that way, and it'll scale when the
company grows."

**You (attempt 1):** "Microservice architecture is advanced nowadays and it's a right
direction which many large platforms have gone. I am worry about the scale of team rather
than the scale of traffic. We can first design our system by using microservice concepts,
making it easy to migrate to microservice architecture in the future."

**Fix:**
- Led with the coach's point and did not surrender at the end - both carried over from
  Turn 1. Real progress.
- Grammar: `I am worry about` -> `I'm worried about` (`worry` verb vs `worried`
  adjective). Logged E015.
- Countability: `the scale of team` -> `the size of the team` (scale/traffic,
  size/team; `team` needs the article). E008 hit 2.
- Logic (P1): circular proposal - argued against microservices, then proposed
  microservice concepts in order to migrate to microservices. Missing the actual
  alternative: **a modular monolith**. Logged E016.
- Smaller: `advanced nowadays` -> `the default` / `industry standard`;
  `a right direction` -> `the right direction`.

**Model:** "Agreed that microservices are the direction most large platforms have gone.
I'm worried about the size of the team rather than the scale of traffic, though - four
people running eight services would stretch us. I'd keep it a modular monolith with
clean boundaries, so that splitting a service out later is straightforward."

**Coach follow-up:** "If we're using microservice concepts anyway, aren't we just doing
microservices with extra steps? What do we specifically *not* pay for by staying with
one deploy?"

**You (attempt 2):** "I'm sorry for making misunderstanding. I mean we can deign our
system by keeping a modular monolith with clean service boundaries. And each of our four
teammate can be responsible for different modules."

**Fix:**
- Tone (P1): `I'm sorry for making misunderstanding` - apologizing for the other
  person's clarifying question undercuts a position already being won. Use
  `Sorry, I wasn't clear -` or `Let me put it differently.` Logged E017.
  Also `making misunderstanding` -> `causing confusion`.
- Function (P1): answered a different question - was asked what cost is avoided,
  answered who owns which module. Logged E018.
- Grammar: `each of our four teammate` -> `each of the four of us` (`each of` +
  plural; "teammate" also excludes the speaker). Logged E019.
- Typo: `deign` -> `design`.
- Good: "modular monolith" was picked up and used correctly.

**Model:** "Let me put it differently - staying with one deploy means we skip the
network hop between services and the distributed tracing that comes with it. And with
four of us, each person can still own a module inside the monolith."

**You (attempt 3):** "Let me put it differently. There might be two problems we'll meet
if we introduce the microservice architecture. First, also the most important one is
network delay. Every single service calls each other through network. Second, distributed
tracing can also be a problem. The more services we have, the long call chain it will. As
our application iterates, this will be a major issue."

**Fix:**
- Answered the actual question with concrete costs - this is what won the exchange.
- Grammar: `The more services we have, the long call chain it will` ->
  `the more services we add, the longer the call chain gets`. The correlative needs a
  comparative in both halves plus a verb. Logged E020.
- Calque: `problems we'll meet` -> `problems we'd run into` / `hit`. Logged E021.
- Precision: `network delay` -> `network latency`. Logged E022.
- Smaller: `First, also the most important one is` -> `First, and most importantly,`.

**Model (learner's own version, minimally repaired):** "Let me put it differently. There
are two problems we'd run into with microservices. First, and most importantly, network
latency - every service call goes over the network. Second, tracing: the more services we
add, the longer the call chain gets, and that gets painful as the app iterates."

**Outcome:** won the exchange; coach agreed to start with a modular monolith. Carry
forward: **answer the question actually asked, and don't apologize for being asked.**

### Turn 3 - False premise about own work (N+1 query)

**Coach (manager):** "So I looked at the dashboard - the checkout API is averaging 800ms.
That's the database being overloaded again, right? Should we just bump the instance size?"
*(Trap: a wrong solution is already proposed.)*

**You (attempt 1):** "Yes, I've noticed this exceptional metric and found out why this
happened yesterday. At the beginning, I had the same thought with you. But after tracing
the code logic, I found the real issue was in the application layer. The logic of querying
issues has significant issues. Actually, we can solve this problem simply by optimizing
the application layer code."

**Fix:**
- Strong: conceded then redirected, killed the wrong solution implicitly, no apology.
  The target pattern landed.
- Preposition: `the same thought with you` -> `the same thought as you` /
  `I thought the same thing`. `Same` pairs with `as`. Logged E023 (2nd time today).
- Repetition: `The logic of querying issues has significant issues` - `issues` twice with
  two different meanings -> `the query logic itself has a real problem`. Logged E024.
- Function (P1): `solve this problem by optimizing the application layer code` is a
  category, not an actionable change. Name the mechanism and the scope. Logged E025.
- Word choice: `this exceptional metric` -> `that spike` (`exceptional` = outstandingly
  good in English). Logged E026.
- Ambiguity: `found out why this happened yesterday` -> `I looked into it yesterday`.

**Model:** "Yeah, I noticed that spike and looked into it yesterday. I thought the same
thing at first, but once I traced it the root cause was in the application layer - we're
firing one query per order in a loop, so a bigger instance wouldn't help. The fix is to
batch it into a single query, about half a day."

**Coach follow-up:** "So bumping the instance wouldn't have helped at all. What's the
actual fix?"

**You (attempt 2):** "See this code snippets. While the code is executing the notification
message of expired orders, it fires one query per order in a loop. We should optimize the
logic here by using a batch query. Like each batch contains only 500 orders."

**Fix:**
- Good: named the real mechanism (one query per order in a loop) and a concrete batch size.
- Grammar: `this code snippets` -> `this snippet` / `these snippets`; `See this` ->
  `Take a look at`. Logged E027.
- Function: mechanism given, effort estimate still missing - the coach had to ask "how
  long?" separately. Fold it in: `about half a day including tests`. Logged E028;
  E012 hit 2.
- Collocation: `executing the notification message` -> `sends expiry notifications`
  (you *send* a notification, not execute a message).
- Smaller: `optimize the logic by using a batch query` -> `batch it into one query`;
  sentence-initial `Like` -> `in chunks of 500`.

**Model (learner's own version, minimally repaired):** "Take a look at this snippet - when
it sends expiry notifications, it fires one query per order in a loop. We should batch it
into one query, in chunks of 500 - about half a day including tests."

**Outcome:** diagnosis was precise and the false premise was corrected cleanly. Recurring
gap: a fix is stated without the effort estimate, forcing an extra round-trip.
## Consolidation

Three turns, three wins on the target pattern: you led with the other person's point
every time after Turn 1, and you never used *no* / *wrong* / *I don't agree*. The
disagreement move itself is working.

**The one thread across all three turns:** you diagnose precisely, then go vague exactly
where a decision is needed. Turn 1 said "more flexible" instead of naming the varying
thing; Turn 2 answered a different question than the one asked; Turn 3 said "optimize
the application layer" and later gave a batch size but no timeline. In each case the
coach had to ask one more question to get something actionable. At work this reads as
tentative, and it costs you a round-trip every time.

The fix is a habit, not a phrase: **end on the concrete thing** - the mechanism, the
number, the date, the named alternative.

## Recap

### 1. Expressions You Learned

- **`Take a look at this snippet -`** - point someone at code
- **`Let me put it differently.`** - reframe without apologizing
- **`The thing is, ...`** - introduce your real objection
- **`the more services we add, the longer the call chain gets`** - the comparative correlative
- **`problems we'd run into`** (not *meet*)
- **`a modular monolith`** - the actual alternative to microservices
- **`network latency`** (not *delay*); **`that spike`** (not *exceptional metric*)
- **`Happy to talk it through if you still prefer X`** - leave the door open without conceding
- **`about half a day including tests`** - scope attached to a fix

**今天最该带走的一点**：三轮里你的诊断都很准，但在"需要对方做决定"的地方都变模糊了。
第一轮说 "more flexible" 而没说清到底什么会变；第二轮答了另一个问题；第三轮说
"optimize the application layer" 却没给工期。结果每轮我都得再追问一次。

英语职场里这不是"谦虚"，而是显得**没准备好**。习惯要改成：**落在具体的东西上**——
机制、数字、日期、或明确的替代方案。

**两个反复出现的语法点**：
- `same` 只搭 `as`，永远不搭 `with`（I thought the same **as** you）。
- `the + 比较级, the + 比较级` 两半都要比较级，还要有动词
  （the more we add, the longer it **gets**）。技术讨论中极其高频。

**一个心态问题**：别为对方的追问道歉。对方问 "aren't we just doing microservices with
extra steps?" 是正常的澄清，不是你犯了错。你当时正在赢，道歉把优势交回去了。
