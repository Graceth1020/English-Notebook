# Lesson 05 - Holding a position when challenged

**Goal:** state a position, then defend it against pushback without caving or getting rigid.

## Input

Watch **18:04-23:15**. Lisa Su explains why AMD walked away from mobile chips and holds the line.

| # | Line | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "We sized it, we looked at it... and we realized that **yes, that is a good business, but that actually is not a good business for us**." [19:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4GM7_Cf0N4&t=1160s) | Concedes the general point, denies the specific one |
| 2 | "**Fundamentally what we were best at**, what I believed we could be best at, is building the highest performance computing out there." [19:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4GM7_Cf0N4&t=1175s) | Re-anchors on her own criterion |
| 3 | "We had to set the expectation - **look, this is going to take three to five years**." [20:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4GM7_Cf0N4&t=1243s) | Names the cost of her position up front |
| 4 | "**people call it a turnaround, yes, maybe I call it more** you have to see where the future is going." [21:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4GM7_Cf0N4&t=1285s) | Accepts the label, then replaces it |

```text
"yes, X is true"  +  "but not for us, because <your criterion>"
```

Never *no*: **agree with the general claim, then disqualify it for your specific case** - and the
criterion is always yours, not theirs.

## Dialogue

### Turn 1 - Your Postgres decision gets challenged  [channel: spoken]

**Coach (senior engineer):** "I heard we're going with Postgres for the audit logs. Honestly that
feels wrong - audit logs are exactly the write-heavy, schema-flexible workload document stores are
built for. Postgres will bottleneck on write throughput. Why not Mongo?"

**You:** "Audit logs are exactly the write-heavy, schema-flexible workload document stores are
built for. But we also need to consider the joins needed for reporting. The conditions can get
complicated which is hard to query in Mongo. You know, Mongo DB is an non-relational database
which doesn't have strong ability to support mutil-table queries. As the bottleneck on write
throughput problem, we can design a new solution like backup old data regularly to solve."

**Fix:**
- Repeated his whole claim, never restated the decision  ->  concede the true half, close on "Postgres is still the right call"  [wrong: wholesale concession reads as agreement]
- "As the bottleneck on write throughput problem"  ->  "As for the write-throughput bottleneck"  [wrong: `as` = because/while; `as for` = regarding]
- "doesn't have strong ability to support"  ->  "doesn't do multi-table queries well"  [awkward: `have the ability to` = a heavy `can`]

**Model (rebuilt: the conceded premise had to be split, not accepted):** "Agreed on the schema flexibility - but our audit schema is fixed,
so that doesn't buy us anything. And we need joins for reporting, where the conditions can get
complicated: Mongo is non-relational, so it doesn't do multi-table queries well. As for the
write-throughput bottleneck, we're at about 2M rows a day, which Postgres handles fine with
monthly partitions. Postgres is still the right call."

**Native:** slightly off - "design a new solution like backup old data regularly to solve" leaves
`to solve` dangling. Name the mechanism: "we can partition and archive old data".

**Split the claim:** concede only the true half. `Agreed on the schema flexibility - but our
schema is fixed` beats `Yes, that's what document stores are built for`, which hands over a
premise you don't accept.

### Turn 2 - Pushback from authority  [channel: spoken]

**Coach (engineering director):** "I've seen this exact debate three times at previous companies,
and Postgres for audit logs was the wrong answer every time. Volume always grows faster than
anyone predicts. I'd rather take the operational hit now than migrate under pressure in a year."

**You:** "Your pass experience is right, but it's not suited for our system. 2M rows a day is
given according to our current situation. Postgres is still the right call."

**Fix:**
- "it's not suited for our system"  ->  "That's fair, and I'd rather not migrate under pressure either - but our numbers are different"  [awkward: dismisses a director's experience; concede the *concern*, split the *numbers*]
- "2M rows a day is given according to our current situation"  ->  "We're at 2M rows a day, and monthly partitions handle that"  [wrong: two calques - `is given` (给出的) is maths/spec register, and `according to` needs an information source, not a basis for a figure: use `based on`. Grammar is fine; the words are not. It also concedes his point - "our current situation" admits the number will change]
- "pass experience"  ->  "past experience"  [wrong: pass/past]

**Model (minimal repair):** "That's fair, and I'd rather not migrate under pressure either - but
our numbers are different. We're at 2M rows a day, and monthly partitions handle that with room
to spare. If we hit ten times that, we revisit it. Postgres is still the right call."

**Native:** slightly off - "it's not suited for our system" is grammatical but dismissive to a
director. "Our numbers are different" - same meaning, no judgement on him.

## Consolidation

Both turns held the position, and Turn 2 closed on the decision that Turn 1 had missed - the
correction stuck within one turn. The remaining gap is **what you concede**: Turn 1 conceded a
technical premise you don't accept; Turn 2 conceded nothing and dismissed his experience instead.

The move is the same both times: **concede the concern, not the claim.** "I'd rather not migrate
under pressure either" costs nothing and makes the disagreement about numbers, not about who is
right.

## Recap

### 1. Expressions You Learned

- **`Agreed on X - but our schema is fixed, so that doesn't buy us anything`** - concede half, disqualify the rest
- **`That's fair, and I'd rather not X either - but our numbers are different`** - concede the concern, split the case
- **`As for the write-throughput bottleneck, ...`** (not *as the ... problem*)
- **`If we hit ten times that, we revisit it.`** - a trigger, not a promise
- **`Postgres is still the right call.`** - restate the decision as the last line
- **`the workload document stores are built for`** - `the N (that) X is built for`

### 2. Your Key Errors

| You said | Say instead |
| --- | --- |
| "it's not suited for our system" | "our numbers are different" |
| "2M rows a day is given according to our current situation" | "we're at 2M rows a day, and monthly partitions handle that" |
| (Turn 1) repeated his claim as your opening | "Agreed on X - but not for us, because ..." |

<details><summary>中文说明</summary>

**两轮都守住了立场**，而且第二轮你把“最后一句重申决定”补上了——上一轮刚纠正的点，一轮就改过来了。

**剩下的问题是“让步让什么”**：
- 第一轮：让步了一个你其实不认同的**技术前提**（write-heavy → 文档数据库）
- 第二轮：什么都没让，直接说对方的经验“不适用”——对 director 这句话很伤人

**正确做法：让步他的“担心”，不让步他的“结论”。**
`I'd rather not migrate under pressure either`（我也不想被迫迁移）——这句话不花你任何代价，
却把争论从“谁对谁错”拉回了“数字是多少”。

**`is given according to` 这类词组要删掉**——让数字直接说话：
`We're at 2M rows a day, and monthly partitions handle that with room to spare.`
