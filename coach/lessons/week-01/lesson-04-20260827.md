# Lesson 04 - Answer first, explain second

**Goal:** lead with the verdict - yes/no, the number, the date - then the reasoning.

## Input

Watch **42:10-45:50**. Four hostile or open questions, every one answered in the first clause.

## Dialogue

### Turn 1 - Sprint planning: Postgres or Mongo  [channel: spoken]

**Coach (tech lead, in front of the team):** "PostgreSQL or MongoDB for the new audit-log
service? You looked into it. What's your call?"

**You:** "I prefer to use PostgreSQL for the audit-log service. The audit logs are append-only
with a fixed schema, and query conditions can be sometimes complicated. So we need mutil-table
joins for reporting. We also run Postgres in production now so we need not to deploy a new
database service."

**Fix:**
- "I prefer to use PostgreSQL"  ->  "Postgres."  [awkward: he asked for a call; `prefer` invites a counter-preference]
- "we need not to deploy"  ->  "we don't need to deploy"  [wrong: `need not` is formal and takes no `to`]
- "can be sometimes complicated"  ->  "can get complicated"  [wrong: adverb between `be` and its complement]

**Model (minimal repair):** "Postgres. The audit logs are append-only with a fixed schema,
and the query conditions can get complicated, so we need multi-table joins for reporting.
We also run Postgres in production, so we don't need to deploy a new database service."

**Native:** clean structure - reasons in the right order, operational argument last (the
strongest). Only the opening was soft.

### Turn 2 - A yes/no question from a stakeholder  [channel: spoken]

**Coach (PM, review meeting):** "Will the audit-log service be ready for the compliance review
on September 15th?"

**You:** "No. The development of the service will be done by 10th. But the compliance review
needs the data-retention policy implemented too. The implement depends on the legal team's spec
and I haven't received it. If I can receive the spec this week, I'll have it done by 25th."

**Fix:**
- "The implement depends on"  ->  "That depends on"  [wrong: `implement` as a noun means *tool*; you want `implementation`, but colleagues say `that`]
- "by 10th" / "by 25th"  ->  "by the 10th" / "by the 25th"  [wrong: ordinal dates take `the`]
- "If I can receive the spec"  ->  "If legal gets me the spec"  [awkward: `receive` hides who owes you]

**Model (minimal repair):** "No. The development of the service will be done by the 10th, but
the compliance review needs the data-retention policy implemented too. That depends on the
legal team's spec, and I haven't received it. If legal gets me the spec this week, I'll have it
done by the 25th."

**Native:** clean - bare "No.", then constraint, blocker, conditional date. Exactly the shape.

### Turn 3 - Interview: commit to a position  [channel: spoken]

**Coach (interviewer):** "If you joined us, would you rather go deep on backend and become the
person we rely on for architecture, or stay broad and keep shipping across the stack?"

**You:** "I'd go deep on backend. Actually, I work for the company, not the company works for me.
If company need someone to design architecture, I will do it. I'm also glad to learn anything new
on backend."

**Fix:**
- "I work for the company, not the company works for me"  ->  say why *you* want depth  [awkward: reads as "I'll do whatever I'm told" and undoes your first clause]
- "If company need someone"  ->  "If you need someone"  [wrong: article + `-s`; and in an interview the company is `you`]
- "I'm also glad to learn"  ->  "I'd be glad to pick up"  [wrong: `I'm glad` = already pleased; `I'd be glad` = willing]

**Model (rebuilt: the middle sentence reversed the answer):** "I'd go deep on backend. The
problems I keep wanting to solve are the architectural ones - that's where I'd add the most value
here. And if you need someone to own architecture, I'd be glad to pick up whatever's new."

**Native:** first clause is exactly right - "I'd go deep on backend", no hedge. The rest
undercut it: `I work for the company, not the company works for me` is a Chinese workplace value
that reads in English as *no opinion of my own* - the opposite of what the question tests.

## Consolidation

Turns 1 and 2 landed the skill cleanly - `Postgres.` and `No.` in the first word, reasons
underneath. Turn 3 shows the limit: you open with a verdict, then walk it back to show deference.
In Chinese that is humility; in English it reads as having no position - which is what the
question tested.

The thread: **commit, then justify - never re-open the decision.**

## Recap

### 1. Expressions You Learned

- **`Postgres.`** / **`No.`** - a one-word verdict is a complete answer
- **`That's my call.`** vs *I prefer* - a decision, not a preference
- **`If legal gets me the spec this week`** - name who you're blocked on
- **`I'd be glad to pick up whatever's new`** - willing (not `I'm glad`)
- **`the problems I keep wanting to solve are the architectural ones`** - justify with your own motivation
- **`can get complicated`** (not *can be sometimes complicated*)

### 2. Your Key Errors

| You said | Say instead |
| --- | --- |
| "I prefer to use PostgreSQL" | "Postgres." |
| "I work for the company, not the company works for me" | "that's where I think I'd add the most value" |
| "we need not to deploy" | "we don't need to deploy" |

<details><summary>中文说明</summary>

**前两轮完成得很好**：`Postgres.` 和 `No.` 开头，理由在后。第二轮是四节课以来结构最好的一次回答。

**第三轮暂露了真正的问题**：你能用结论开头，但一到面试压力下就开始“表现顾全”，把结论收回去了。

`I work for the company, not the company works for me` 在中文语境里是“谦适、听指揥”；
但英文面试官听到的是“**这人没有自己的主张**”——而这道题考的恰好就是你能不能在模糊中坚持一个立场。
这句话不是语法错，是**文化误传**，代价比语法错大得多。

**规则**：说完 `I'd go deep on backend` 之后，后面每一句都只能**支撑**它，不能削弱它。

</details>
